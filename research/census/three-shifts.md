# Exact completion with three prescribed leaf shifts

**Theorem.** In $\mathbb Z_k$, let $R,C$ be arbitrary sets of available vertex labels and edge weights, of the same cardinality. Fix three distinct shifts and the number of times each must occur. A deterministic algorithm polynomial in $k$ decides whether $R$ can be matched bijectively to $C$ with those displacement counts, and constructs a matching when possible. The sets may have arbitrary holes. In particular, at order $k$ this decides whether a frozen injective core with distinct edge weights can be completed by leaves at three prescribed parents. Try each missing weight outside the core weights. More generally the effective shifts can include leaf-edge offsets.

Translate the columns to make the shifts $0,x,y$. Rows in each coset of $\langle x,y\rangle$ can use only columns in that coset. Within one coset identify the subgroup with $\mathbb Z_M$, where $x,y$ now generate $\mathbb Z_M$. Require the row and column counts there to agree. Choose any unweighted matching on the surviving allowed pairs by augmenting paths, or reject that coset if none exists. Its local displacement counts are $c^0_0,c^0_1,c^0_2$. The next test decides whether prescribed local counts $c_0,c_1,c_2$ are possible. A table over the successive cosets adds attainable pairs $(c_1,c_2)$ to meet the global totals. There are polynomially many local count pairs, table entries and operations.

## Difference inequalities on the cyclic positions

Write $d_j=c_j-c^0_j$. The local totals agree, so $d_0+d_1+d_2=0$. The sums of the rows and columns are fixed, requiring $d_1x+d_2y=0\pmod M$. Let $u^0_j(v)$ indicate that the baseline matching uses the shift of type $j$ at row $v$. Seek indicators $u_j(v)$ for the desired matching. For each cyclic position $i$ make the three directed arcs below, where $v$ identifies the matching row associated with the arc.

| Type $j$ | Start | End | Row $v$ | Shift at that row |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | $i$ | $i+y-x$ | $i$ | $0$ |
| 1 | $i$ | $i-y$ | $i-y$ | $x$ |
| 2 | $i$ | $i+x$ | $i+x-y$ | $y$ |

For an arc $a\to b$ of type $j$ and row $v$, write $K=d_j+M u^0_j(v)$. Impose
$$
 K-M\,\mathrm{cap}\ \le X(b)-X(a)\le K
$$
where $\mathrm{cap}=1$ if the matching row and its indicated column both survive, and zero otherwise. These are ordinary directed difference constraints. One can additionally forbid individual surviving pairs by setting capacity zero; the baseline must respect the same forbiddances.

**Necessity.** Given a desired matching, prescribe the increment on this arc to be $d_j-M(u_j(v)-u^0_j(v))$. The sum is zero around both elementary triangles with steps $y-x,-y,x$. In the listed order the three indicators count preimages of column $i$; in order $-y,y-x,x$ they count the choices at row $i-y$. These counts agree for the two matchings even at missing rows and columns. The constant terms sum to zero. On the triangular lattice generated by two formal steps, the triangle identities therefore give a path-independent integral. For a path that closes modulo $M$, the integral around its translate by any group element is the same. Indeed, connect the two starting points by a lattice path and commute that path past the period path; periodicity cancels the connecting increments. Summing the integrals over all translates gives zero, since each type of arc has mean increment zero by the prescribed counts. Consequently every closed path has integral zero. The increments descend to a potential $X$ on the finite cyclic group, and satisfy the inequalities.

**Sufficiency.** Test the directed inequalities by shortest paths from one source. A negative cycle rules out the prescribed counts; otherwise the distances give a feasible potential. The graph is connected in both directions. All lengths along a given arc are congruent to $d_j$ modulo $M$ in its forward direction, and to $-d_j$ on its reverse. There is a scalar $\lambda$ with $\lambda x=d_2$ and $\lambda y=-d_1$ modulo $M$. Indeed choose $s x+t y=1\pmod M$ and put $\lambda=s d_2-t d_1$; the moment condition verifies both equations. Thus $\lambda(y-x)=d_0$ also. Every walk between two positions has the same total length modulo $M$. In particular the shortest potential has $X(b)-X(a)\equiv K\pmod M$.

Set $u_j(v)=(K-X(b)+X(a))/M$. These integers lie between zero and capacity. The two triangle identities give precisely the baseline number of choices at every row and every column. Summing the potential differences for one type over all $i$ gives zero, so that type occurs exactly $c_j$ times. This constructs the requested matching.

## Uniform increments for two branching neighbors at all composite moduli

For the [two-branch tree](two-branch-joint.md) write the joint label as zero. The centers $x,y$ have $a,b>0$ leaf children, respectively, with $\ell$ other leaves at the joint and $k=a+b+\ell+2$. A uniform increment identifies each free vertex label with its child-edge weight. This is a permutation with jumps $0,x,y$, with counts $\ell+2,a,b$, in which both positions $x,y$ are fixed. The following specialized implementation avoids finding a baseline.

If $\gcd(k,a)>1$ or $\gcd(k,b)>1$, place whole shift cycles on one side and use label zero at the other center as in the no-direct-leaf construction. Assign surplus labels at the joint. In the remaining case both counts are units, so the equation $ax+by=0$ forces $y$ from $x$ and both are nonzero. For each proper divisor $g$ of $k$ try $x=g$. Unit scaling covers all residues with $\gcd(x,k)=g$. In each subgroup coset use coordinates modulo $M=k/g$ with steps $x'=1$ and $y'=-ab^{-1}\pmod M$. Discard $y'=1$ since the centers must be distinct.

In a coset with local counts $h,i,j$ summing to $M$, require $i x'+j y'=0$. For the three forward arcs of the table use nonnegative lengths $h,i,j$. The potential construction above applies by taking the all-fixed baseline of size $M$. The forward lengths then equal $h,i,j$. Reverse bounds for unit capacity follow from the other two forward steps returning to the starting position. This constructs a permutation on any nonzero coset. In the coset of the two centers, fixed positions force an increase of $M-h$ across each of the steps $x'-y'$ ending at $y'$ and $x'$. Use shortest distances from $2y'-x'$. The two fixed positions are possible exactly when the distance to $x'$ is $2(M-h)$. Necessity follows from the potential upper bounds. For sufficiency this distance is achieved over two steps each bounded by $M-h$, so both increases are forced and the recovered indicators fix both centers. The coset table then gives an exact criterion at every composite modulus. At a prime it reduces to the three-generator test. ## Checks

Direct permutation enumeration on arbitrary missing rows and columns, and random higher-order cases, gave 23,780 exact comparisons; a further 960 planted partial matchings checked the output quotas directly. The two-center implementation checked every positive split through modulus 69, producing 45,379 verified uniform blocks and 4,737 exclusions. It matched direct exhaustive searches on all 165 splits through modulus 12 and the independent prime criterion in 11,120 cases. The exclusions concern a uniform increment at a prescribed joint. A cordial extension with the core's specified deficient weight always exists for these trees.
