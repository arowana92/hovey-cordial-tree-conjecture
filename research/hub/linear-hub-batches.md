# A linear modulus cutoff for a small core of bounded nonroot degree

**Modular theorem.** For each fixed $D\ge2$ there is a computable $K_D$ with the following property. In an $h$-vertex tree $H$ rooted at $o$, suppose all vertices except $o$ have degree at most $D$. At each $k>K_D(h+1)$ there are distinct vertex labels in $\mathbb Z_k$, with $o$ at zero, whose actual edge sums are all vertex labels except one. Every number of extra leaves at $o$ therefore gives a $k$-cordial tree. The degree of $o$ in $H$ may itself be unbounded.

**All-orders consequence.** If the ordinary core $H$ has bounded degree at **every** vertex, then at all sufficiently large $k$ the conclusion of cordiality holds for arbitrary $h$ and arbitrary numbers of leaves at $o$. Take $\delta=1/(2K_D+2)$. For $h<\delta k$ use the modular theorem. For $h\ge\delta k$ use the [branching-core embedding](branching-pools.md) with a single site. The first assertion constructs a particular omitted-label certificate. The second asserts cordiality over the full range of tree orders.

## Absorbing an entire bounded forest at once

Keep an ancestor-closed labeled subtree with multisets $E=V-[0]$, initially the lone root at zero. Suppose some small hanging components are to be completely added, and a separate hanging component supplies ten new vertices in downward path order. Put the first three path vertices with the ordinary vertices and reserve the last seven. The first layer of the ordinary vertices has already labeled parents, which may include zero. Treat each incoming parent label as a jump modulo $k$.

Starting at zero, walk through these jumps, excising a simple cycle whenever a position repeats. Label the residual path's steps by $X$ plus their respective tail positions. Translate the tails of each cycle by one common intercept before adding $X$, so they avoid the residual tails, its endpoint $e$, and the other cycles. The residual path has distinct tails and its endpoint is distinct from all its tails, including when the path is empty. For a batch of size at most $M$, at most $M(M+1)$ translates are forbidden per cycle. All first-layer forms of slope one are now distinct and avoid the terminal $L=X+e$. The new contribution to $E-V$ is $[L]-[X]$. [Cycle decomposition and exact translation rule](modular-injection.md#a-round-using-eight-consecutive-vertices)

Process the rest of the ordinary vertices in parent-before-child order. Give each the current $L$, then increase $L$ by its parent's form. The first parent was assigned a positive slope in the first layer, and every subsequent such parent has positive slope. Thus terminal slopes strictly increase after each assignment, and the contribution telescopes to $[L]-[X]$ with the final $L$. The first assignment in this step may still have slope one, but its intercept was reserved in the preceding step. All assigned forms are distinct. On the third path vertex the form $p$ has slope $c\ge2$, since the second path vertex has already advanced the terminal. The terminal's final slope $b$ is strictly greater than $c$ and every assigned ordinary slope.

On the seven reserved vertices below $p$, use in order
$$
 L,\quad -p-2L,\quad p+L,\quad X+L,\quad -L,\quad -p-L,\quad p+X+2L .
$$
Adding consecutive labels, starting from $p$, gives these same seven labels with $L$ replaced by $X$. Their slopes are $b,-c-2b,c+b,1+b,-b,-c-b,c+1+2b$. They are pairwise different; each is negative or at least $b$. Thus they are new, and the discrepancy of the batch vanishes. [Actual seven-edge multiset identity](../large-order/GapPath.lean) · [Slope separation in Lean](BatchTransfer.lean)

Specialize $X$ to a residue. With at most $M$ new vertices the absolute slopes are bounded just in terms of $M$: each ordinary update at most doubles the running slope, and the closing seven have absolute slopes at most $4\cdot2^M+1$. Two equal slopes have unequal intercepts modulo $k$. An equality of distinct slopes excludes at most the absolute slope difference choices of $X$, also for composite $k$. Thus at most $O_M(h+1)$ choices are forbidden by collisions among the batch and with the previously assigned labels. The latter are distinct constants, so **old–old pairs cost no exclusions**. This completes a neutral batch whenever $k$ exceeds a constant times $h+1$.

## Scheduling the batches with a bounded last remainder

A root branch is short here if it has at most fifteen vertices on any downward path starting at its top. Its size is bounded by $B=1+D+\cdots+D^{14}$. Pair equal rooted shapes among all short root branches; temporarily omit those pairs. There are fewer than $4^B$ remaining short root branches, using the plane rooted-tree count as an upper bound on the number of shapes of order at most $B$. The root may still have arbitrarily many long branches.

If the residual tree has depth at most four, its size is bounded in terms of $D$, so the [integer rooted construction](polynomial-hub.md) works modulo every $k$ above a constant. Otherwise apply the [terminal four-site construction](modular-injection.md#stopping-with-at-most-twelve-levels) within four edges of a deepest vertex. Withhold its nonroot boundary $w$ and the entire subtree below $w$, of height at most four. This can shorten at most one root branch. In the eligible tree above this withheld part, start from the root. At any stage call an exposed component short if every downward path in it uses fewer than ten vertices.

If a tall component exists, take the first ten vertices of a longest downward path from its top. Process **every currently exposed short component in full**, together with these ten path vertices, by one neutral batch. Other tall components remain untouched. Every newly exposed short component hangs immediately off one of the ten path vertices, and there are at most $10D$ of them. No old short component remains. Initially the number of exposed short components was at most $4^B+1$. This proves a uniform batch-size bound throughout, for example $M=10+(4^B+10D+3)B$. Each step labels ten or more new vertices, so the process ends.

At termination only boundedly many short components and the withheld subtree remain, together of size at most $M$. Put back the withheld subtree but set aside just the four special sites and their pendant leaves. Its boundary $w$ is still unassigned, and each remaining ordinary site is within at most fourteen downward edges of the labeled subtree (nine eligible sites, one to reach $w$, and four below). Apply the [final layer and four-site proof](modular-injection.md#stopping-with-at-most-twelve-levels): the first layer again has slope one and a reserved terminal, each later ordinary site's positive-slope parent advances the terminal, and the four sites restore weight zero leaving one gap. The four-site ordering and pairing handle all their remaining pendant leaves. The argument there uses only the number of processed sites to bound the slopes; fourteen rather than twelve levels changes no identity. All moving forms number at most $M$, and their slope magnitudes have a computable bound in terms of $M$ alone. Earlier labels are pairwise distinct constants. Consequently specialization again excludes only $O_D(h+1)$ values, and produces $E=V-[g]$ for a vertex label $g$.

Finally restore the paired short root branches. For each rooted branch shape the [signed construction](signed-tree.md) supplies nonzero integers of distinct absolute values whose edge-sum magnitudes are its nonroot label magnitudes. Put $\lambda x_v$ on one copy and $-\lambda x_v$ on the other. The two hub edges supply the two opposite branch-root labels and the internal sums supply all the remaining pairs. This adds exactly identical multisets to $E$ and $V$. All integer slopes in a pair are distinct and nonzero, bounded by $81B$. Thus avoiding all previously occupied residues and collisions within the pair again costs $O_D(h+1)$ choices of $\lambda$. Restore each pair separately. Taking a common constant over the indicated finite bounds gives $K_D$. To add arbitrary further leaves use the [periodic complement and omitted-weight construction](README.md#uniform-witnesses-for-large-moduli).

## Reproduction

The checker independently computes every actual edge sum, counts multiplicities and checks the omitted label. It covers 1,205 rooted shapes through order ten, long paths, binary and ternary trees and a high-degree root carrying repeated branches. It replays 2,870 batches and 1,678 reflected branch pairs and checks every prefix of two leaf periods in one small modulus. These checks exercise the construction, while the counting argument gives the uniform linear cutoff. [Constructor](linear_hub_batches.py) · [Independent checker](check_linear_hub_batches.py)
