# Several vertices of unrestricted degree and their distance from leaves

**Theorem.** Fix $D\ge3$ and $0<\epsilon<1$. For every sufficiently large $k$, let $T$ be a finite tree with at most $k^{1-\epsilon}$ vertices of degree greater than $D$. If at most $k^{1-\epsilon}$ leaves of $T$ are within distance two of those vertices, $T$ is $k$-cordial. The large vertices can be adjacent and their degrees and the order of $T$ are unrestricted.

**Subdivision corollary.** Start with any finite tree with at most $k^{1-\epsilon}$ vertices of degree greater than $D$. Subdivide each pendant edge at least twice. The resulting tree is $k$-cordial at every sufficiently large $k$ satisfying that bound. Each leaf is now at distance at least three from every high-degree vertex. The other edges can have any number of subdivisions, including zero. In particular this covers arbitrary trees in the degree-count class with every edge replaced by a path of at least three edges.

The proof allows many independent external parent labels. The required combinatorial condition is that a positive proportion of edges remain between nonroots after cutting below the high-degree vertices.

## Forest extension with multiple boundary labels

Consider a rooted forest on $k-1$ vertices. Count an incoming edge at every vertex, including the prescribed external edge at each component root. Let $S$ contain all vertices of free degree above $D$, with $|S|\le k^{1-\epsilon}$. There are at most $k^{1-\epsilon}$ distinct originally prescribed external labels, with arbitrary multiplicities. Cut every outgoing edge at every member of $S$. A child on a cut edge will need the eventual label of its original parent as its external residue. Call the resulting forest $F$ and all its component roots simply roots. Suppose $F$ contains at least $\delta k$ edges with **both endpoints nonroots**, for a fixed $\delta>0$.

For all sufficiently large $k$, there is a bijection of the free vertices onto the complement of any specified residue $u$, with distinct weights on all original incoming edges. In particular every cut edge gets the sum of the labels at its two original endpoints.

**Preparing the pins.** Write $c$ for the original component count. If $c+E_D\le k^{1-\epsilon/2}$, use the [degree-excess forest lemma](../bounded-degree/excess.md#forest-lemma). Otherwise $F$ has at least $c+E_D>k^{1-\epsilon/2}$ components, since cutting the outgoing edges at the high vertices cuts at least their entire degree excess. All free degrees of $F$, counting its new external edges, are at most $D$. Pin each vertex of $S$ and its original free parent if present. In original parent-before-child order assign distinct labels to the pins, avoiding $u$ and **all originally prescribed external residues**. Keep distinct weights on all original pin–pin and incoming external edges at pins. Only an incoming edge can become fully specified at each step. The number of pinned vertices and induced weights is $O(k^{1-\epsilon})$, independently of their original degrees. Each cut parent now has its actual label. Let $B$ consist of the original external labels and these new shifts. Then $|B|=O(k^{1-\epsilon})$.

There is one precaution concerning roots. If a free child of a root is pinned, its label differs from that root's external label. For an original external residue this was required of every pin. For a new external label the only pin with that label is the cut parent, and this parent is different from the root's child. This guarantees that root repairs will be possible even next to pins.

**Choosing the last edges.** Use $t=\lceil2(\log k)^7\rceil$ and $r=\lfloor\delta k/(1000(D+3)(t-1))\rfloor$. In $F$ reserve a vertex of degree $d\ge2$ for a matching, and two nonadjacent degree-one vertices for paths, away from each other, the pins and their free neighbors. There are $\Omega_D(\delta k)$ choices of degree at least two, and at least $k^{1-\epsilon/2}$ terminals. Distances here are in $F$, where the pins have bounded degree.

Delete every root of $F$ for the purpose of selecting gadgets. This leaves a downward-closed forest with at least $\delta k$ internal edges. The following argument gives $r$ disjoint terminal edges or $r$ disjoint bare paths of length $t$, avoiding all pins, reserves and their neighbors. If there are at least $10r$ nonsingleton components, take one nonroot terminal edge per component and discard exclusions. Otherwise join the tops of the nonsingleton components in a chain, use Lemma 3.7 on the resulting tree, and discard gadgets meeting a join vertex or an excluded vertex. The tree has at least $\delta k$ vertices, degree at most $D+2$, and fewer than $10r$ join vertices. The lemma supplies at least $\delta k/(10(D+2)t)$ disjoint gadgets before discards. In both cases true terminal edges remain terminal in $F$. Path interiors keep their degree two since no retained path meets a component top in the chain argument. All marked endpoints and deferred vertices are **nonroots of $F$**, including the matching parents in the many-component case. Keep only the reserves needed for the chosen gadget. [Müyesser and Pokrovskiy, Lemma 3.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05280v2#S3.SS3)

**Sampling the root assignments.** Let $R$ be the number of roots of $F$ and $n_p$ the count at external shift $p\in B$. Put $g=1-R/k\ge\delta$. Give all roots together vertex mass $1-g$, and give all root edges together the same color mass. This uses one external edge per root even for pinned roots. On the other vertices, including a dummy for $u$, use the positional roles of the [common-boundary proof](one-exceptional-degree.md#embedding-the-remaining-forest). More explicitly, remove pins, active reserves and deferred vertices in $F$. Delete at most $k/m$ separators by the bottom-up construction there, now with
$$
m=\lceil k^\beta\rceil,\qquad \beta=\min(1/10000,\epsilon/20).
$$
Order each surviving piece parent before child. All surviving roots belong to the first position in their respective pieces. Split nonroot bulk roles by position and marked endpoint type, with matching internal color roles at positions two and later. Use $V_*,C_*$ for the deferred new vertices and their gadget edges, and use leftover roles for all other positions, splitting off marked endpoint types. Pinned or separated roots remain in the outside root mass and are counted just once.

On the nonroot rows and nonexternal columns use the transportation law $a_i b_j+(1-g)D_{ij}$ of the common-boundary proof. Now impose the full independence rectangle between $C_*$ and the **union of all endpoint roles and $V_*$**, retaining each vertex type separately. These rows all lie inside the nonroot mass. Their sum is at most $\max(2r,(t+1)r)/k<g/2$, and $C_*$ has mass at most $tr/k<g/2$, so weighted Hall again supplies $D$. Give the pair of outside roles mass $1-g$ and no other mass in their row or column.

Round $kn_p/R$ to quotas totaling $k$, each within one of its target. Change one of the $k$ listed shifts to make their sum zero and flag that entry. Hall's prescribed-differences theorem gives a permutation $\pi$ of $\mathbb Z_k$ with this multiset of displacements. Draw the joint categorical law independently for vertex $x$ and color $\pi(x)$ at each source coordinate $x$. The root source and target arrays are permutation images, and the entire marked/new vertex arrays retain independence from $C_*$ after permutation. Each separate bulk vertex or color role is still an ordinary Bernoulli random set. [Marshall Hall Jr., primary theorem](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1952-0050579-7) · [Quota construction and its distribution](permuted-pools.md#permuting-the-random-coordinates)

Among the outside arcs $x\mapsto x+p$, assign up to $n_p$ of each type to different roots of that type. Skip the flagged arc. The expected sum of absolute count errors is $O(\sqrt{|B|k}+|B|+1)$ by binomial variance, Cauchy–Schwarz and rounding. With high probability at most $O(k^{1-\epsilon/3})$ root sites fail to obtain an arc. One may first distribute these arcs over **all** root sites, then disregard arcs assigned to removed sites. Among the eligible bulk roots, therefore, only the preceding quota error can cause a miss. No order or type of a root's children is prescribed by its arc. Its actual label and edge weight are already fixed, regardless of which children follow.

**Embedding and repair.** Assign the other first-position sites within their vertex roles. For each later position and type use Lemma 7.7 with its own vertex and color sets. Available parents for one role are distinct because their children belong to different pieces. A parent originally placed on a root arc is outside every nonroot vertex role, exactly as required. Skip roles below density $k^{-1/600}$. Omit every missed vertex and its descendants in its piece. The losses, including the descendants of root-quota misses, are bounded by
$$
O\big(m k^{1-\epsilon/3}+m^2(k^{1-1/600}+k^{1-1/500}+k^{0.6})\big).
$$
This is polynomially sublinear. In particular root quotas are handled before asking the matching lemma to embed their children. The bulk weights are rainbow, since the outside targets, the position colors and the gadget colors are disjoint. [Müyesser and Pokrovskiy, Lemma 7.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05280v2#A1)

Remove bulk collisions with the pin labels or $u$, and endpoints of bulk edges whose weights collide with pin weights. Also remove ordinary neighbors of pins along uncut edges of $F$, and all bulk vertices whose labels lie in $B$. The last deletion costs at most $|B|$ vertices by injectivity. The new external edges at neighbors of cut parents have already been paid for by their arcs or belong to missing roots. Thus a pin's possibly enormous original degree causes no uncontrolled deletion. Reinstate the pins. There are $O(k^{1-\gamma})$ missing nongadget vertices other than the reserves for some fixed $\gamma>0$, including all separators. Marked and deferred roles have the same symmetric-difference guarantees as in the common-boundary proof. In addition to the previously counted discrepancies the root source/target roles can have unused arcs. Their number is bounded by the total quota error, the dummy, the $O(k/m+k^{1-\epsilon}+1)$ initially removed roots and the subsequent bulk deletions.

Use the independent $V_*,C_*$ reservoir to repair missing sites. At a root with shift $p$ request the extra incident color $x+p$ even if the free parent on the original edge is a high pin. Each already labeled free child has label different from $p$. This holds initially by the deletion of bulk labels in $B$ and the choice of pins. Preserve it by forbidding the external residue of a vertex's root parent when assigning that vertex. This excludes at most one extra value at a step. All checks at a vertex now cost at most $D$ incident colors counting its external edge. The improved Chernoff estimate for one additional affine color also holds, since the **entire** arrays $V_*,C_*$ are independent. [Reservoir, matching reserve and path reserve equations](one-exceptional-degree.md#repair-and-the-last-group-sum)

For a matching use the degree-$d$ reserve $z$, its actual neighbor labels $p_i$ (including an external shift when necessary), and the unspent $V,C$. Omit the available weight $\sum C-\sum V-\sum X-\sum_i p_i-(d-1)z$ where $X$ is the set of marked parents. The remaining sets have the exact sum and sizes. Here the two disjoint vertex reference sets are jointly independent of the color reference. Thus the simpler Theorem 3.1 applies over every sufficiently large cyclic group, including even orders; its identity exclusion concerns elementary abelian groups of exponent two. For paths use the two reserved terminals exactly as in the common-boundary proof. Their parent labels may be different prescribed shifts or may coincide. Lemma 3.2 applies to the full independence rectangle. The rounding and error comparisons are unchanged, with the fixed positive exponent $\gamma$ from this proof. This finishes the forest extension and restores every cut weight at its original endpoints. [Müyesser and Pokrovskiy, Theorem 3.1 and Lemma 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05280v2#S3.SS1)

## Consecutive preorder blocks

Root the tree at any vertex. In preorder visit child subtrees in nondecreasing order of size. Start with the root, whose residue is the sole surplus among vertex frequencies and whose edge frequencies are all zero. Whenever the current prefix has order $1\bmod k$ and uniform edge frequencies, take the next $b=\min(k-1,\text{number remaining})$ vertices. They form a rooted forest with prescribed edges to the prefix. Label this block injectively avoiding the surplus vertex residue and using distinct new weights. If the tree finishes in the block, these are precisely the two balance requirements. Otherwise the block has size $k-1$. The enlarged prefix then has uniform vertex frequencies and one deficient edge weight. The next preorder vertex has one edge to its already labeled parent, so assign it the label realizing that weight. This again gives a prefix of order $1\bmod k$ with uniform edge frequencies. Repeating proves the theorem once the block assertion is established.

The block has at most $2+\log_2(k+1)$ distinct boundary parents even when the unvisited remainder is much larger than $k$. At its start the rest of the preorder consists of whole hanging subtrees grouped by their boundary parent, deepest group first. At each change of group the next subtree has size larger than the total of the preceding groups. This follows from the increasing-child-size ordering and the additional ancestor on the branch just traversed. The block can truncate at most the last subtree it meets. Every earlier group has been visited in full, so their total size at least doubles plus one per change, giving the claimed bound. Repeated edges to one high boundary parent contribute one shift. [Ordered traversal argument](../rainbow/ordered-cut.md)

Call vertices of original degree greater than $D$ high. Cut all outgoing edges at the high vertices inside the block, taking those vertices as $S$. A root of the resulting forest whose original parent is low must come from the external boundary of the block. There are at most $D(2+\log_2(k+1))$ such roots. Consider any remaining root $v$. Its original parent is high, either inside the block or in the earlier prefix. If $v$ occurs among the last two block sites, count its component as exceptional. Otherwise inspect $v$ and its first child $w$ in preorder. If $v$ is high or an original leaf, charge this component to $v$. If $v$ is low and has a child but $w$ is high or an original leaf, charge it to $w$. These charges are all distinct since the charged vertex belongs to its own component after cutting. Charged leaves are at distance one or two from a high vertex. Thus at most $2k^{1-\epsilon}+D(2+\log_2(k+1))+2$ components are exceptional.

In each remaining component the first three consecutive sites are $v,w$ and the first child of $w$. Both $v,w$ are low and both edges survive the cut. The last edge joins two nonroots. More generally a component of order $j$ has $j-1$ internal edges and at most $D-1$ of them have a root endpoint. Every nonexceptional component therefore contributes at least $\max(1,j-D)\ge j/(D+1)$ eligible edges. Each exceptional component contributes at least $j/(D+1)-D$. Summation gives at least $b/(D+1)-O_D(k^{1-\epsilon}+\log k)$ edges between nonroots in the whole block.

If the final block is shorter than $k-1$, extend a terminal of the original block forest by a temporary path of length $k-1-b$. This adds at least $k-1-b-2$ edges with two nonroot endpoints even when the terminal is in $S$. It raises the terminal's free degree to at most two. The padded block therefore has at least $k/(2(D+1))$ eligible edges for sufficiently large $k$. Its high set has at most $k^{1-\epsilon}$ members. Its $O(\log k)$ original external labels and its high set fit the forest lemma, decreasing $\epsilon$ slightly if necessary. Restrict the resulting injection to the actual block. This proves the theorem. The proof also permits the bounds on high vertices and charged leaves to be checked separately on each block.

## Reproduction

The block checker works on the actual subdivided tree, verifies its external edges and eligible internal edges after each cut, and checks short terminal padding. Separate finite searches pin several cut parents and reconstruct all original weights using a range of external residues. The asymptotic embedding invokes the primary papers in the proof.

~~~sh
python3 -B -m research.hub.check_subdivided_blocks
python3 -B -m research.hub.check_common_boundary
python3 -B -m research.hub.check_permuted_pools
~~~
