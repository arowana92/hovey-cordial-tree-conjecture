# A cube-root bound on exceptional leaves, and a square-root bound with a bare path

**Theorem.** Fix $s\ge1$. There is $c_s>0$ such that, for all sufficiently large $k$, every tree with all but at most $c_s k^{1/3}$ leaves adjacent to $s$ specified vertices is $k$-cordial. If the ordinary core obtained by deleting the direct leaves at the sites also has a sufficiently long segment with no internal site or branch vertex, the allowed number increases to $c_s\sqrt k$. The required segment length depends only on $s$. Both conclusions hold at every tree order. The one-site [unconditional square-root theorem](quadratic-root.md) still applies separately.

**Integer theorem.** In a tree $H$ of order $h$ with $l$ leaves, attach prescribed leaf populations at at most $s$ chosen sites. Write $m_1$ for the largest population. There is a constant $A_s$ such that
$$
 m_1\ge A_s\big(h+(l+1)^3\big)
$$
suffices for a super edge-magic labeling, **uniformly over all the other populations**. If $H$ contains the long segment above, it suffices that $m_1\ge A_s(h+(l+1)^2)$. In particular the cubic threshold in terms of $h$ works even if only one of the marked populations is large. For one site and a long bare segment this gives a quadratic bound in the core order for the number of leaves needed to obtain consecutive integer sums.

## Paying for an interval with one large population

We first give the accounting used for both bounds. Suppose that on the relevant ordinary trees of order $h$ and with $l$ leaves we have interval seeds for each leading set of $j\ge2$ positive sites, with the following costs, for numbers $1\le B\le T$:
$$
 O_s(T)\text{ fillers in total},\qquad O_s(B)\text{ fillers away from zero}.
$$
Assume all site digits have magnitude bounded by $F_s$, and that the two-site orientation or the [large-site moment slacks](polynomial-marked.md#exact-populations) are available. Include one actual leaf from each positive population in the core beforehand to handle the residual caterpillars, as in that theorem. The resulting changes to order and leaf count are bounded in terms of $s$. When needed, the zero site will be chosen on the initial hull and kept throughout the construction.

Suppose $m_1\ge C_s T$. Choose a fixed small $\beta_s>0$ with $(s+1)(F_s+2)\beta_s<1/4$. In the case $m_2\ge\beta_s m_1$, there is a $j\ge2$ with $m_j\ge D_s(T+\sum_{i>j}m_i)$ for any preassigned $D_s$ by choosing $C_s$ sufficiently large. Indeed failure at all $j$ gives $m_2<(1+D_s)^s T$ by descending induction. The [exact population lemma](marked-leaf-threshold.md#realizing-the-exact-leaf-populations) applies with both slacks and all anchor populations paying the total cost.

In the remaining case $m_2<\beta_s m_1$, suppose first that $m_2\ge (1+D_s)^s B$. The same descent finds $m_j\ge D_s(B+\sum_{i>j}m_i)$. At two sites the largest digit is zero. At three or more sites it must be zero or one before orientation: all other terms in the large-site moment have magnitude at most $sF_s\beta_s m_1$ and that moment lies between zero and the total mass. Reflect the **initial** seed if necessary to put the largest site at zero; a reflected triple has a quadruple available after including that site. Use the nonzero-parent cost bound in this orientation. The lower slack is at least $\eta_s m_j-O_s(B+\sum_{i>j}m_i)$, since a zero-parent filler costs no moment. The upper slack before deductions is at least $m_1-s(F_s+1)m_2$ including all sites. The total seed cost in that slack is $O_s(T)$. These inequalities pay the bounded reserves and keep enough actual leaves at every selected site. Here $D_s$ can first be increased to absorb all site and slack constants, then $C_s$ to absorb all total costs. If $m_2<(1+D_s)^s B$, absorb every smaller population into the core. At most $O_s(B)$ ordinary leaves are added and only the largest site requires further leaves. We use the one-site bounds below for precisely this case.

## One bare segment supplies all the corrections

Here a segment has internal vertices of degree two and no internal sites. Mark the sites and all degree-different-from-two vertices. There are $O_s(l+1)$ segments between successive marks, also for a path. We explain the construction in the presence of one sufficiently long segment.

Shorten segments, preserving lengths modulo twelve. On any segment longer than a fixed threshold keep a congruent length in a fixed interval of twelve integers. All short segments retain their original lengths. Choose the fixed threshold sufficiently large to leave at least nine untouched consecutive edges on each segment that will need further expansion. On the distinguished long segment, additionally retain a run of **12,149** internal vertices reserved for correction, with at least nine retained exterior edges. One can use a common large retention threshold on all segments. For the two-site witness pins (a short path and twig, or five consecutive hub-path edges), choose the retained correction run disjoint from those pins. They are bounded in number independently of lengths: a sufficiently long retained segment contains such a consecutive run even after discarding all pinned positions and its two ends. The original short witness, if needed, survives shortening since every shortened length is at least nine. Equivalently one may split first at these boundedly many extra pins and choose the long run in one of the resulting pieces. Thus a uniform sufficiently large original length suffices, including if equality to the chosen retention length means that segment itself does not need later expansion. A fixed threshold in terms of $s$ suffices simultaneously for all choices of leading sites. The short tree has order $b=O_s(l+1)$.

Compress the reserved run to a single edge $uv$. For two or more leading sites choose the [linear-width hull seed](polynomial-marked.md#a-seed-of-width-at-most-a-constant-times-the-third-power) in this compressed tree, on the hull of the sites and the witness pins. Its cost is $O_s(b+1)$, all site digits are bounded, and it has the moment slacks needed above. The two-site residual caterpillar case on the original core already works. The witness pins prevent loss of eligibility during compression in the other cases. Fix the orientation now. For one site just use a seed consisting of the prescribed root at zero, with no fillers. Use the [integer forest preparation](integer-forest.md) on the rest of the compressed short tree, with the entire seed interval fixed. The integers and targets are $O_s((b+1)^2)$ and there are at most 864 protected pairs. The histogram difference includes the one omitted seed endpoint $g$ and the edge $uv$ is still being counted.

Withhold that edge. Starting from $u$, use ten neutral path vertices to obtain a fresh nonzero parent. Use 864 slots of fourteen path vertices, one slot per error and neutral slots for the rest. Each error slot uses the [paired seven-step transfer](bare-path-repair.md#repairing-the-actual-weights) below the current parent. Its two constant arrivals are the reserved $t,-t$ and its moving slopes have distinct equal-slope intercepts. Each neutral slot uses seven ordinary arrivals followed by a closing seven-step transfer. Its two initial equal slopes have different intercepts, including at a zero endpoint, and the other repeated slopes are separated as in the [neutral batch proof](bare-path-repair.md). Each fixed-length placement excludes $O_s(b+1)$ integer values of its parameter. All inputs before the ten-vertex arm have magnitude $O_s((b+1)^2)$; the bounded number of affine placements therefore leaves all new magnitudes of that same order. Alternatively choose each parameter near the root of its last arrival to keep successive parent magnitudes small. All prepared discrepancies have now been corrected.

Use the last 43 internal vertices as two 21-vertex bridges with middle vertex $G=-N$. The first bridge prescribes weight $g$, and the second the original $u+v$, writing endpoints by their labels. The new discrepancy relative to the virtual edge is $[g]-[G]$, so $G$ becomes the only omitted weight. Given any $R\ge0$, take $N$ a sufficiently large multiple of 630 of order $O_s((b+1)^2+R)$, with $N>R$. Center the bridge parameters at $12N/7$ and $10N/9$ respectively. The [integer coefficient lists](cubic-endpoint.md#a-block-with-constant-leaf-counts) put every arrival other than $G$ between $-(4/9)N$ and $(4/7)N$ up to additive $O_s((b+1)^2)$ errors. Center perturbations on boxes of side $O_s(b+1)$ suffice for avoiding all previous labels and each other. Hence $G$ is the strict minimum. The total number of restored internal sites is $10+14\cdot864+43=12149$. [Constructor](integer_bare_endpoint.py) · [Lean coefficient checks](EndpointSlopes.lean)

## Restoring all other lengths at zero

All short-tree vertices outside the final 43 have magnitude at most $K=O_s((b+1)^2)$, determined before choosing $N$. On every segment needing elongation choose a retained edge **outside the correction run** with nonzero endpoints and sum. There are at least nine untouched edges, only two edges on a path can meet zero, and at most one whole-tree edge weighs zero. Its endpoints have magnitude at most $K$. Write $I=O_s(b+1)$ for the number of initial fillers at nonzero parents. Choose $D=100(h+I+1)$ and $R=16D+4K$. Fill every hole between the current minimum and maximum at zero, extending the upper end further to at least $R$ by leaves at zero. Both radii now cover $R$. The total width is $O_s(h+(l+1)^2)$.

Apply the [neutral twelve-vertex subdivision](long-branches.md#a-short-core-with-arbitrarily-elongated-edges) repeatedly on the designated edges, using parameters $a,4a$ with $1\le a\le D$. Each time avoid all actual vertices and the $I$ nonzero-parent fillers. The twelve affine coordinates have magnitude below $R$, initially by the bound on the designated endpoints and subsequently by the fresh $a,4a$ on the inner edge. They therefore replace twelve zero-parent leaves. This preserves both intervals and leaves every site digit fixed. All original lengths are restored. This gives the accounting lemma with
$$
 T=h+(l+1)^2,\qquad B=l+1 .
$$
At a single site all filler leaves are there. In the small-$m_2$ case of that lemma, absorbing $O_s(l+1)$ minority leaves leaves $O_s(l+1)$ ordinary tips, changes order by $O_s(l+1)$, and preserves the long bare segment: no interior vertex was a site. The same one-site construction therefore costs $O_s(T)$, proving the long-segment integer assertion.

If there is no segment of the required length, $h=O_s(l+1)$. Use the [asymmetric marked endpoint seed](sparse-marked-endpoint.md#a-marked-interval-with-limited-cost-away-from-zero) directly on this core. It costs $O_s((h+1)^3)$ in total and $O_s(h+1)$ away from zero. The accounting lemma applies with $T=(h+1)^3$ and $B=h+1$. Absorbing small populations now gives a core of order $O_s(h+1)$, where the [one-site cubic endpoint theorem](cubic-endpoint.md) costs $O_s(T)$. This proves the unconditional integer assertion as well. Isolated cores and a single positive population are included by the one-site construction or the direct star labeling.

## From integer intervals to all orders at a given modulus

Remove leaves adjacent to the sites, keeping the sites themselves. If there were $l_0$ exceptional leaves, the resulting core has at most $l=l_0+O_s(1)$ ordinary leaves; only a site can become a new leaf. The [positive-density theorem](concentrated-leaves.md) covers all ordinary orders $h\ge\delta k$ at large $k$ for any fixed $\delta>0$. Both the count of ordinary leaves and that of ordinary vertices of degree greater than three are $O_s(1)+O(\sqrt k)$ in the two assertions.

For smaller ordinary orders, first take total order $k$. There is a site with $m_1\ge(k-h)/s$. Choose $\delta$ sufficiently small in terms of $A_s,s$, and then choose $c_s>0$ sufficiently small. With $l_0\le c_s k^{1/3}$ this ensures $A_s(h+(l+1)^3)\le(k-h)/s$ for large $k$. With the specified long segment and $l_0\le c_s\sqrt k$ the same conclusion holds with exponent two. Thus the interval construction labels the order-$k$ tree. For orders below $k$, pad at a site and restrict the resulting injection. For larger orders, use induction on the added population, removing one leaf at order congruent to one, otherwise $(n-1)\bmod k$ leaves. They can all be removed from the added populations since $h<k$. Restore the deficient edge weight or use [Hall's distinct-leaf extension](../counterexample-bound.md#removing-leaves). Throughout this argument the ordinary core used for the order-$k$ statement is fixed, including its long segment when required. This proves the theorem.

In particular, any counterexample sequence at growing $k$ with all but $c_s\sqrt k$ leaves at $s$ sites must have all ordinary segments shorter than the fixed threshold, $h=O_s(l+1)$, and more than $c_s k^{1/3}$ exceptional leaves after decreasing the common constant. These statements restrict two concentration scales. They leave the full conjecture open.

## Reproduction

The independent checker reconstructs the original compressed edge through the entire new path, recomputes all integer edge sums, checks the extreme, and restores other old edges by removing only zero-parent fillers. Its four inputs include one full 12,149-vertex repair, 54 protected pairs, 104 further twelve-vertex subdivisions and 14,824 actual edges in total. It checks large intervals by sweeping their range endpoints. The finite examples test the constructions; the constant and all-orders assertions use the uniform proofs above.
