# One vertex of unrestricted degree

**Theorem.** Fix $D\ge 2$. There is a $k_0(D)$ such that every finite tree with at most one vertex of degree greater than $D$ is $k$-cordial for every $k\ge k_0(D)$. Both the order of the tree and the number of nonleaf neighbors of the exceptional vertex are unrestricted. In any sequence of counterexamples with $k\to\infty$, the second-largest vertex degree therefore tends to infinity.

We use the [linear cutoff for a small rooted core](linear-hub-batches.md) and prove a forest extension for the complementary range. Its main feature is that arbitrarily many component roots may have the same prescribed parent label. Their vertex labels supply their incoming edge weights simultaneously.

## A forest with a common external label

Fix $D\ge 3$, $\delta>0$ and $0<\epsilon<1$. Take a rooted forest on $k-1$ free vertices. Every vertex has one incoming edge, to its free parent or, for a component root, to a prescribed external residue. Count that edge in its degree. Assume these degrees are at most $D$, there are at least $\delta k$ edges between free vertices, and all but at most $k^{1-\epsilon}$ of the component roots have the same external residue $b$. For all sufficiently large $k$, the free vertices can receive all residues except a prescribed $u$, with all $k-1$ actual weights different.

Subtract $b$ from all labels, including the external ones and the forbidden label, translating weights by $-2b$. Call a root with external parent zero a primary root. If the number of components is at most $k^{1-\epsilon/2}$, use the [degree-excess forest lemma](../bounded-degree/excess.md#forest-lemma). Otherwise there are more than $k^{1-\epsilon/2}-k^{1-\epsilon}$ primary roots. Each component has a terminal of free degree one. All neighborhoods below refer only to free edges, so their sizes are bounded in terms of $D$.

### Prescribing the identity and selecting the last edges

If $u\ne0$, pin zero at a primary root. Its incoming edge already uses weight zero. If $u=0$, pin opposite nonzero, distinct labels on an internal edge outside distance two of the nonprimary roots. There are at least $\delta k-O_D(k^{1-\epsilon})$ eligible edges and at most one endpoint is a primary root. Its incoming weight, if present, differs from zero. This uses weight zero in both cases and keeps zero off all final vertex sets. Also pin every nonprimary root and all its children. Label these additional pins in parent-before-child order avoiding previous vertex labels, $u$ and all previous weights on newly induced edges. Their components are separate from a pinned primary root, and their pin neighborhoods are disjoint from the chosen opposite pair. Each new pin has just its incoming edge to check. There are $O_D(k^{1-\epsilon}+1)$ pins. If $u\ne0$, the only free vertex of label zero is a primary root, so no free child of a primary root can have label zero. This ensures that even when primary roots are assigned late, their external zero and their assigned free neighbors are distinct.

Put $t=\lceil2(\log k)^7\rceil$ and $r=\lfloor\delta k/(1000(D+3)(t-1))\rfloor$. Set aside one vertex of degree $d\ge2$ and two nonadjacent vertices of degree one, away from the pins and their neighbors and away from one another. There are at least $\delta k/(D-1)$ choices of the first kind by summing free degrees minus one, and at least one terminal per component for the other choices. These reserved vertices and their free neighbors may be excluded when choosing gadgets. Only the reserves for the chosen type of gadget will be needed.

If there are at least $10r$ nonsingleton components, take a nonroot terminal edge in each. These edges form a matching. Discard edges meeting a pin, reserve or their free neighbors, and keep $r$. Otherwise connect the roots of the nonsingleton components in a chain. The resulting tree has maximum degree at most $D+2$ and at least $\delta k$ vertices. Lemma 3.7 supplies at least $\delta k/(10(D+2)t)$ vertex-disjoint terminal edges or at least $\delta k/(10t)$ disjoint bare paths of length $t$. Discard every gadget meeting one of the fewer than $10r$ roots, and those meeting any pin or reserve or their neighbors. At least $r$ survive. These terminal edges are oriented toward genuine nonroot terminals, and every path vertex in this case is a nonroot. Keep $r$ gadgets of the same type. [Müyesser and Pokrovskiy, Lemma 3.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05280v2#S3.SS3)

In the matching case mark the parents, defer the children, and reserve the vertex of degree $d$ for the end. In the path case orient each path, mark both endpoints by type, defer its $t-1$ interior vertices, and reserve the two degree-one vertices. The orientations need not agree with the rooted forest. All gadget edges will be colored at the end.

### Embedding the remaining forest

Remove the pins, the active reserves and the deferred vertices. Split the remaining forest into pieces of order less than $m=\lceil k^{1/10000}\rceil$ by deleting at most $k/m$ more vertices. Process each tree upwards. When the as-yet attached part below a vertex reaches size $m$ counting the vertex itself, select that vertex as separator and dismiss its whole current part from the upwards count. Its child parts each have size less than $m$. The sets charged to different separators are disjoint. The only edges still joining vertices in different pieces would have to pass a removed vertex.

Order each piece parent before child, starting with its top vertex in the original orientation. Give its $j$th vertex a role specified by $j$, its marked endpoint type if any, and its primary-root status. The number of roles is $O(m)$. Primary roots occur only in position one. Each role at $j\ge2$ has one internal edge per vertex. Within that role these edges form a matching because every piece contributes at most one vertex at that position.

Give each vertex position a role, including one dummy position for $u$. Use the above bulk roles, a role $V_*$ for all deferred new vertices, and leftover roles for pins, reserves, separators and the dummy. Split leftover roles by endpoint type and primary-root status where necessary. Give each of the $k$ color positions a role too. Each primary-root role has its own color role of equal size for its external edges. Each bulk role at position $j\ge2$ has its own internal color role of equal size. Use $C_*$ for the gadget edges, and gather all remaining colors, including one final omitted weight, in a leftover role. A primary root can never be deferred. The matching parents may include primary roots.

Normalize role sizes by $k$. At each residue assign joint vertex and color roles independently of all other residues. A primary-root vertex role is paired with its own color role, with probability its mass and with no other occurrence of that row or column. The remaining rows and columns each sum to $g=1-\#\{\text{primary roots}\}/k\ge\delta$. Require independence between $V_*$ and $C_*$, and in the path case require jointly the two marked endpoint types and $V_*$ to be independent of $C_*$. All these rows are nonprimary in the path case. For a remaining row $i$ of mass $a_i$ and column $j$ of mass $b_j$, use probability
$$
a_i b_j+(1-g)D_{ij}.
$$
Here $D$ is a transportation table with marginals $a_i,b_j$ supported outside the independence rectangle. Its constrained row mass is at most $(t+1)r/k<g/2$, and constrained column mass is at most $tr/k<g/2$. Thus the required table exists by weighted Hall. The marginals and independence, including the complement of the selected vertex types, follow by summation. All roles on either one side are disjoint categorical random sets. Their coordinate trials are independent on that side. The required independence between selected vertex types and $C_*$ holds for their entire arrays. [Transportation proof and finite implementation](branching-pools.md#several-approximate-embeddings-in-the-same-cyclic-group)

In each position-one role, assign distinct labels from its random set until that role or its available vertices are exhausted. For a primary root this spends its label as a color at once. Every role size differs from its expectation by at most $k^{0.6}$ with high probability, simultaneously over the $O(m)$ roles, by concentration. In increasing order of $j\ge2$, treat the roles separately. If the density $p$ of a role is smaller than $k^{-1/600}$, skip it. Otherwise its vertex and color sets are each $p$-random. The parent labels of its remaining children are distinct, belong to earlier vertex roles and number at most $pk$. Apply Lemma 7.7 to match these parents into the vertex role using distinct colors in its color role, with at most $2k^{1-1/500}$ failures. The sum coloring of the loopless complete graph is proper, and for $k>2$ uses all $k$ colors. The lemma explicitly allows dependence between its two random sets. Its conclusion is simultaneous for all eligible parent sets with failure probability at most $k^{-2}$ per role. [Müyesser and Pokrovskiy, Appendix, Lemma 7.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05280v2#A1)

Omit each skipped or unmatched vertex and its descendants within its piece. A loss costs at most $m$ vertices. The total number lost is $O(m^2(k^{1-1/600}+k^{1-1/500}+k^{0.6}))=O(k^{1-\eta})$ for some $\eta>0$. This also counts any skipped small roles. The bulk assignment is injective and all its current weights, including the incoming weights of its primary roots, are distinct. Remove any bulk vertex colliding with a pin or the dummy, endpoints of bulk edges whose weights collide with pin weights (for a boundary edge remove its free endpoint), and all free neighbors of pins. At most $O_D(k^{1-\epsilon}+1)$ more vertices are removed, since the bulk labels and bulk weights are each distinct. Reinstate the original pins. Every currently induced edge has now been checked.

Apart from the deferred vertices and active reserves, only $O(k^{1-\gamma})$ vertices remain unassigned for a fixed $\gamma>0$, decreasing $\eta$ to account for the separators and pins. Each deletion or restoration changes at most $D$ weights. The actual marked endpoint images, when restored, differ from the union of their reference roles by $O(k^{1-\gamma})$. The unspent label and color sets will likewise differ from $V_*,C_*$ by that error. Indeed almost every bulk vertex and its internal edge (if present) used its own role, the sum of all role-size fluctuations is $O(mk^{0.6})$, and leftovers beyond the primary and bulk roles account for at most $O_D(k^{1-\gamma})$ positions outside the gadgets. For the last count, every nonprimary boundary edge belongs to a pin, and every other nongadget edge outside the bulk or primary roles touches a pin, separator or reserve.

### Repair and the last group sum

The independent arrays $V_*,C_*$ each have density at least a constant times $(\log k)^{-7}$. For every list of at most $D$ distinct neighbor labels $p_i$, there are simultaneously $\Omega(k/(\log k)^{O_D(1)})$ values $x\in V_*$ with every $x+p_i\in C_*$. One can additionally require $A+Bx\in C_*$ for any $A$ and integer $1\le|B|\le D$. Indeed, each trial asks for a bounded number of coordinates in two independent arrays and has probability at least an inverse power of $\log k$. Each color coordinate occurs in at most $D+|B|$ trials. A linear number of trials can therefore be made independent, and Chernoff's bound permits a union bound over all lists, $A,B$. Polynomially sublinear contamination excludes only $O_D(k^{1-\gamma})$ choices. This argument includes composite $k$.

Restore the missing vertices apart from the active reserves and deferred gadgets. Use the preceding choices with all already assigned free neighbors and any external label at that vertex. Nonprimary roots are already pinned. At a primary root the external zero is distinct from its free neighbors, as arranged above. Incident weights are therefore distinct and can all be chosen fresh. Every restoration uses at most one label from $V_*$ and $D$ colors from $C_*$. The discrepancy estimates still hold.

**A final matching.** Write $V,C$ for all unspent labels and colors before assigning the degree-$d$ reserve $z$, and $X$ for the marked parent labels. Let $p_1,\ldots,p_d$ be the actual neighbor labels of $z$, including an external zero if applicable. The weight to omit is forced to be
$$
s=A-(d-1)z,\qquad A=\sum C-\sum V-\sum X-\sum_i p_i.
$$
Choose a fresh $z$ with all $z+p_i$ and $s$ fresh in $C_*$. Each possible coincidence $s=z+p_i$ excludes at most $d$ values of $z$. The remaining labels $Y$ and remaining colors $Z$ after omitting $s$ now have size $r$ and satisfy $\sum X+\sum Y-\sum Z=0$. None of $X,Y,Z$ includes zero, by the identity pin or dummy and by the use of weight zero away from the gadgets.

Put $p=r/k$. The reference sets for $X,Y,-Z$ are each $p$-random, with reflection on the color side. They are $q$-slightly-independent in the sense of Definition 4.1 with $q=p^2/3$. Independently for each inverse orbit $\{x,-x\}$, choose one of the three indices uniformly. Put the whole orbit in that index's symmetric subset if both coordinates lie in its reference set. On a singleton orbit require membership and additionally accept with probability $p$. Each specified index then receives an orbit with probability exactly $q$, the three choices are disjoint, and distinct orbits are independent. Reflection preserves the orbit. This gives the required coupling even when all matching parents are primary roots. The actual sets differ from their references by $O(k^{1-\gamma})$, eventually below $q^{10^{17}}k/(\log k)^{10^{17}}$, and $q\ge k^{-1/10^{101}}$ eventually. Theorem 4.3 now gives a perfect matching of triples $x+y-z=0$ on the three actual sets. Assign each child its matched label. [Müyesser and Pokrovskiy, Definition 4.1 and Theorem 4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2204.09666#S4) · [Published paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-025-01328-x)

**Final paths.** Write $P$ for the sum of the labels at all marked endpoints, $p,q$ for the parents of the two degree-one reserves, and $V,C$ for the unspent sets before assigning these reserves. A parent may be external, and $p=q$ is allowed. For reserve labels $z,w$ the required omitted weight is
$$
s=A+z+w,\qquad A=\sum C-P-2\sum V-p-q.
$$
This follows by removing $z+p,w+q$ from the color side and counting every final middle label twice on the vertex side. First choose a fresh $w\in V_*$ with $w+q$ fresh in $C_*$, avoiding $A+w=p$. Then choose fresh $z\in V_*$ with both $z+p$ and $z+A+w$ fresh in $C_*$. These two translates are different and both avoid $w+q$. Set the labels and omit $s$. The endpoints, middle labels and remaining colors have cardinalities $r,r,(t-1)r,tr$ and satisfy the required sum equation. The three vertex reference sets are disjoint random sets, independent of the color reference as a triple. Their discrepancies satisfy the polynomial error bound above. The middle density tends to the positive constant $\delta/(1000(D+3))$. If a fixed density parameter is used in Lemma 3.2, couple the rounded categorical probabilities with that constant and the corresponding endpoint and color densities by changing $O(t+\sqrt{k}\log k)$ coordinates with high probability, preserving the required independence. Lemma 3.2 supplies the paths for the prescribed pairing of endpoints. This proves the forest lemma. [Müyesser and Pokrovskiy, Lemma 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05280v2#S3.SS1)

## All tree orders

Choose the exceptional vertex $o$, or any root if all degrees are bounded. Deleting its $m$ direct leaf neighbors leaves an ordinary tree $H$ of order $h$, with every branch at $o$ having at least two vertices. The single-vertex tree is immediate. Fix the constant $K_D\ge1$ from the [small-core theorem](linear-hub-batches.md). When $k>K_D(h+1)$ that theorem gives cordiality at every population $m$. Otherwise $h\ge\delta_0 k$ for sufficiently large $k$, where $\delta_0=1/(2K_D+2)$.

At order $k$, put zero at $o$. Delete $o$ and take its label as the common external boundary. Its ordinary branches have at least $(h-1)/2$ internal edges in total. Its $m$ leaves contribute singleton components. The forest lemma with forbidden label zero and density $\delta_0/3$ gives distinct labels and distinct weights. For order less than $k$ in the dense case pad to order $k$ by direct leaves at $o$, retaining the same ordinary branches, and restrict the injection. This settles all orders at most $k$.

For larger orders use strong induction within the entire class at this fixed $k$. At order $1\bmod k$ remove a leaf other than $o$, then choose its label to give the unique deficient edge weight. Otherwise put $t=(n-1)\bmod k>0$. If $m\ge t$, remove $t$ direct leaves and restore them with distinct labels avoiding the unique surplus vertex residue of the prefix. Their common parent guarantees distinct new weights. In the remaining case $m<t$ and $h>k$. Put $a=\lfloor k/8\rfloor$, $b=\min(m,\max(0,t-a))$ and $s=t-b$. Remove $b$ direct leaves and the last $s$ ordinary vertices of the preorder of $H$ rooted at $o$, traversing children in increasing subtree size. The remaining tree is connected, belongs to the induction class and has order $1\bmod k$.

The ordinary suffix consists of whole nonsingleton branches at $o$ and possibly part of one further branch. Inside that partial branch the number of distinct boundary parents is at most $2+\log_2 k$ by the [ordered traversal bound](../rainbow/ordered-cut.md). Each such parent has degree at most $D$. Hence at most $L=D(2+\log_2 k)$ suffix components have a boundary parent other than $o$. The number of internal edges among the $s$ ordinary vertices is at least $s/2-L$. Each full branch contributes at least half its order. If the partial branch supplies $v$ vertices in $\ell$ components, it contributes $v-\ell\ge v/2-L$. The $b$ removed direct leaves are further primary singleton components.

Extend one terminal of the ordinary suffix by a temporary path of length $k-1-t$, counting every added edge as internal. The padded forest has order $k-1$, maximum free degree at most $\max(D,3)$, and at least $s/2-L+k-1-t$ internal edges. When $t\ge a$ one has $s\ge a$, and when $t<a$ the padding alone has length $k-1-t$. Thus at least $k/20$ edges are internal for sufficiently large $k$. Label the prefix by induction. Apply the forest lemma with its surplus residue forbidden, with $o$'s actual label as the common external residue, and with the $O_D(\log k)$ exceptions just bounded. Discard the temporary path. Distinct weights extend the prefix's uniform edge frequencies, and distinct vertex labels avoiding its unique surplus give balanced vertex frequencies. This completes the induction.

For any fixed $D$ only finitely many isomorphism classes of tree–modulus pairs in this class can fail. The theorem leaves finitely many moduli, and for each of them the [linear order bound](../large-order/linear.md) leaves finitely many trees. The threshold in the present argument is asymptotic. The all-moduli conjecture remains open.
