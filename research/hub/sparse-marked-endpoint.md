# Up to a cube-root power of leaves away from finitely many vertices

**Theorem.** Fix $s\ge1$ and $0<\alpha<1/3$. For all sufficiently large $k$, every finite tree with all but at most $k^\alpha$ leaves adjacent to at most $s$ vertices is $k$-cordial. The tree order, the positions of the sites and all individual populations are unrestricted. The same proof works along sequences with $l=o(k^{1/3}/\log(2k))$ leaves away from the sites. At one site the [square-root result](quadratic-root.md) still gives the larger range.

The improvement on the [exponent below $1/6$](polynomial-marked.md) uses a seed that consumes only linearly many filler leaves at all parents other than zero, even though its full interval can have cubic width.

## A marked interval with limited cost away from zero

Use the marked setup, witness pins, single preliminary leaf at each positive site and bounded-leaf hull of the [polynomial marked-site proof](polynomial-marked.md#a-seed-of-width-at-most-a-constant-times-the-third-power). Allow a bounded number of extra pins. On an ordinary tree of order $b$, the seed can be chosen with all the following properties, or the original residual caterpillar already supplies the result.

1. All site and pin labels belong to a bounded set. Fillers are allowed only at the specified leading sites. The large-site moment slacks and choice of triple, quadruple or two-site anchors are as before.
2. The vertex and edge intervals are $[L,U]$ and $[L+1,U]$, with width $O_s((b+1)^3)$.
3. At most $O_s(b+1)$ fillers lie at nonzero parents in total. We may ensure $-L\ge U+D_s$ for any prescribed constant $D_s$.

Select repair branches and windows exactly as in the [extreme integer construction](cubic-endpoint.md), protecting every site, the witness pins and any additional pins. No last broom is required. In a repair alternative, label the bounded-leaf hull on the compressed tree as before. It has width $O_s(b+1)$ and all pins have bounded digits. At three or more sites, if the largest site has digit one and we require it at zero, reflect the **initial interval** now. The largest site then supplies zero, the moment slacks swap, and a reflected triple supplies a quadruple with the new zero. At two sites take the desired orientation at the outset. Pad the lower end by a bounded number of leaves to avoid the three bad values for an initial gap at a common nonzero star supplier. The entire hull seed, including this padding, costs $O_s(b+1)$ leaves and its difference is minus its lower endpoint. The [two-group forest preparation](integer-forest.md) adds only ordinary vertices, with quadratic magnitudes and at most 864 guarded pairs.

The extreme repairs work for this starting gap and these ordinary boundary labels. They use only the protected-pair inequalities and the common-supplier initial gap condition, with at most $O_s(b+1)$ occupied labels from the hull. They restore actual vertices only. Their final gap is a strict minimum with $-\min V>\max V+D_s$, by increasing the constant in the final parameter scale to cover $D_s$. The integer span is cubic. Fill all holes by leaves at zero. This adds nothing at nonzero parents and proves all three assertions. In the alternative with boundedly many nonleaf vertices include all of them, the sites and the pins in the initial hull. It has bounded order, including any terminal vertices needed for a witness. All other vertices are terminal children of bounded parent labels. They take $O_s(b+1)$ fillers by the interval insertion rules, and both endpoints have magnitude $O_s(b+1)$. Further lower padding of that same order ensures the desired inequality. This argument handles arbitrarily many nonleaves on the hull through the repair alternatives above.

## Protecting an expansion edge of bounded label

In the long-segment case of the [shortening proof](polynomial-marked.md#long-subdivisions-and-arbitrary-moduli), keep the retained part of a longest segment pinned, at most 21 vertices. For two sites there is an edge with strictly opposite signs on that part, by the crossing argument in that proof. **Both its endpoint digits are bounded**, since they are pinned. Orient the final zero at the intended site before constructing the sparse seed.

With three or more sites reserve two real internal vertices on the longest segment in shortening, as in the previous proof. They can produce a bounded opposite-sign edge *before* restoring the long pieces of the hull. Here are the order and costs explicitly. Pin all vertices of the retained short segment and treat their intervening edges as adjacent pins. The structural selection therefore leaves these edges in the compressed tree. On the bounded parity or shortened hull with its bounded seed, after choosing any necessary reflection, use one of those edges $d,e$, oriented with $d\ne0$. Insert a low child $q$ at $d$ and an upper child $p=d+e-q$ at $e$, using only the selected anchors, and reconnect $q,p$ in place of $d,e$. Ensure $q<0<p$ and $d+q\ne0$. Since the hull at this stage has bounded order and seed width, all these positions and filler counts are bounded. The edge chosen for this operation lies between consecutive pins and has retained length one, so it requires no other hull elongation. The two new vertices are actual vertices taken from the distinguished segment's surplus. All other hull segments restore as in the linear-width hull proof. The inserted edge survives forest preparation and every repair. This gives the required bounded opposite-sign edge for three or more sites as well. The finite seed and hull constants can be chosen uniformly over the possible initial shapes, orientations and marked-site profiles.

Let $W=O_s((l+1)^3)$ bound the resulting short-core seed width, on a core of $O_s(l+1)$ vertices. The negative endpoint $q$, positive endpoint $p$ and upper block length for a child at $q$ are all bounded. Choose the constant $D_s$ of the preceding section sufficiently large over this finite collection. Then the first [asymmetric buffer move](bounded-marked-sites.md#a-wide-interval-with-few-leaves-away-from-zero) is feasible with $x=L-p$ and $y=2p+q-L$: the inequalities $x\le q-d'-2$ and $y-i>U$ hold, where $d'$ is the upper block length and $i$ its child slot. This uses $0\le U$ and $-L\ge U+D_s$. Bounded extra lower padding would also be harmless. The two-site formulas are the same for $q<0$. All subsequent moves use this same $q$. Hence each costs $O_s(1)$ leaves away from zero.

For an ordinary tree of order $h=o(k)$ choose a longest segment with enough surplus for $C_s\log(2k)$ moves when one exists. Stop at a positive multiple of six moves with both radii above $16D+4W_1$ for $D=100(h+I+1)$ and $W_1=O_s(W+1)$ bounding the starting short-core labels; $I$ counts existing fillers away from zero. The proof of [stopping and neutral restoration](polynomial-marked.md#long-subdivisions-and-arbitrary-moduli) gives width $O_s(h+W+\log(2k))$ and $I=O_s(l+1+\log(2k))$. In particular, even if $-L$ starts much larger than $U$, after the first move the endpoint magnitudes are within a fixed factor up to a bounded additive constant. All other length restorations replace leaves at zero. If no segment is long enough, apply the seed lemma directly on the full core, now of order $O_s((l+1)\log(2k))$. In either case the costs are
$$
 o(k)\text{ in total},\qquad O_s((l+1)\log(2k))\text{ away from zero}
$$
whenever $l=o(k^{1/3}/\log(2k))$. These bounds include the initial seed and all length restorations.

## Completing the populations

Use the [positive-density and leaf-removal reductions](polynomial-marked.md#long-subdivisions-and-arbitrary-moduli). Along a sequence of order-$k$ failures the ordinary order is $h=o(k)$ and the second population satisfies $m_2\ge c_s\sqrt k$. This uses the one-site theorem since the ordinary leaves and the leaves at all smaller sites together number at most $l+(s-1)m_2$. If $m_2$ has positive limiting proportion of $k$, choose all sites of positive limiting proportion after passing to a subsequence. They pay the total $o(k)$ cost and absorb the remaining $o(k)$ populations within their moment slacks.

Otherwise $m_1=k-o(k)$. Let $R=(l+1)\log(2k)=o(\sqrt k)$. Choose the last $j$ with $m_j/R\to\infty$ after passing to a subsequence so that all these ratios have extended-real limits. We have $j\ge2$ and the later populations total $O(R)$. The bounded large-site digits and the two moment inequalities force the largest digit to be zero or one, as before. Make it zero in the initial hull. The $O_s(R)$ nonzero-parent cost and the later small populations reduce the lower moment slack $\eta_s m_j$ by at most $O(R)$. The zero-parent cost is $o(k)$ and the other large sites have only $o(k)$ mass in total, so the other slack is still $k-o(k)$. Every anchor has ample reserve for the [exact population blocks](marked-leaf-threshold.md#realizing-the-exact-leaf-populations). This contradicts the failure. Padding, Hall's extension and the positive-density theorem give every other tree order as in the linked reduction.

Six combined marked-endpoint replays grow the compressed interval through 36 buffer moves, using at most twelve nonzero-parent leaves in any replay. Each reconstructs a further eight neutral subdivisions and checks both full integer intervals against the actual edges. [Combined verifier](check_polynomial_marked.py)
