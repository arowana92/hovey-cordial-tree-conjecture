# Arbitrary leaf populations on a positive-density branching core

**Theorem.** Fix $D\ge3$ and $0<\delta,\epsilon<1$. For all sufficiently large $k$, let $H$ be a tree of order $h\ge\delta k$ with
$$
E_D(H)=\sum_v (\deg_H(v)-D)_+\le k^{1-\epsilon}.
$$
At any set of at most $k^{1-\epsilon}$ vertices of $H$, add independently prescribed, arbitrarily many leaves. Every resulting tree is $k$-cordial. There is no upper bound on $h$, on the total order, or on an added population.

For example the ordinary core can be a complete binary tree with linearly many distinct leaf neighbors. Its added leaf populations can create arbitrarily high degrees at widely separated or adjacent sites. The [earlier positive-density theorem](permuted-pools.md) required a bound on the total number of ordinary leaves. The [degree-excess theorem](../bounded-degree/excess.md) applies here to the ordinary core itself. To handle the added populations we couple a permutation for those leaves to several approximate embeddings and one exact completion.

## The prescribed forest problem

Take $k-1$ free vertices, with one incoming edge each. The ordinary vertices form a rooted forest, the root of each component having its edge to a prescribed external residue. Other free vertices are terminal decorations with prescribed multiplicities at ordinary vertices or external residues. Require distinct free labels avoiding one specified residue $u$ and distinct weights on all incoming edges. The number of ordinary vertices is $a\ge\delta k$, and the component count, the ordinary excess above $D$ (including external incoming edges), and the number of decoration sites are each at most $k^{1-\epsilon}$. We prove this forest statement first. Throughout the proof a bound $O(k^{1-c})$ may use a smaller positive constant $c$ at successive steps, in terms of the fixed parameters.

Add a dummy position of label $u$ without an edge. The ordinary vertex positions counting the dummy and the ordinary colors counting one eventual unused weight both number $a+1=gk$. The decoration count is $m=k(1-g)$. For $m\le1$ use the [prescribed degree-excess forest lemma](../bounded-degree/excess.md#forest-lemma) on the entire forest, slightly decreasing its exponent. Henceforth $m\ge2$.

Pin all ordinary component roots, all ordinary decoration sites, and every ordinary vertex of degree above $D$ together with its ordinary neighbors. Counting the total degrees of high vertices by $(D+1)E_D$ shows that these pins and all incident ordinary edges total $O_D(k^{1-\epsilon})$: apart from the high vertices every pin has bounded degree. Greedily assign the pins distinct labels avoiding $u$ and with distinct weights on their induced edges and each root's external edge. In original parent-before-child order each pin has at most one earlier incoming constraint and no earlier children. External labels may repeat or equal other free labels. All decoration shifts are now fixed.

Put $t=\lceil 2(\log k)^7\rceil$, $r=\lfloor\delta k/(1000(D+3)(t-1))\rfloor$. Removing the pins leaves $O_D(k^{1-\epsilon})$ bounded-degree components. Join them in a chain using one representative from each, increasing maximum degree by at most two. This auxiliary tree has at least $\delta k-O_D(k^{1-\epsilon})$ vertices. The leaf-or-bare-path lemma gives either a matching of leaf edges of size $\Omega(\delta k/((D+2)t))$ or that many vertex-disjoint bare paths of length $t$; specifically the divisors in the lemma are $10(D+2)t$ and $10t$. Discard gadgets meeting join vertices or original neighbors of pins; there are only $O_D(k^{1-\epsilon})$ such gadgets. Keep precisely $r$ surviving gadgets of one type. The leaf edges are genuine ordinary terminal edges (roots were pinned), and the bare paths retain their incidences in the original forest. [Müyesser and Pokrovskiy, §3.3, Lemma 3.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05280v2#S3.SS3)

Defer the $r$ terminal children or the $(t-1)r$ internal path vertices, and all gadget edges. Mark the $r$ matching parents, or mark separately the starts and ends of the oriented paths. In the matching case reserve one additional ordinary vertex $z$ of degree $2\le d\le D$ outside the pins, their neighbors, and the selected gadgets. Such vertices number $\Omega_D(\delta k)$ before the gadget exclusion: the residual forest has average degree $2-o(1)$ and maximum degree $D$. At most $2r$ vertices are on the matching. All neighbors of $z$ are ordinary and have different labels once embedded.

## Several approximate embeddings in the same cyclic group

After removing pins, deferred vertices and the optional $z$, split large components by deleting centroids until each has at most $a/12$ vertices. At each halving depth there are at most twelve larger components, and at most four depths suffice. These deleted vertices will be assigned later. Group the remaining components into a bounded number of forests $F_i$, each of size at most $a/4$: greedily fill a group until the next component would exceed this bound. Each finished group then has at least $a/6$ vertices. Within each $F_i$ distinguish the two endpoint classes (one class in the matching case) and the remaining vertices.

The following finite coupling describes all palettes. The ordinary **vertex roles** are the three classes in each batch, the deferred vertices, and all leftover positions, split by endpoint class where needed. Their probabilities $a_j$ are the corresponding counts divided by $k$. The ordinary **color roles** are the internal edges of each $F_i$, the deferred gadget edges, and all remaining colors including the eventual gap, with probabilities $b_l$ defined likewise. Both role totals are $g$. Write $V_*,C_*$ for the random roles of deferred new vertices and gadget colors. Use the following independence constraints:

- All vertex roles belonging to $F_i$ must be jointly independent of its own edge-color role.
- The deferred vertex role and all marked endpoint roles together must be jointly independent of the gadget color role.

For each vertex row the constrained columns have total mass at most $g/4+tr/k<g/2$. For each color column the constrained rows have mass at most $\max(g/4,(t+1)r/k,2r/k)<g/2$. There exists a transportation table $D_{jl}$ with row sums $a_j$, column sums $b_l$, supported **off** the constrained cells. For a nonempty set of rows of mass at most $g/2$, even one row can access columns of mass at least $g/2$. A set of larger mass can access every column. This is weighted Hall, or ordinary Hall after making copies in units of $1/k$. Use joint probabilities
$$
J_{jl}=a_jb_l+(1-g)D_{jl}.
$$
Give the pair of outside roles probability $1-g$ and all mixed inside/outside cells probability zero. The marginals are correct; in every required rectangle, every cell has precisely product mass. This gives joint independence of the selected categorical vertex variable (taking a common default off those rows) and its selected color indicator, by subtraction for the default category.

Use Hall's prescribed-differences theorem to choose a permutation $\pi$ of $\mathbb Z_k$ from the rounded decoration quotas, changing one entry to make their total displacement zero. Independently at each $x$ draw roles according to $J$ for vertex $x$ and color $\pi(x)$. Thus the two sets of all ordinary roles are permutation images, while each required independence holds for the **entire indicator arrays**, even after the color coordinates are reordered. Each side separately consists of disjoint random sets, meaning independent categorical trials across its coordinates. [Marshall Hall Jr., primary theorem](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1952-0050579-7)

Assign the $m$ decorations by their shifts as in the [permuted quota proof](permuted-pools.md#meeting-every-leaf-demand), using only its quota stage. For clarity, the candidates outside the ordinary roles consist of arcs $x\to\pi(x)$ of the correct type. Their total expected absolute count error from the quotas is $O(\sqrt{sk}+s+1)$ for $s\le k^{1-\epsilon}$ shifts, by summing the binomial standard deviations and rounding errors. Omit the dummy arc and arcs colliding with pins or their colors. By Markov's inequality all but $O(k^{1-c})$ demands are filled with high probability. Repair these in the ordinary roles: for each shift $p$ there are $\Omega_\delta(k)$ pairs $y,y+p$ in the ordinary vertex/color sets. Indeed each trial requests at most two ordinary vertex coordinates $y,\pi^{-1}(y+p)$ and at least $k/4$ trials can be chosen disjointly. Apply Chernoff and a union bound, then exclude the few used or pinned positions. The final ordinary pools, the complements of the decoration sources and their colors, differ from their random unions by $O(k^{1-c})$. Counts of unused outside coordinates follow also from concentration of the union sizes. Colors on pinned edges and the dummy and pin labels have been protected.

For each $F_i$, use the primary approximate embedding lemma for a bounded-degree forest in the properly $k$-colored sum graph on $\mathbb Z_k$. It gives, with high probability, a rainbow injection whose image, its two marked class images, and its edge colors differ by $O(k^{1-c})$ from their prescribed random roles. More explicitly, Lemma 7.9 and Definition 3.4 give a joint law of the embedding and witnesses consisting of disjoint random vertex categories, independent of the random color array; the third category follows by subtraction. Their parameters are $|U_j|/k$ and $|E(F_i)|/k$. The forest size is at most $k/4$ so the required slack holds. We have constructed exactly this witness marginal for each $i$. Sample its embedding conditional on those witnesses, using the lemma's finite joint law. Its marginal success probability stays the same. No independence *between* embeddings is required; there are boundedly many batches. Empty classes or batches cause no constraint. [Müyesser and Pokrovskiy, Definition 3.4 and Lemma 3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05280v2#S3.SS2) · [Their rainbow embedding construction, Lemma 7.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05280v2#A1)

Within each batch discard vertices outside their own assigned roles or colliding with pins or decoration labels. Discard endpoints of any edge outside its batch color role or colliding with a pin or decoration color. The injections and rainbowness inside each batch bound the discards by $O(k^{1-c})$, using the pool discrepancy and class errors. Also discard neighbors of pins, so every edge currently present was checked in a pin set or a single batch. All surviving vertices and colors of different batches are disjoint. The number of vertices to restore, including the centroid separators but excluding the deferred gadgets and $z$, is $O(k^{1-c})$. Each restoration has at most $D$ already assigned neighbors. The remaining desired labels and colors for the deferred factor are within $O(k^{1-c})$ of $V_*,C_*$ once restorations are done: the bulk class images and colors exhaust their random roles up to these errors, and the leftover roles at pins, separators and their incident edges have total expected size $O(k^{1-c})$. Here deleting a vertex costs at most $D$ batch colors. The marked endpoint sets also differ from their random roles by $O(k^{1-c})$.

## Restoring vertices and fixing the omitted weight

The arrays $V_*,C_*$ are independent. Their densities are bounded below by a constant times $(\log k)^{-7}$, with positive constant vertex density in the path case. With high probability, simultaneously for every list of at most $D$ different neighbor labels $p_i$, there are $\Omega(k/(\log k)^{O_D(1)})$ choices $v\in V_*$ with $v+p_i\in C_*$ for all $i$. This remains true with one further color constraint $A+Bv\in C_*$ for any $A$ and any integer $1\le |B|\le D$. For each $v$ the success probability is at least a fixed inverse power of $\log k$. In the arrays each color coordinate is requested in at most $D+|B|$ trials, since a congruence with coefficient $B$ has at most $|B|$ preimages. Thus a positive fraction of the trials can be taken disjoint, uniformly at composite moduli too. Chernoff's inequality beats the polynomial number of lists and constants. Discarding $O(k^{1-c})$ used coordinates still leaves choices. Use this property to restore the ordinary vertices in any order, with distinct labels and unused incident weights from these two palettes. Different already assigned neighbors give different incident weights. All ordinary roots with external edges were pinned from the outset.

**Matching.** Let $V,C$ be all unspent labels and colors before assigning $z$, and $X$ the labels of the $r$ actual matching parents. The unused weight after assigning $z$ and completing the matching must be
$$
s=A-(d-1)z,\qquad A=\sum C-\sum V-\sum X-\sum_i p_i,
$$
where $p_i$ are the $d$ neighbor labels. Indeed assigning $z$ spends one vertex and the colors $z+p_i$, and each final matching color sums one parent and one new label. Choose $z\in V_*$ with all $z+p_i$ and $s$ in the available $C_*$. The strengthened property just proved applies since $d\ge2$. Exclude $s=z+p_i$ as well; each equality forbids at most $d$ labels. After setting $z$, let $Y$ be the $r$ remaining labels and $Z$ the $r$ remaining colors after omitting $s$. Then $\sum X+\sum Y=\sum Z$.

The reference roles for $X,Y$ are disjoint $r/k$-random sets and their color role is an independent $r/k$-random set. All discrepancies are $O(k^{1-c})$. This is smaller than $(r/k)^{10^{18}}k/(\log k)^{10^{18}}$ for sufficiently large $k$. The cyclic group is not elementary abelian of exponent two at these orders. The primary perfect matching theorem now places one new leaf at each actual parent, using exactly $Y,Z$. [Müyesser and Pokrovskiy, Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05280v2#S3.SS1)

**Paths.** Let $X_1,X_2$ be the endpoint label sets, $Y$ the unspent labels and $C$ the unspent colors. The required omitted weight is $s=\sum C-\sum X_1-\sum X_2-2\sum Y$, since every internal path vertex is used twice. If $s\notin C$, take an already placed decorated leaf of label $x$ and parent $p$ with $x+p\ne s$, possible since $m\ge2$. Move it to a currently unused $y\in V_*$ with $y+p$ and $y+s-x$ both in the unspent $C_*$. This uses the same reservoir property for two distinct translates. The resulting required omitted weight is $s+y-x$ and it remains available, being different from $y+p$. Only one leaf assignment changed.

The start and end roles are disjoint $r/k$-random sets and the new-vertex role is a disjoint $(t-1)r/k$-random set. The gadget color role is an independent $tr/k$-random set. The middle density is bounded below in terms of $D,\delta$; alternatively thin or enlarge its reference roles by $O(t/k)$ in the probabilities to replace the integer rounding by a fixed density, at a cost $O(t+\sqrt k\log k)$ with high probability. The four sets have correct cardinalities, the sum condition just established, and polynomially sublinear errors. Lemma 3.2 supplies the paths for the prescribed bijection of starts to ends, with exactly the requisite vertices and colors. This proves the forest statement. [Müyesser and Pokrovskiy, Lemma 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05280v2#S3.SS1)

## From prescribed forests to all tree orders

At total order $k$, fix one ordinary root's residue and delete it from $H$. Its neighbors become ordinary component roots with that external parent. The component count is at most $D+E_D(H)$ and the other ordinary degrees do not change when their external edges are counted. The fixed root's own decorations may still be assigned using its residue. The forest statement (decrease $\delta,\epsilon$ slightly) supplies all $k-1$ remaining labels and distinct weights. If $h\le k$, pad to total order $k$ and restrict for smaller populations; choose one temporary site if necessary. For larger populations use induction on total order, removing one added leaf at orders $1\bmod k$ or $t=(n-1)\bmod k$ leaves otherwise. The former leaf can restore the deficient weight and the latter use [Hall's distinct-leaf lemma](../counterexample-bound.md#removing-leaves) avoiding the sole surplus vertex residue.

For $h>k$ use induction on the total order with the same parameters, first decreasing $\delta$ to at most $1/2$. When there are no added leaves the ordinary degree-excess theorem applies. At order $1\bmod k$ remove a decoration if present. When their count $m$ is at least the positive remainder $t$, remove $t$ of them as before. It remains to handle $m<t\le k-1$. Put $u=\lfloor k/8\rfloor$.

If $t<2u$ or $t-m\ge u$, remove **all** decorations and an ordinary suffix of $t-m$ vertices in increasing-child-size preorder. The prefix, now entirely ordinary, admits a labeling by the degree-excess theorem at any order. The removed forest has only $O(D\log k+E_D(H))$ components: the number of distinct boundary parents in this traversal is at most $2+\log_2 k$. [Ordered traversal bound](../rainbow/ordered-cut.md) Extend one ordinary terminal by a temporary path with $k-1-t$ new vertices. This padded ordinary forest has $k-1-m$ vertices, at least $u$ in either case. The added decorations in the suffix may have ordinary free parents or external prescribed residues.

Otherwise $t\ge2u$ and $t-m<u$. We need a suffix with precisely $u$ ordinary vertices that forces the removal of at most $t-u$ decorations. At an ordinary centroid of $H$ every branch has at most $h/2$ vertices. Partition its branches into two groups each of total order at least $u$: take a single branch reaching $u$ if available, or accumulate smaller ones until first reaching $u$. Each group can be searched last in a preorder rooted at the centroid. Within its individual branches use increasing-child-size preorder. Take the last $u$ vertices separately for the two groups. These disjoint candidates each have connected complement. Their boundary parents are the centroid and those along at most one partially used branch, so the same $O(D\log k+E_D(H))$ component bound holds. At least one candidate carries at most $m/2\le t-u$ decorations. Remove it and exactly $t-u$ decorations, including all its own; there are enough since $m>t-u$. The prefix retains at least $h-u>k/2$ ordinary vertices, satisfies the original excess and site bounds and is smaller, so induction labels it. Pad the ordinary suffix by a path of $k-1-t$ vertices; it has at least $u$ ordinary vertices.

In both cases the ordinary free degrees are bounded by their original degrees (at a terminal the temporary extension raises the free degree to at most two). The prefix of order $1\bmod k$ has uniform edge counts and one surplus vertex residue. Apply the forest statement to the padded suffix with this forbidden residue, a fixed density such as $1/9$ and a smaller fixed exponent to absorb the component bound. Restrict to the real suffix. Its distinct new weights and distinct new labels avoiding the surplus give exactly the required balance. The induction proves the theorem.

The independent checks enumerate joint categorical probabilities after permuting coordinates, recompute boundary components and forced leaf budgets on 5,946 pairs of suffixes, and recompute the matching gap on actual labeled trees at prime and composite moduli. On 140 finite instances they also exhaust the terminal matching assignments after choosing an internal label. The asymptotic embedding steps use the primary results cited above.
