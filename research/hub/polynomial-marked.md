# Polynomial leaf bounds at any fixed number of specified vertices

**Integer theorem.** Fix $s\ge2$. There is a constant $C_s$ such that for every tree $H$ of order $h$ and every choice of at most $s$ sites, attaching prescribed leaf populations with second largest at least $C_s(h+1)^3$ gives a super edge-magic tree. The largest population is unrestricted. One ordinary integer labeling gives cordiality at every positive modulus by reduction. [Consecutive sums and cordiality, Youssef, Theorem 4](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/43/ajc_v43_p031.pdf)

**Modulus theorem.** Fix $s$ and $0<\alpha<1/6$. For all sufficiently large $k$ in terms of $s,\alpha$, every tree with all but at most $k^\alpha$ leaves adjacent to at most $s$ vertices is $k$-cordial. All tree orders and all distances between the vertices are allowed. For example $\alpha=1/7$ works for every fixed $s$. For a single site the [square-root theorem](quadratic-root.md) gives a stronger estimate. Here the exponent is uniform in the number of sites, which can be any fixed constant.

## A seed of width at most a constant times the third power

Put one actual leaf from each positive population into the ordinary core at the outset, subtracting it from that request. Its order is still $O_s(h+1)$ and every site with remaining requests is a nonleaf of this core. Order the remaining positive populations $m_1\ge\cdots\ge m_r$. For now choose any initial $j\ge2$ of these sites to supply temporary filler leaves. A bounded number of additional prescribed vertices may be pinned. We will keep **all site and pin labels bounded in terms of their number**. The resulting integer seed has vertices $[L,U]$, weights $[L+1,U]$, and $O_s((h+1)^3)$ fillers, all at the selected sites. Constants also depend on the number of optional pins. The details below retain the moment bound required when $j\ge3$.

For $j=2$, inspect the path between the two selected sites. If their distance is two or four, pin the whole path and the short witness twig from the [two-site seed proof](two-hubs.md), whenever it exists. In the remaining case that proof gives a caterpillar with all core nonleaves on a path. Attaching arbitrary further leaves at the nonleaf sites keeps it a caterpillar and gives the conclusion directly. At distance at least five pin six consecutive vertices starting at the first site on the hub path, to retain at least five edges during any subsequent compression. At distance one or three pin the whole path. For $j\ge3$ no witness pins are needed.

Let $S$ be the smallest connected subtree containing the sites and pins. Its number of leaves is bounded. We repeatedly use the following fact about such a hull, also after adding boundedly many pins or compressing selected windows outside the pins. Make every pin and site a segment endpoint. Shorten the segments of this bounded-leaf hull to bounded lengths, obtaining finitely many shapes. For $j\ge3$ choose the [finite marked seed](marked-leaf-threshold.md#constructing-a-bounded-seed-with-moment-to-spare) for the populations on the first $j$ sites, then restore lengths by [scaled twelve-vertex subdivisions](marked-subdivision.md#restoring-long-segments-at-linear-cost). At $j=2$ use the [asymmetric two-site construction](two-hub-all-orders.md#creating-an-interval-with-few-leaves-at-the-second-site), splitting also at the pins. A short hub witness or the five preserved edges guarantees that the noncaterpillar seed alternative remains available whenever required. If no segment finances the buffer, the hull order $a$ satisfies $a=O_s(1+\log(2a))$ and is itself bounded. In either construction the initial short-core labels on every site and pin are retained. This gives a seed of width $O_s(h+1)$ on the hull. Its selected parents include $0,1$ in the two-site case, and $-1,0,2$ or $-a,0,1,b$ ($a\ge1,b\ge2$) in the multi-site case. For the latter, some uniform $\eta_s>0$ satisfies
$$
 \min(W,M-W)\ge\eta_s m_j,\qquad W=\sum_{i\le j} f_i m_i,\quad M=\sum_{i\le j}m_i ,
$$
where $f_i$ are the bounded site digits. This follows on the finite shortened hulls, including all choices of additional pins, and the digits are preserved in restoring lengths. For two sites orient $f_1=0,f_2=1$.

If all vertices outside $S$ are single children of its vertices, use the hull construction and insert them directly. At $0,1$ the [single child rule](two-hub-contraction.md#keeping-track-of-intervals) costs $O(1+|p|)$ for parent $p$. With three or four available parents the [integer band rule](marked-leaf-threshold.md#integer-intervals-and-three-available-parent-labels) has the same cost with a site-dependent constant. Indeed for an upper band, a jump from zero to $p$ can return by unit steps in one direction and by a fixed opposite jump followed by unit steps in the other. It stays in an interval of length $O_s(1+|p|)$; erase loops to get a simple return. The lower-band argument uses $p-1$ and shifted steps. All these single children have parents in the original hull, so even their combined cost is $O_s((h+1)^2)$.

Otherwise choose a deepest tip away from $S$. Its parent $z$ is outside $S$, and all $m\ge1$ of the children of $z$ are leaves. Reserve $z$ and those children for the last step; pin its parent $q$. Root the remaining tree at a selected site. Apply the [structural dichotomy](integer-repair.md#restoring-withheld-branches-over-the-integers) with the sites, pins and $q$ protected. In the bounded-nonleaf alternative the original core also has boundedly many nonleaves. Include all of them in the hull, which now has bounded order after including the bounded number of sites, pins and witness vertices. Insert the remaining terminal children at the bounded parent labels. This already gives the required seed.

In a repair alternative, withhold its jobs and use the linear-width hull construction **in the compressed tree**. Include the common supplier among the pins when it occurs. No vertex of a reserved interior is needed for this compressed hull. The prescribed twig and the short consecutive path survive. Orient the two-site seed temporarily by reflecting $x\mapsto1-x$ if necessary to make the label $p$ of $q$ nonpositive. Reflection exchanges the two sites and preserves the interval requirement. After orientation, if the common star supplier has nonzero label $d$, pad the lower end so that its new endpoint $g$ avoids $-d,-2d,-3d$. A two-site seed or quadruple can pad below by any count and a triple by every count at least two. This costs boundedly many extra leaves.

For all compressed-tree vertices outside the hull, use the integer forest lemma with their actual boundary labels and the entire interval as fixed occupied set. It has size and radius $O_s(h+1)$. The new discrepancies are guarded and all labels and targets have magnitude $O_s((h+1)^2)$. The initial hull difference is $-[g]$. The [integer restorations, including the pooled leaf walk](integer-repair.md), now give a sparse injection on the core without the last broom, keeping every seed filler and leaving exactly one omitted label $g'$. Its largest absolute label $D$ is $O_s((h+1)^3)$. The omission is occupied, by the histogram identity.

To restore the broom, take a fresh **upper** insertion block for its root $z$ at parent $p=f(q)$. At two sites $p\le0$ and the parents can be $-p$ copies of one followed by $p$ itself; the child occupies the last position. At three or four available parents the usual upper band works at either sign. The block uses $O_s(1)$ places since $q$ was pinned. Write $d$ for its length and $i$ for its child slot. Let $A$ be the full occupied set, $c=|A|=O_s(h+1)$, and $a=\min A$. Place this block at $x-i$, so its child has label $x$. It can occupy any fresh consecutive band: its integer edge weights permute that same band independently of its location. Give the $m$ broom tips the labels $g'-x,\ldots,g'-mx$. Search for an integer $x$ beginning at
$$
 X_0=\max\{i+1,\lfloor(g'-a)/m\rfloor+1\}.
$$
This makes the band positive and the final tip strictly below the old minimum. At most $dc$ choices make a band position equal an old label, at most $mc$ choices make a tip equal an old label, and at most $md$ choices make a tip equal a band position. Each equality has nonzero coefficient in $x$. The tips are distinct because $x>0$. Thus $x\le X_0+dc+mc+md$, in particular $mx=O_s(D+(h+1)^3)$. The tip weights are $g',g'-x,\ldots,g'-(m-1)x$. Since zero is occupied, the final tip is strictly below the positive band as well as every old label and every other tip. The omitted weight is now the **global minimum**, and all labels still have magnitude $O_s((h+1)^3)$. Fill every unoccupied integer up to the maximum by a leaf at zero. Each adds the same weight and label. This produces the two consecutive intervals. Undo the temporary reflection for two sites if it was used. The site digits and moment promise from the hull now hold on the entire seed. [Broom constructor](integer_broom.py) · [Lean proof of the weight list](IntegerForest.lean)

## Exact populations

Let $Q=(h+1)^3$. If $m_j\ge A_s(Q+\sum_{i>j}m_i)$, use the first $j$ sites in the construction above. The seed costs $O_s(Q)$ leaves and all their parent digits are bounded. Deducting them and bringing in the remaining smaller sites lowers either moment slack by at most $O_s(Q+\sum_{i>j}m_i)$. At two sites the original slacks are $m_2,m_1$. At three or more use the displayed $\eta_s m_j$ bound. A large enough $A_s$ leaves sufficient reserves at the anchors and sufficient slack for the [exact population completion](marked-leaf-threshold.md#realizing-the-exact-leaf-populations). Its upper and lower permutation blocks only need the **support of the requested populations**, at most $s$ bounded site digits; no unmarked vertex is requested as a parent. Thus all its reserve constants are uniform here. If the displayed condition fails at every $j\ge2$, descending induction gives $m_2<(1+A_s)^s Q$. This proves the integer theorem after absorbing the initial single leaves.

## Long subdivisions and arbitrary moduli

Deleting the leaves at the indicated sites gives a core with at most $l\le k^\alpha+O_s(1)$ leaves. Keep the sites themselves. It suffices to consider any core with this leaf bound and arbitrary specified populations. At ordinary orders $h\ge\delta k$ the [positive-density theorem](concentrated-leaves.md) applies for every fixed $\delta>0$: the ordinary leaves and the vertices of degree above three are each bounded by $l$, and the site count is fixed. For lower core orders first consider total order exactly $k$. On a supposed sequence of failures we have $h=o(k)$. The one-site square-root theorem settles the case where the leaves away from a largest site total at most $c\sqrt k$. Along a failure sequence therefore $m_2\ge c_s\sqrt k$, since all those leaves total at most $l+(s-1)m_2$. Putting one leaf per positive population into the core changes the counts by $O_s(1)$.

Split the core at the sites and all vertices of degree different from two. There are $O_s(l+1)$ segments. Shorten each longer than twenty to its congruent length modulo twelve in $[9,20]$. The short core has order $O_s(l+1)$. For $j\ge3$ and long-branch reconstruction reserve two additional real vertices on a longest segment for preparing the expansion edge: retain a length in $[9,20]$ congruent to the true length minus two there. For $j=2$ retain the usual congruent length and **pin all the retained vertices** of that segment in the seed proof. There are at most 21 extra pins. Apply the preceding seed proof to the short tree. The site digits are bounded, and the width $W_0$ and total fillers are $O_s((l+1)^3)$. A residual two-site caterpillar with a path of length at least nine cannot arise: the residual core has a hub path of length at most four with only single leaves outside it (with the off-path leaves restricted to the endpoints at distance four). The short cores retaining long segments also keep every positive site a nonleaf.

Here are the costs of restoring lengths. If no segment has enough surplus vertices for $C_s\log(2k)$ moves of two vertices each, the original order is $O_s((l+1)\log(2k))$. Simply use the seed proof on the full core, at total cost $O_s(((l+1)\log(2k))^3)$. Otherwise choose a longest segment to fund the moves. For two sites its protected path lies in the bounded-leaf hull. Each of its retained edges survives the compressed-hull construction because its endpoints are pins. In the initial bounded short hull the two-site construction crosses the cut $(-\infty,0],[1,\infty)$ except at most the six edges of its small exceptional seed. Child insertions and crossing-edge subdivisions have the stated cut property outside that seed. At most two edges of our path touch zero. Hence at least one of its nine or more edges has strictly opposite signs, also after reflection if needed. This is the [protected-path argument](polynomial-two-hubs.md#preserving-a-long-branch-and-removing-the-restriction-on-tree-order). At three or more sites, on one retained edge $d,e$ ($d\ne0$), use the two reserved real vertices to insert a sufficiently low child $q$ at $d$ and an upper child $p=d+e-q$ at $e$, giving $q<0<p$ and $d+q\ne0$. Their positions, padding, and number of fillers are $O_s(W_0+1)$ by the linear-length band rule above. Reconnect $q,p$ to restore the old edge weight.

Starting on the opposite-sign edge, apply the [asymmetric buffer recurrence](bounded-marked-sites.md#a-wide-interval-with-few-leaves-away-from-zero), or its [two-site version](two-hub-all-orders.md#creating-an-interval-with-few-leaves-at-the-second-site). The fixed negative endpoint has magnitude $O_s(W_0+1)$, so its upper block has length $b=O_s(W_0+1)$. The initial lower padding to make the first move feasible costs $O_s(W_0+1)$. At each move $x=L-p$, $y=p+q-x$ and the positive endpoint becomes $y$ with $q$ fixed. After the initial padding both endpoint magnitudes grow at least by a factor two every bounded number of moves. Their ratio is bounded up to an additive $O_s(W_0+1)$, and each move increases the larger magnitude by at most a factor three plus $O_s(W_0+1)$. Each uses at most $b-1$ fillers away from zero. Stop at a multiple of six moves with both radii at least $16D+4W_1$, where $D=100(h+I+1)$, $I$ counts the current fillers away from zero, and $W_1=O_s(W_0+1)$ bounds the start after preparation. Since $h\le k$ and $W_0=O_s((l+1)^3)\le O_s(k)$, $O_s(\log(2k))$ moves suffice even though $I$ grows by $O_s(W_0+1)$ each time. The first such stopping width is $O_s(h+(W_0+1)\log(2k))$ by the upper growth bound over the last six moves.

All remaining increments are multiples of twelve. Each segment still has an unused short-core edge with nonzero endpoints and sum: among nine edges at most two touch zero, at most one has sum zero and at most one was used for the buffer. On each such edge and its successive inner edges use [neutral twelve-vertex subdivisions](long-branches.md#a-short-core-with-arbitrarily-elongated-edges), parameters $u,4u$ with $1\le u\le D$. Avoid at most $h+I$ real vertices and nonzero-parent fillers, then replace the zero-parent leaves at the twelve chosen positions by the actual internal vertices. The parameter rows stay within the stopping radii; each first pair of endpoints is bounded by $W_1$. All site digits survive. The total cost is $O_s(h+(W_0+1)\log(2k))$, and the cost **away from zero** is $O_s((W_0+1)\log(2k))$.

## Choosing the large populations

Set $Q_k=((l+1)\log(2k))^3=o(\sqrt k)$. Pass to a subsequence on which the number of positive sites is fixed and the ratios below have limits in the extended nonnegative reals. If $m_2/k$ has positive limit, use exactly the initial sites whose populations have positive limiting proportion of $k$. Every selected population is $\Omega(k)$ and the others sum to $o(k)$. The total seed and length restoration cost is $o(k)$ in either branch-length case. Both original moment slacks are $\Omega(k)$ and all site digits are bounded, so the exact completion applies.

Otherwise $m_2=o(k)$ and $m_1=k-o(k)$. Choose the last $j$ for which $m_j/Q_k\to\infty$. There are at least two because $m_2\ge c_s\sqrt k$. Later populations sum to $O_s(Q_k)$ on the chosen sequence, allowing a sequence-dependent constant. When using three or more sites, the largest site digit is zero or one since $0\le W\le M$, $m_1=k-o(k)$ and all other contributions are $o(k)$. Orient the completed short seed with this digit at zero **before** the asymmetric restoration. Reflection swaps the two slacks and takes a quadruple to a quadruple. A reflected triple supplies $-1,1,2$ and now also zero at the largest site, giving an available quadruple. At two sites use the original ordering. With no long branch the cost on every parent is $O_s(Q_k)$. With a long branch it is $O_s(Q_k)$ away from zero and $O_s(h+Q_k)=o(k)$ at zero. Consequently the remaining moment is at least $\eta_s m_j-O(Q_k)$ and tends to infinity, and the remaining mass minus moment is $k-o(k)$. Every needed anchor reserve remains available. This finishes the exact populations and contradicts the supposed order-$k$ counterexamples.

For $h<k$ and total order below $k$, pad with temporary leaves at a site and restrict the order-$k$ labeling. Above order $k$, remove one added leaf at orders congruent to one, otherwise remove $(n-1)\bmod k$ added leaves, where $n$ is total order. There are enough since $h<k$. Apply induction with the same core, then restore the deficient weight or use the [distinct-leaf extension](../counterexample-bound.md#removing-leaves), respectively. The positive-density theorem handles $h\ge k$. This proves the all-orders assertion. The same estimates apply along sequences with $l=o(k^{1/6}/\log(2k))$.

## Verification and scope

An end-to-end checker composes the forest, multiple repairs, final broom and exact leaf counts on 18 marked trees. It reconstructs the subdivided edges and checks the complete integer intervals by their range endpoints, through width 198,374,672,255. It recounts 29,270 ordinary edges and all requested populations. [End-to-end verifier](check_polynomial_marked.py)

The forest checker measures the discrepancy before repair and the pooled-window checker recomputes sums after repair. The broom audit works at both two-site orientations after choosing its nonpositive parent, and at the signed triple and quadruple. The previous subdivision checkers exercise the marked-site buffers, actual segment lengths and exact remaining populations. The uniform bounds and the all-orders reduction use the arguments above. The full conjecture for unrestricted sites and moduli remains open.
