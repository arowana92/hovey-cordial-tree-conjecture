# A quadratic modulus bound for every rooted tree

**Theorem.** There is an absolute constant $C$ with the following property. For any tree $H$ of order $h$, any prescribed vertex $o$, and any integer $k>C(h+1)^2$, there is an injection $f:V(H)\to\mathbb Z_k$ taking $o$ to zero whose actual edge sums use every label of the injection once except for one omitted label. There is no degree or diameter bound.

**Consequences.** The injection extends cordially to any number of additional leaves at $o$ by the [periodic complement construction](README.md#uniform-witnesses-for-large-moduli). An absolute $C'$ suffices for adding **$C'(h+1)^2$ or more leaves at a prescribed vertex to make any tree cordial at every modulus**. Use the injection for large $k$ and the [linear hub-leaf bound](../large-order/gap-path.md) when $k\le C(h+1)^2$. Different moduli may use different labelings.

There is also an absolute $c>0$ such that, at every sufficiently large $k$, **every tree with all but at most $c\sqrt{k}$ of its leaves adjacent to one vertex is $k$-cordial**, at every tree order. In particular, the number of leaves away from *any* proposed hub in a counterexample must exceed this bound. This strengthens the [degree-independent bound with exponent $1/15$](modular-injection.md) and removes the [long bare segment hypothesis at the square-root scale](seven-errors.md).

## Preparing at most seven discrepancies

We prove existence of an absolute $C$ by ruling out a sequence of failures with $k/(h+1)^2\to\infty$. If $h$ is bounded, the [integer certificate for any fixed rooted tree](universal-hub.md) already works. We may take $h\to\infty$. All trees in the construction are rooted at $o$.

Use the [seven-error preparation](seven-errors.md) on a core obtained by withholding the repair sites specified below. Its order is at most $h$. It gives $E-V=-[0]+\sum([t]-[b])$. All deficits $b$ are occupied. The surplus labels $t$ and their negatives are pairwise distinct and reserved. We can additionally require
$$
 2p,3p\ne0\quad(p\ne0),\qquad t\ne2p,\qquad b\notin\{-p,-2p,-3p\}
 \tag{1}
$$
for every ordinary vertex label $p$ and every prepared pair. To see this, forbid the nonzero elements killed by 2 or 3 throughout the preparation. The initial child labels $1,\ldots,\deg(o)$ satisfy this for large $k$. No discrepancy occurs before the last seven levels. In a level with a nonzero total jump, the new deficit is $X$, the surplus is $X+P$, and every new vertex has the form $X+a$. Against these new vertices the extra tests in (1) have coefficients $-1,2,3,4$ in $X$. Against prior vertices each has a nonzero coefficient as well. Once such a pair is placed, forbid all solutions of $2y=t$ and $jy=-b$, $1\le j\le3$, for future arrivals. These add at most eight points per pair. Zero-total levels use disjoint translated cycles as before. All placements still exclude only $O(h^2)$ choices. [Guarded implementation](seven_errors.py)

Each exact repair deals with a prepared pair $(b,t)$ and the current omitted weight $g$, starting with $g=0$. It contributes $[b]-[t]+[g]-[g']$. We arrange eight repair groups of the same type before preparation. Complete unused groups with $b=t$ fresh, satisfying the same finite exclusions at their chosen parents. The protected old values always number $O(h)$. In this problem there is no dense interval of preassigned hub leaves.

## Choosing parameters over composite moduli

We give the estimate needed when individual branch arities can grow with $h$. Each group uses a bounded number of independent uniform parameters in $\mathbb Z_k$. Before its final leaf walk, the group assigns only boundedly many new spine vertices, star roots and distinguished leaves. Their affine coefficient vectors are bounded by absolute constants. Some placements, such as the ordinary bridges preceding an opposite pair of star roots, can be made successively and then regarded as fixed. The remaining at most $h$ leaves have assigned parents. A backward walk labels each leaf with the preceding weight minus its parent's label, so its edge supplies that preceding weight.

The constructions use two independent coordinates $U,V$ on two groups of leaf parents. Every jump in the first group has a bounded nonzero coefficient in $U$ and none in $V$, and the second group has the reverse property. All other jumps are independent of both. A group can have arbitrarily many leaves, but its coefficients are repeated in boundedly many constant runs, since only boundedly many parent vertices are used. Unless stated otherwise, order each list with the sign of its total first, within each sign grouping by parent. A specified first jump of that sign may be taken first. Every nonempty prefix then has the sign of the total. By reversing the sign of a coordinate in the counting argument, treat these prefix coefficients as positive. Their magnitudes are $O(h)$.

Start with one jump from each group, then process the leaves independent of $U,V$. Finish a prefix of the first group, then finish the second group, then finish the first. We show how to choose the stopping point of the prefix. For a tail with coefficients $(u,v)$ the probability of hitting a *fixed* label is at most $\gcd(u,v,k)/k$, conditioning on the other parameters. Indeed the image of $(U,V)\mapsto uU+vV$ is the subgroup of multiples of that gcd. We need the sum of these gcds over the tails to be $O(h)$ when $k\le h^4$.

Let $B$ be an absolute bound on individual jump coefficients and the number of their constant runs. Stop at the latest prefix of the first list with $\gcd(u,k)\le B$. Its first jump qualifies. For $L=\lceil h^{1/4}\rceil$, at most $O_B(L)$ jumps follow this stopping point. If there is no run of length $L$, this already holds. Otherwise, in the last $L$ terms of the last run of length at least $L$, the prefix coefficients are an arithmetic progression of nonzero step $d$, $|d|\le B$. Divide its start and step by their gcd, at most $B$. In this primitive progression a prime dividing the step divides no entry. For primes dividing $k$ but not the new step there is one forbidden residue class per prime. Inclusion-exclusion leaves at least
$$
 L\,2^{-\omega(k)}-2^{\omega(k)}
$$
indices coprime to $k$, where $\omega(k)$ counts distinct prime factors. For each squarefree divisor the count differs from its length divided by that divisor by at most one. The displayed expression is positive for all sufficiently large $h$ because $4^{\omega(k)}=k^{o(1)}$ and $k\le h^4$. For the elementary divisor estimate, a number with $r$ distinct prime factors is at least $r!$. Thus some prefix in that final portion has the required gcd, and all subsequent short runs together have length $O_B(L)$.

Up to the first group's stopping point the second coefficient, once present, stays at its bounded first jump. While finishing the second group the first coordinate has gcd at most $B$ with $k$. The initial single-coordinate tail also has bounded cost. The remaining $O_B(L)$ tails have second coefficient equal to a fixed nonzero integer $S$ of magnitude $O_B(h)$. Their first coefficients lie in an interval of width $W=O_B(L)$, and each value occurs at most twice, by strict increase followed by strict decrease. At most one distinct value $u$ in this interval can have $\gcd(u,S)^2>|S|W$. Indeed, for two distinct values the common gcd of their two divisors of $S$ divides their difference, while the lcm of the divisors divides $S$. Their product is at most $W|S|$. The one exceptional value costs at most $2|S|$. All others together cost $O_B(L\sqrt{hL})=O_B(h)$. This proves the claimed sum, including an interval consisting of one value. [Stopping rule and arithmetic checks](check_mixed_sparse.py)

There are $O(h)$ previously fixed labels. By a union bound the probability that any tail hits any of them is $O(h^2/k)$. There are only $O(1)$ other new assigned affine labels to compare with the tails. As checked below, the slope vectors differ, except for explicitly separated fixed intercepts. Each nonconstant comparison has a nonzero coefficient of magnitude $O(h)$ and hence probability $O(h/k)$, for a total of $O(h^2/k)$ here as well. Constraints on the other new labels cost at most $O(h^2/k)$. If $k>h^4$ the crude bound $O(h^3/k)$ from all tail comparisons suffices directly. These bounds tend to zero on the proposed failure sequence.

Repetitions *among* the walk's tails require no probability estimate. Erase simple cycles after specialization and translate them one at a time to avoid the residual tails and occupied labels. Each translation excludes at most $O(h^2)$ residues. Translated cycles keep precisely the same edge-minus-vertex contribution. The residual walk ends at the prescribed old omission or intermediate mark and leaves the new gap as required. [Walk identity and cycle erasure](pendant-stars.md#one-repair-with-a-moving-omitted-weight)

## Short branches with unequal populations

We adapt three local identities. The distinction from their earlier use is that all arities can now vary independently and can be unbounded.

1. **Many pendant stars at one vertex.** At nonzero boundary $p$ use the [five-root table](pendant-stars.md#one-repair-with-a-moving-omitted-weight). For each of the two groups controlling the final walk, put the three largest leaf populations at its three positive-coefficient roots. All five populations are positive. The signed number of pending jumps is the sum of those three minus the sum of the other two. The two prefilled positive and two prefilled negative tips cancel in this count. Hence the signed total is positive. The first jump is at the open root with coefficient one. At a zero boundary a single star per group suffices, with all jumps of coefficient one. The three primary groups and their leaf walk from $t$ to $b$ use only one open leaf each and need no equality of arities. The repeated-slope tests require $2p,3p\ne0$, $t\notin\{0,p,-p,2p\}$, $b,g\notin\{-p,-2p,-3p\}$ when $p\ne0$. The preparation gives these for $b,t$ and the initial $g=0$. Avoid the same three fixed values for each successive $g'$ by the tail estimate. Thus 25 stars per repair at nonzero $p$, or five at zero, suffice.

2. **Two pendant stars at many distinct vertices.** Use the [four-boundary table and auxiliary-root walk](paired-stars.md#completing-one-repair-and-all-unused-stars). In each repair select six quadruples of boundary labels. Make *every* ordering within each quadruple admissible by requiring sums of nonempty subsets of at most three members to avoid $\{0,t,-b,-g\}$, and singletons to avoid $-t$. Greedy selection costs only boundedly many forbidden residues per pick, as each new label enters each sum once and all boundary labels are distinct. Retain a fixed extra pool of that size in addition to the 24 suppliers per job. Include its two stars per unused supplier among the auxiliary roots of the last job, as in the original walk. The pool size is independent of arities. For the control groups write $a,q,c,h_0$ for the four roles in the table. Use the larger star at $a,q,c$ for the three positive roots, and the smaller star at $h_0,q$ for the two negative roots. Order the suppliers so that the chosen arity at $c$ is at least the smaller arity at $h_0$. The positive total then exceeds the negative total, including the cancellations for prefilled tips. The first jump is at the open coefficient-one root. The other groups and auxiliary offsets use exactly the original affine identities and equal-slope tests. The two control coordinates occur only in jumps at their respective five selected roots.

3. **Branches with two successive supports.** A branch consists of $x$ below its boundary, $y$ below $x$, $l\ge0$ direct leaf children of $x$, and $m\ge1$ leaf children of $y$. Use the [three-boundary and zero-boundary tables](depth-three-repair.md#a-free-leaf-parent-on-a-three-edge-path). At three nonzero boundaries use profiles $(l_a,m_a),(l_b,m_b),(l_c,m_c)$. The total coefficient of pending jumps in a control group is
$$
 l_a+l_b-2l_c+m_a+2(m_b-1)-2(m_c-1).
$$
Its mean over the six permutations is $(m_a+m_b+m_c)/3>0$. Choose a positive permutation. The table's positive first jump is at $d$. At a common zero boundary the two-branch table uses $x=Jd$, now with $J\in\{4,-4\}$. Its five assigned slopes are distinct, nonzero and different from $-1$. Averaged over the two orders of the branches, its signed total is $\bar m+(J+1)(\bar m-1-\bar l)$, where bars are the two-profile means. Choose $J=4$ if the parenthesis is nonnegative and $J=-4$ otherwise. Some order gives positive total with a bounded multiplier. The primary groups may use either multiplier. [Lean proof for arbitrary populations](MixedGuards.lean)

For the third case a large collection of disjoint branches gives either sufficiently many at one boundary or sufficiently many distinct boundaries. In the common case save fifteen branches per repair at a nonzero boundary or ten at zero. At common $p\ne0$ the tests are $2p\ne0$ and $t\ne0,-p,-2p$, all ensured by (1) and reservation. In the distinct case exclude the root boundary and save fifteen branches per job. After preparation allocate the two control triples of **all** jobs first, requiring all pairwise sums in each triple to be nonzero. Each pick excludes at most two negatives. Permutation for a positive signed total is now free. Next allocate every job's first primary triple, choosing $a\ne-t$ and $b_1\ne-a,-a-t$ in its boundary order. The six remaining branches per job supply its last two primary triples. Within any triple of distinct nonzero residues one can put two with nonzero sum first. No further exclusions depend on the omission. The three tables' collision checks thus apply to every job including the last. The branch parents lie outside all saved branches.

In all three cases keep the first jump of each control group, then complete the other groups' leaf lists, and use the prefix scheduling above to finish the two control groups. The first tail is separated by the original table tests. Subsequent tails contain both control coordinates and no other newly assigned label does. The primary path contributes $[b]-[t]$ and the backwards leaf walk contributes $[g]-[g']$. The [mixed-profile constructor](mixed_sparse.py) implements the third case, including allocation of the triples, congruence protection and the stopping rule.

## Paths left after pruning

Let $J$ be the rooted subtree of vertices of descendant height at least two. Each tip of $J$ gives either two pendant stars at its own vertex, or one branch of the third type attached at its parent. Tip subtrees are disjoint and those parents have height at least three. Discard the root itself if needed. Thus, on a failure sequence, the tip count is bounded by an absolute constant by the preceding alternatives. So are the maximum degree and the number of junctions of $J$. Every child outside $J$ is a leaf or the root of a pendant star. The first alternative bounds their number of star roots per vertex, and the second bounds the number of vertices with at least two such stars. All these bounds are absolute because the number of jobs is fixed at eight. No high-degree site needs to be discarded.

If the order of $J$ is bounded on a subsequence, the whole tree has boundedly many nonleaf vertices there. Pad to order $k$ by leaves at $o$. The [bounded-nonleaf finiteness theorem](universal-hub.md#cordiality-and-finite-exceptions) labels this padded tree for all sufficiently large $k$, even if $o$ becomes one additional nonleaf. Translate the root label to zero, preserving injectivity and distinct edge sums. Each removed padding leaf takes its own label from both multisets. The original tree therefore already has the required injection.

Otherwise delete the boundedly many junctions, tips, the root, and positions with multiple stars from $J$. Boundedly many paths remain. If unboundedly many positions have one star, select twelve sufficiently separated 43-position windows centered at such stars per job. Use the [opposite boundary repair](exceptional-degrees.md#windows-centered-at-stars). The opposite selected star roots give exclusively positive jumps in their two control groups, regardless of arity. There are at most 43 auxiliary roots per window and boundedly many spine labels. Perform its sequential spine placements first; each fixed-length bridge costs $O(h)$ exclusions. Its remaining steps satisfy the estimate above, including the auxiliary-root translation and all direct leaves.

If only boundedly many positions bear stars, remove them. An arbitrarily long remaining path has direct leaves of arbitrary counts. Use the [forty-position lemma and the two leaf-only repairs](exceptional-degrees.md#leaf-counts-that-vary-along-the-path). Nonconstant blocks supply two active control bridges and two windows with movable omissions. The coefficients per jump are bounded by 126; sort by sign, with at most 21 parent runs per control bridge. The two-coordinate estimate applies, working backwards from the independent mark $G$. Its first tail also uses the first bridge parameter, a combination no assigned label has.

A constant block with $m>0$ uses the zero-total control bridges of the [87-position repair](exceptional-degrees.md#constant-leaf-counts). Here take one jump from each, then every leaf outside those bridges, then all but the last remaining jump from the first bridge, then all but the last from the second, then the two last jumps. Before the two final jumps, every tail after the first contains both control coordinates. One is always bounded and nonzero. In particular, during the second bridge's run the first control coefficient is the negative of the first bridge's held-out coefficient, because the first bridge has total zero. Thus these comparisons against fixed labels cost $O(h^2/k)$ directly. The last two tails are the new omission $g'=(m+1)G-m(b-t+g)$ and its sum with the last held-out jump from the second bridge, in reverse chronological order. They differ in slope from every assigned label as in the constant-block proof. For these two tails alone the $O(h/k)$ bound against each of $O(h)$ fixed values suffices. Zero leaf count needs only the bare placement. The integer matrix lemma applies unchanged to arbitrary integer counts, and nonzero control totals have magnitude $O(h)<k$.

Compress precisely the chosen windows or remove precisely the chosen short branches before the seven-error preparation. Each admissible placement restores its actual edge sums, including the contribution of any removed virtual edge. All eight jobs together leave $E=V-[g']$ with the prescribed root still at zero. This rules out the failure sequence and proves the quadratic theorem.

## The square-root leaf bound

Delete all leaves adjacent to the proposed hub $o$ and retain $o$. The remaining rooted tree $H$ has at most $a$ nonroot terminals if at most $a$ original leaves lay away from $o$. The singleton is immediate. If $h\ge k/20$ and $a\le c\sqrt k$, the [positive-density theorem](concentrated-leaves.md) applies directly to $H$ with the single decoration site $o$. It has at most $a+1$ unrooted leaves and hence also at most $a+1$ vertices of degree greater than three.

If $h<k/20$, shorten each segment between the root, branch vertices and terminals as in the [twelve-vertex subdivision argument](long-branches.md#a-short-core-with-arbitrarily-elongated-edges). The shortened tree has order at most $28a+1$. Choose the absolute $c$ sufficiently small that $C(28a+2)^2<k$ for $a\le c\sqrt k$ and large $k$. Label the shortened tree by the new theorem. Every segment to be expanded has at least three edges; at most one touches the root and at most one has weight zero. On an eligible edge restore multiples of twelve internal vertices, avoiding all occupied labels. The required $k>12h+132$ holds for sufficiently large $k$. This preserves the incidence-sum histogram and the omission. The periodic extension then restores any hub population, proving the stated all-orders conclusion.
