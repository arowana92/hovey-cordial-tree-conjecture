# Quadratic super edge-magic extension and square-root leaf concentration

**Integer theorem.** Fix $s\ge1$. Let $H$ have order $h$ and $l$ leaves. Add any prescribed numbers of leaves at at most $s$ chosen vertices. If the **largest** population is at least $A_s(h+(l+1)^2)$, the result has a super edge-magic labeling. One labeling works at every positive modulus, irrespective of all smaller populations. In particular, for one prescribed root in any $h$-vertex tree a quadratic number of additional root leaves suffices for simultaneous cordiality at every modulus. The original rooted tree itself has distinct ordinary integer labels of magnitude $O((h+1)^2)$ with root zero and edge sums equal to its vertex labels except for one **extreme**.

**Modulus theorem.** For each fixed $s$ there is $c_s>0$ such that every tree with all but at most $c_s\sqrt k$ leaves adjacent to at most $s$ vertices is $k$-cordial at all sufficiently large $k$, at every tree order. This extends the [one-site square-root theorem](quadratic-root.md) and removes the [long bare segment hypothesis for several sites](cube-root-leaves.md).

## Short repair groups with few terminal children

First work on an ordinary tree of order $b$ with $n$ nonleaf vertices. Root at a selected site. We may use a bounded number of pins, in particular the site and witness pins from the [marked hull construction](polynomial-marked.md#a-seed-of-width-at-most-a-constant-times-the-third-power). We show how to obtain an extreme seed of width $O_s((b+1)^2)$, with at most $O_s(b+1)$ filler leaves away from zero and the usual bounded site digits and moment slacks. For one site, outside the bounded-nonleaf alternative the sole initial vertex can be the root itself.

We need a quantitative version of the [structural dichotomy](integer-repair.md#restoring-withheld-branches-over-the-integers). Prescribe any fixed numbers of the ordinary and additional control groups used in the [extreme repair](cubic-endpoint.md), allowing one extra guarded discrepancy. With $t$ vertices forbidden as **interiors** of the saved groups, either one of the alternatives can be selected disjointly, or the number of nonleaves is at most $C(t+1)$. The constant depends on the required fixed numbers and the number of site pins.

Here is a count proving the linear dependence. Call an interior clean if it contains no forbidden vertex. At any one supplier there are only boundedly many clean child stars if the common-star alternative fails. Only boundedly many suppliers can have two clean child stars if the distributed alternative fails. For the branches with two successive supports, among any disjoint collection only boundedly many can be clean: too many force enough at one boundary or enough at distinct boundaries. In the rooted subtree $J$ of height at least two, each tip except possibly the root supplies two stars at that tip or a branch of the last type at its parent. Tip subtrees are disjoint, and at most $t$ meet a forbidden vertex (in addition to the bounded permanent pins). Hence $J$ has $O(t+1)$ tips. The number of junctions and the sum of the degrees in excess of two are also $O(t+1)$. All the child stars outside $J$ are disjoint. There are at most $t+O_s(1)$ of them that contain a forbidden vertex.

Split $J$ at its root, junctions, tips, forbidden positions, positions having a forbidden off-spine descendant outside $J$, and positions with multiple stars. In this last list multiple **clean** stars occur at only boundedly many suppliers, and a dirty star accounts for at most one more position. These cuts leave $O(t+1)$ paths: the total excess degree bounds the extra components from cutting at junctions. A clean path position can have arbitrary direct leaves and at most one star. The number of starred positions within a fixed distance of path ends is $O(t+1)$. Away from ends, greedy separation selects disjoint starred windows unless the number of further starred positions is bounded. Split there too. On the remaining $O(t+1)$ paths of leaf-only positions, cut disjoint separated 87-position blocks. An unbounded number of blocks of any one of the three types (active, constant positive, all zero) supplies its fixed number of required jobs. If all fail, only $O(t+1)$ total positions remain, counting the bounded remainders per path. This gives $|J|=O(t+1)$. Its child stars number $O(|J|+t+1)$ by the clean-star bound per supplier. All other nonleaves have now been counted.

Let $C$ cover these counts. If $n$ is sufficiently large, forbid the $t=\lfloor n/(2C)\rfloor$ vertices with the most original terminal children, besides the permanent pins. The bounded-nonleaf outcome is then impossible. **Do not add these $t$ vertices to the marked hull.** They are used solely to avoid group interiors. Every parent internal to the chosen groups has at most $O_s((b+1)/(n+1))$ original terminal children, because there are at most $b$ terminals in the tree. Since all the saved groups together have only boundedly many spine vertices, star roots and distinguished children, their total number of pending leaf jumps is
$$
 q=O_s((b+1)/(n+1)).
$$
The number of groups will be an absolute constant from the weighted forest lemma below, increased by the fixed number of extreme controls. This choice is made **before** fixing $C$; neither the forest lemma nor its absolute depth depends on the structural threshold.

## Delay the original terminal leaves

Remove the selected branches or compress the selected windows. Set aside every surviving original terminal leaf that is not a permanent pin or the root. Keep every surviving original nonleaf, even one that has just become a terminal. All parents of the set-aside leaves therefore survive except in the direct two-vertex trivial case. Their vertices never appear as deferred leaves of a saved group. The compressed core now has $O_s(n+1)$ vertices. At several sites take the usual linear-width seed on the hull of the permanent pins, orient before preparation, and pad the omitted endpoint at a common nonzero star supplier as in the [marked seed proof](polynomial-marked.md). When the supplier has to be pinned, only its single vertex enters the hull. The seed has size and label magnitude $O_s(n+1)$. Its marked digits are bounded. The two-site residual caterpillar is handled as before. At one site use the root at zero, so the initial gap condition holds without pinning a supplier in the seed.

Attach to every remaining vertex the mass counting its set-aside leaf children. Apply the [simultaneous weighted forest lemma](weighted-forest.md) to the non-hull forest with its actual parent labels. Thus the core labels and targets have magnitude $O_s((n+1)^2)$ and only an absolute constant number of pairs need correction. Adding the hull parents' contribution (each of magnitude $O_s(n+1)$) gives
$$
 \left|\sum_{v\text{ set aside}}f(\operatorname{parent}(v))\right|
      = O_s((b+1)(n+1)).
$$
Order these later leaf jumps by the signed-walk rule, treating their whole list as one uninterrupted piece when inserted in a backward walk. Every partial sum has that same bound, since each individual parent has magnitude $O_s((n+1)^2)$. The individual set-aside leaves can be arbitrarily numerous.

Use an integer parameter box of side $H_0=C_s(b+1)(n+1)$ for the primary, bridge and ordinary control placements that precede the final translations. Until the final leaf walk there are only $O_s(n+1)$ occupied or reserved values, including the hull fillers and the bounded number of selected roots and distinguished leaves. All $O_s(b+1)$ raw walk tails compared with these fixed or assigned values therefore give $O_s((b+1)(n+1))$ equality tests. Tail-tail coincidences can be erased. The other comparisons cost at most this many as well. Each nonconstant comparison excludes probability at most the reciprocal of the side length by conditioning on a nonzero coefficient coordinate. A sufficiently large $C_s$ suffices. Earlier fixed-length placements can use this box too.

For the short-branch and starred alternatives **defer the ordinary leaf walk of every job to the last job**. In the common-star and depth-three tables its neutral roots, prefilled children and distinguished primary tips give $[b_i]-[t_i]$. In the distributed-pair and starred tables include each job's auxiliary-root walk in this same primary part, restoring its virtual edges as appropriate. These formulas involve its boundaries and pair, but no previous omission. Choose any boundary order requiring a gap test using the initial $g$ throughout, and impose the common-supplier test before preparation. Only the last job actually starts a leaf walk. In every earlier job the independent roots belonging to its two usual guard lists can be placed as part of its bare table; the leaves on those roots are deferred. Keep the last job's two guard coordinates independent and unused elsewhere. From $g$, take its usual first guarded jumps. Insert the entire ordered list of set-aside original leaves and the pending leaves of the earlier jobs while both guards are active. Complete its guards with the original sign order. Its own extra extreme controls come last. Hence all new tails after the first two have both ordinary guard coordinates, which no assigned label does. The first-tail and primary equal-slope tests are unchanged. For the active and positive-uniform spine alternatives use the [pooled spines](integer-repair.md#a-common-final-walk-for-leaf-only-windows) already proved, inserting the ordered set-aside leaves alongside the earlier jobs' independent leaves. In the uniform first moment their total is part of the fixed older sum.

All new parents in these operations have magnitude $O_s(H_0)$ before the large final parameter centers. They involve only bounded-length affine formulas in old boundary labels, pairs and parameters, including boundedly many auxiliary-root jumps per group. The common-star, depth-three, distributed-pair and starred formulas are recorded in the [integer repair proof](integer-repair.md#restoring-withheld-branches-over-the-integers). In the pooled leaf-only cases each preceding bare gap mark is itself a chosen parameter. For any portion of a leaf walk the ordinary additive error, after separating the leading extreme coefficients, is now at most
$$
 E=C'_s\big((q+1)H_0+(b+1)(n+1)\big)=O_s((b+1)^2).
$$
Indeed at most $q$ saved-job jumps can each cost $O_s(H_0)$, and any portion of the original terminal list is bounded by twice its signed partial-sum bound. Old assigned magnitudes before the extreme placements also fit in $E$.

In the nonzero-control alternatives let $S>2B$ be the signed total over the additional groups, where $B$ bounds individual coefficients as in the [extreme proof](cubic-endpoint.md#forcing-a-minimum-in-the-integer-repairs). Here $S=O(q)$, since only leaves at the extra groups have these coordinates. Use a scale $N$ of order $C_s(H_0+E/S)$, with a sufficiently large multiplier. Individual leading jumps retain their sign, $(S-B)N>4E$, and $SN=O_s((b+1)^2)$. The signed balancing and final positive jumps put the omission strictly below every other label. For a positive uniform last count $m\le q$ use a multiple of 630 of order $C_s(H_0+E/m)$, the [same bridge coefficients and endpoint order](cubic-endpoint.md#a-block-with-constant-leaf-counts). Now $(m+1)N=O_s((b+1)^2)$. The final two held-out jumps can be made positive with parameter centers of order $O_s(H_0)$. The final omission is again a strict minimum and all required raw tails can avoid preassigned labels. In either case translate erased cycles upward by at most $O_s((b+1)^2)$, using the full number of actual tails for this last count. This preserves the minimum and the quadratic width.

**All-zero uniform blocks.** These have no positive guard jumps for the original terminal list. Instead label the set-aside original leaves in **one further level** before restoring any of these spines. Use the [signed walk and common translation](integer-forest.md#the-integer-construction) on their known jumps. Its total is $O_s((b+1)(n+1))$. The simple offsets, including translations of cycles, are $O_s((b+1)^2)$. The ordinary translation and all guards exclude $O_s((b+1)(n+1))$ integers because the previously occupied and reserved set has size $O_s(n+1)$; within this level the offsets are already distinct. At most one extra guarded pair is created. The resulting full sparse occupied set has size $O_s(b+1)$ and magnitude $O_s((b+1)^2)$. Every saved window is now bare. Its fixed-length affine placements, using boxes of side $O_s((b+1)^2)$, maintain that bound. In the last block use the negative extreme mark with scale $C_s(b+1)^2$ and the two inclined bridges as in the zero-count proof. There are no pending leaves. This also gives a quadratic extreme.

## The bounded-nonleaf case and exact completion

When $n$ is below the fixed threshold, several marked sites use the bounded-support hull and its single child insertions from the [asymmetric seed proof](sparse-marked-endpoint.md#a-marked-interval-with-limited-cost-away-from-zero). This costs $O_s(b+1)$ fillers. At a single site remove its own terminal children first. Its degree in the retained tree is now at most $n+1$. In the [support-first endpoint construction](cubic-endpoint.md#bounded-nonleaf-count), the initial running magnitudes are consequently bounded solely in terms of $n$. The bounded number of support steps gives all old support parents bounded labels. The subsequent terminal steps and the final distinguished path or fork sites have magnitude $O_n(b+1)$, and the final leaf and pair steps give span $O_n((b+1)^2)$. In the short-branch alternative the same bound follows by performing all available support steps before terminal ones. Restore the removed root children beyond the opposite end from the omitted extreme. Their labels equal their edge weights, they cost only linear additional span, and the prescribed root stays zero. A star itself works immediately.

This proves the claimed quadratic extreme labeling at one prescribed vertex. For several sites fill all remaining interval holes at zero. All fillers away from zero were already in the linear-width hull; thus they number $O_s(b+1)$. The original site digits and both moment slacks from that hull survive. The seed is now ready for exact populations.

If the ordinary tree has a sufficiently long site-free segment, the [long-segment theorem](cube-root-leaves.md#one-bare-segment-supplies-all-the-corrections) already gives the asserted total threshold $A_s(h+(l+1)^2)$. Otherwise $h=O_s(l+1)$. Apply the [largest-population accounting lemma](cube-root-leaves.md#paying-for-an-interval-with-one-large-population) with $T=(h+1)^2$ and $B=h+1$. Include one actual leaf from each positive site at the outset so the residual two-site caterpillars stay caterpillars. In the small-second-population alternative at most $O_s(h+1)$ further leaves are absorbed into the core, and the one-site quadratic theorem just proved applies there. This proves the integer theorem for every distribution.

Finally remove the site leaves. At most $s$ new ordinary leaves arise. The [positive-density theorem](concentrated-leaves.md) covers every order when $h\ge\delta k$. For $h<\delta k$ at total order $k$, a largest site has at least $(k-h)/s$ leaves. Choose $\delta,c_s>0$ sufficiently small that this pays $A_s(h+(l+1)^2)$ whenever the number of original exceptional leaves was at most $c_s\sqrt k$. Pad and restrict below order $k$. Above it, inductively remove added leaves and restore one missing weight or apply Hall's distinct-leaf extension, keeping the same ordinary tree [as in the all-orders proof](cube-root-leaves.md#from-integer-intervals-to-all-orders-at-a-given-modulus). This proves the stated concentration bound.

## Verification and limitations

The weighted-forest verifier recomputes all entering weights and checks rational moment identities independently. Its final six trees exercise delayed ordinary leaves, multiple jobs, active bridges, positive uniform and all-zero uniform blocks; they have 27,963 actual edges, 154 repairs and 2,871 set-aside ordinary terminal leaves. The checker also verifies 36 support-first certificates and a deep forest with 1,491 actual entering edges. The endpoint checker separately checks common suppliers of both signs. These finite checks exercise the algebra. The light-group count, simultaneous bounds and uniform-in-order statements are mathematical arguments above. The constants arising from planning up to 432 levels are too large for a practical numerical modulus threshold. The unrestricted conjecture is still open.
