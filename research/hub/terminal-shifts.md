# Terminal matchings at several prescribed shifts

**Theorem.** Fix $D\ge3$ and $0<\epsilon<1$. For all sufficiently large $k$, a finite tree is $k$-cordial if it has at most $k^{1-\epsilon}$ vertices of degree greater than $D$ and at most $k^{1-\epsilon}$ leaves **directly adjacent** to those vertices. There is no bound on the number of leaves at distance two from them, on maximum degree or on tree order.

In particular, take any tree with at most $k^{1-\epsilon}$ vertices above degree $D$ and subdivide each pendant edge **once**. Every resulting tree is $k$-cordial at sufficiently large $k$. Subdividing only the leaf edges at the high vertices already suffices. The [earlier result](subdivided-blocks.md) used two subdivisions. The difference is already visible on trees made almost entirely of length-two arms at several centers.

## A stronger prescribed forest lemma

Use the setup and notation of the [multiple-boundary forest extension](subdivided-blocks.md#forest-extension-with-multiple-boundary-labels). There are $k-1$ free vertices with incoming edges, one forbidden vertex residue $u$, at most $k^{1-\epsilon}$ originally prescribed external residues and at most $k^{1-\epsilon}$ selected vertices including all vertices of free degree greater than $D$. Cut outgoing edges at the selected vertices. In the resulting forest $F$ each cut child has the actual label of its original parent as its external shift. It suffices to assume that $F$ has at least $\delta k$ internal edges for a fixed $\delta>0$. **Edges from a component root to one of its children now count.** The conclusion is an injection avoiding $u$ with all original incoming weights distinct.

Follow the earlier pin construction on the selected vertices and their free parents. Before translation, also avoid twice every originally supplied external residue among the pinned weights. There are only $O(k^{1-\epsilon})$ extra exclusions and each pin adds at most one incoming weight in the original order. Pin labels avoid all original external residues. The resulting collection of shifts has size $O(k^{1-\epsilon})$, and the remaining degree in $F$ is bounded by $D$, including an external edge at every root.

If at least $\delta k/4$ internal edges have two nonroot endpoints, the earlier forest lemma applies directly, starting again with its own pins if needed. Otherwise at least $3\delta k/4$ edges go from a root to a child. At most $\delta k/4$ of these children are nonterminal, since each nonterminal child gives a different edge with two nonroot endpoints. Thus at least $\delta k/(2D)$ different component roots have a terminal child. Keep one such edge per chosen root. Choose a degree-$d$ vertex $z$, $2\le d\le D$, away from pins and their free neighbors for the final sum equation. There are at least $\delta k/(D-1)$ possible vertices before exclusions, by summing degrees minus one. Exclude the pins, $z$ and their free neighbors from all chosen edges. Mark exactly $M=\lfloor\delta k/(100D)\rfloor$ surviving parents and defer their $M$ terminal children. These parents are distinct component roots, with arbitrary external shifts.

As in the [positional embedding](one-exceptional-degree.md#embedding-the-remaining-forest), delete the pins, $z$ and the deferred children, and split the rest into pieces of size less than $m=\lceil k^{\min(1/10000,\epsilon/20)}\rceil$ using at most $k/m$ separators. Each remaining component root of $F$ starts its piece. A polynomially sublinear error below means $O(k^{1-c})$ for some fixed $c>0$, which can decrease between estimates. In particular the number of marked parents ineligible to be an initial seed due to removal or proximity to a pin is polynomially sublinear. The choice of leaves to defer and the positional batches do not depend on the later random assignments.

### How many of the marked parents to place separately

Group the eligible marked roots by external shift. Sort shifts into dyadic classes according to their positive root counts. Some class of $b$ shifts, each having between $h$ and $2h$ eligible marked roots, satisfies $bh=\Omega_{D,\delta}(k/\log k)$. There are $O(k^{1-\epsilon})$ shifts, so $h=\Omega(k^\epsilon/\log k)$ in this class.

If $b\le(\log k)^{20}$, choose one shift $p_0$ in the class and translate labels by $-p_0$ and weights by $-2p_0$. All the separately placed parents will have shift zero. If the translated zero is neither forbidden nor pinned, $p_0$ was an original external residue. In this case pin one extra eligible root of this shift at zero and keep it out of the matching. Its external edge has weight zero, which was protected by the extra pin-weight exclusions. Take a replacement marked edge if needed. Avoid existing pins and $z$ when choosing this new pin. One may rebuild the separators after this step. At most $O(k/m)$ parents become ineligible, negligible compared to the chosen shift's $\Omega(k/\log^{21}k)$ parents. Set $r=\lfloor k/(\log k)^{24}\rfloor$ in this case.

If $b>(\log k)^{20}$, translate the forbidden label to zero. Remove the zero shift from the class if present, and use $b$ for the remaining size. Set $r=\lfloor k/(\log k)^8\rfloor$. In both cases, zero is excluded from the final parent and child label sets by a dummy or a nongadget pin. Put $s=2r$. Budget $s$ separate root slots at the chosen shift in the first case, and in the second round $s/b$ to counts $s_p$ differing by at most one and totaling $s$ across the class. These budgets are fixed before the random draws.

### Two independent large sets inside the positional assignment

Let $R$ be the number of roots of $F$. Reserve outside vertex and color mass $(R-s)/k$ for ordinary root arcs. Its shift quotas use the counts of all roots of $F$ minus the budgeted separate counts. These are nonnegative. Pins, $z$ and separators which are roots may be counted in these quotas and subsequently disregarded. Follow the [permuted-coordinate transportation construction](subdivided-blocks.md#forest-extension-with-multiple-boundary-labels). In its inside mass $g=1-(R-s)/k\ge\delta$, allocate vertex and color roles $A,C$ of equal density
$$
v=(M+s)/k.
$$
They account for the $M$ deferred children and their edges and the $s$ separately placed roots and their external edges. All remaining roles are the nonroot bulk positions, the nonexternal edges of their positional batches and the leftovers, including the dummy and color gap. In a layer $j\ge2$ there is one internal edge per vertex. Use only the independence constraint $A\perp C$. Since $v<g/2$ for large $k$, the transportation exists. Hall's prescribed-differences permutation, with one flagged change, is chosen from the budgeted ordinary quotas. At each source $x$ draw its vertex role and the color role of $\pi(x)$ from the joint law, independently over $x$. The *whole* arrays $A,C$ are independent Bernoulli sets, despite the permutation. Every separate positional role is also a Bernoulli set. [Marshall Hall Jr., prescribed differences](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1952-0050579-7) · [Transportation formula](branching-pools.md#several-approximate-embeddings-in-the-same-cyclic-group)

In the common intersection $H=A\cap C$, sample six disjoint roles $X_1,Y_1,Z_1,X_0,Y_0,Z_0$, each of density $r/k$ in the group. In the many-shift case also sample a disjoint $W$ of density $s/k$ and a disjoint $J$ of fixed positive density $\tau$ sufficiently small in terms of $\delta,D$. At each coordinate in $H$ choose with the indicated probabilities divided by $v^2$. This is possible since $v$ is bounded below and $r/k\to0$. Crucially, the resulting joint categorical trials are independent at **physical group coordinates**, because $A,C$ are independent arrays. Write $X=X_1\cup X_0$, and similarly $Y,Z$. These unions have density $\rho=s/k$. In the one-shift case set $W=X$. The sets
$$
A'=A\setminus(X\cup Y),\qquad C'=C\setminus(W\cup Z)
$$
are each Bernoulli of density $(M-s)/k$, since all the stated subtractions are disjoint on the respective side. They need not be independent. Roles $X_i,Y_i,Z_i$ will be the references for two successive matching theorems. Their sizes and $|X|,|W|$ have fluctuations at most $O(k^{0.6})$ with high probability.

### Assigning an external edge to each seed in $X$

In the one-shift case $x\mapsto x$ supplies every target, spending $W=X$ exactly. In the many-shift case we show that all $x\in X$ can get distinct targets in $W\cup J$, using their allowed shifts from the chosen class, spending all but $o(r)$ of $W$, and differing from the shift budgets by total absolute error $o(r)$. No shift needs more than its inventory of eligible marked parents.

On the bipartite sets $X,W$ take the arc $x\to x+p$ when its target is in $W$ and $p$ is in the class. For each shift $p$ make $s_p$ copies in a third part. Independently give each of its arcs a uniformly random one of these copies as its third endpoint. The resulting tripartite hypergraph is linear. Two vertices on the first two sides determine their only possible arc, and a site together with a shift copy determines at most one neighbor. Put $\lambda=\rho b$. With high probability every degree is $(1+o(1))\lambda$ and $\lambda\gg(\log k)^2$.

For details, at any site the prospective number of neighbors in the other side has binomial parameters $b,\rho$. Each shift is nonzero, so testing membership at the site is unnecessary for this uniform degree bound. For each fixed $p$, the number of $X$ to $W$ arcs is $(1+o(1))\rho^2 k$ simultaneously. Its indicators use coordinate pairs $x,x+p$. The dependency graph has degree at most two and can be split into at most three independent classes. For each class Chernoff bounds the deviation from its mean by $O(\sqrt{k}\log k)$ with failure probability smaller than any polynomial inverse, including for order-two shifts. Distinct coordinates give the product probability $\rho^2$. Since $s/b\to\infty$, the expected degree of each copy conditional on these counts is $(1+o(1))\rho^2k/(s/b)=(1+o(1))\lambda$. Chernoff and a union bound give the assertions, using $b>(\log k)^{20}-1$.

The edge-coloring theorem of Molloy and Reed partitions this linear 3-uniform hypergraph into $\Delta+O(\Delta^{2/3}\log^4\Delta)=(1+o(1))\lambda$ matchings. The number of arcs is $(1+o(1))s\lambda$, so one matching has size $s-o(s)$, where throughout this estimate means a lower bound. Its shift counts do not exceed $s_p$. [Molloy and Reed, *Near-optimal list colorings*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/1098-2418%28200010/12%2917%3A3/4%3C376%3A%3AAID-RSA10%3E3.0.CO%3B2-0)

To cover every missed $x$, partition the class of shifts into groups of sizes in $[G,2G]$ with $G=\lceil(\log k)^2\rceil$. Independently for each $x\in X$ choose a group with probability proportional to its size. Allow targets in $J$ using this group. Every source has at least $\tau G/2$ available neighbors with high probability, uniformly since the trials for $J$ at the shifts in any one group are independent. Every target has fewer than $\log k$ prospective sources with high probability. Indeed, for a fixed target $y$ the $b$ possible sources $y-p$ are different. Independently each belongs to $X$ and chooses the group of $p$ with probability at most $2\rho G/b$. The mean is $O(\rho G)=O((\log k)^{-6})$, and the upper binomial tail at $\log k$ beats a union over $k$ targets. These uniform statements do not require independence of $X$ and $J$ at the same coordinate. By edge counting, every subset of missed sources has at least as many neighbors in $J$ as its cardinality. Hall's theorem gives distinct fresh targets. Each group is chosen by $O(sG/b+\log k)=o(h)$ sources with high probability, by concentration and $bh=\Omega(k/\log k)$. Each shift's first matching count was at most $\lceil s/b\rceil=o(h)$, so there are enough real roots of that shift even if all additions from its group use it. Only $o(r)$ additions were needed. Both the target discrepancy from $W$ and the total absolute shift-quota discrepancy are therefore $o(r)$.

Assign each resulting source to a different eligible marked root of its actual shift. Disregard pairs colliding with a pin, the dummy or a pin weight. There are polynomially sublinearly many such pairs by vertex and target injectivity. We can also omit sources in the entire shift set to avoid coincidences with a prescribed neighbor label. The remaining two seed images differ from $X_1,X_0$ by polynomially sublinear errors, which are much smaller than $r$. In the one-shift case the new pin at zero, if used, was excluded from this choice.

### The bulk embedding and the missing weight

Place the remaining roots from the ordinary outside arcs of $\pi$, using arcs only at the required shift. The [binomial quota estimate](permuted-pools.md#meeting-every-leaf-demand) gives a polynomially sublinear error against the **budgeted** ordinary root counts. The actual counts differ by $o(r)$ in total, by the preceding seed assignment. Ignore the flagged arc and pin conflicts. As in the earlier proof, first distribute arcs over all remaining root positions and disregard those at removed sites. The number of missing *eligible* bulk roots is $o(r)$. Repair all these at the first position, **before embedding their children**. For shift $p$ use a fresh $x\in A$ with $x+p\in C$. The independent arrays have linearly many choices simultaneously for all $p$, by concentration. Keep away from pins, the shift set for the label, all seed labels and used seed weights, and all $Y_i,Z_i$ on their respective sides. These exclusions within $A,C$ cost $O(r)+o(r)$ coordinates. The initial outside arcs spend no coordinate of $A$ or $C$.

Now embed nonroot first positions from their own roles and process all later positions using Lemma 7.7. Every assigned root label, including a main-pool repair or a seed, is outside every ordinary child role. Parent labels in a positional batch are distinct. The layer losses and their descendants are polynomially sublinear as in the earlier proof. In particular, no factor $m$ multiplies the $o(r)$ root-quota error. All internal bulk weights lie in disjoint ordinary color roles. Delete pin/dummy collisions, uncut neighbors of pins and any bulk labels in the shift set, reinstating pins. These deletions cost polynomially sublinearly many sites and uncut weights, including any affected seeds. Cut-parent edges at roots were already accounted for. Repair missing nongadget vertices other than $z$ using the independent $A,C$ common-neighbor property. At roots request the external color as well, and at children of roots keep their actual parent's external residue off the child. Pinned children have this property by the pin construction. Protect the surviving seed assignments and $Y_i,Z_i$ on their respective sides. Only polynomially sublinearly many vertices remain to repair after the first-position repairs. [Layer estimate and bounded-degree repairs](subdivided-blocks.md#forest-extension-with-multiple-boundary-labels) · [Müyesser and Pokrovskiy, Lemma 7.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05280v2#A1)

Choose the remaining label of $z$ and the single gap by the [matching sum calculation](one-exceptional-degree.md#repair-and-the-last-group-sum), using the sum of **all $M$ actual marked parents**. If $V,L$ are the unspent label and weight sets and $p_1,\ldots,p_d$ the actual distinct neighbors, the omitted weight must be
$$
w=\sum L-\sum V-\sum_{\rm marked} x-\sum_i p_i-(d-1)z.
$$
The constant-density independent $A,C$ give linearly many choices with $z\in A$, all $z+p_i\in C$ and $w\in C$. The affine map for $w$ has at most $D$ preimages, even at composite moduli. All prior exclusions and the protected roles within these sets cost $O_D(r)+o(r)$, and exclude $w=z+p_i$ as well. Now the remaining child labels and the remaining weights after omitting $w$ both have size $M$ and satisfy the matching sum with the $M$ parents.

### Three matchings on the terminal edges

First take the ordinary marked parents outside the two seed classes. All but $o(r)$ lie outside $A'$ because their genuine root arcs were outside $A$ and the exceptional placements in $A$ number $o(r)$. The count of ordinary marked parents is $M-s$ up to a polynomially sublinear error, so discard any excess above $M-s$. Apply Lemma 7.7 to these parents and the two individually $(M-s)/k$-random sets $A',C'$. It leaves polynomially sublinearly many unmatched. Discard any matched triple touching an already used label, weight or the gap. Inside $A'$ the initial seed labels and protected child sets are absent by construction. Inside $C'$ the initial seed weights are absent except for $o(r)$ targets outside $W$. All other collisions on these two sides cost $o(r)$ by the repair estimates. The sets $Y_i,Z_i$ were excluded in defining $A',C'$. Consequently only $o(r)$ ordinary triples per side remain to be assigned. The actual remaining parent, label and color sets still contain the two triples of small reference sets up to **polynomially sublinear** omissions. The $o(r)$ error concerns the number of extra sites outside them.

Set aside $r$ actual sites on each side as close as possible to $X_0,Y_0,Z_0$ respectively. They differ from those references by polynomially sublinear errors, using concentration of their sizes and the preceding omissions. The other remaining sites include $X_1,Y_1,Z_1$ up to polynomially sublinear errors plus $o(r)$ extras on each side. Reflect the weight side throughout to use the multiplication hypergraph $x+y+z'=0$. Apply Müyesser and Pokrovskiy's three-set approximate matching lemma to the three $r/k$-random references (reflected on the weight side). More explicitly, outside the references trim the three extra sets to the smallest of their sizes. Trimming costs polynomially sublinearly many sites, since the actual part sizes are equal and reference sizes and omissions have only those errors. Their common size is $qk=o(r)$, in particular $q<(r/k)/4$. Invoke the lemma on the ideal references and trimmed extras. Triples using unavailable reference sites can be discarded, one triple per missing site. Together with its $k^{1-10^{-4}}$ bound, this leaves only polynomially sublinearly many sites outside the set-aside triple. This use is in the range $2q<r/k$ of the proof's three-way split. [Müyesser and Pokrovskiy, Lemmas 3.8 and 3.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2204.09666#S3.SS1)

Finally the actual equal-size sets differ from $X_0,Y_0,-Z_0$ on the reflected sides by a polynomially sublinear error, and their total group sum is zero. The true parent and child sets avoid zero. If weight zero remains, match it with $x,-x$ from these two sets. On inverse orbits of size two there are $\Omega((r/k)^2 k)$ such pairs in the references with high probability, by independence across orbits and disjoint categorical sampling at individual coordinates. Enough pairs survive the error. After this possible deletion none of the three actual sets contains zero.

The three reference marginals are $p=r/k$-random. They are $p^2/3$-slightly-independent. At each inverse orbit choose one of the three indices uniformly and give it that orbit if both members lie in its reference. Thin the acceptance of a singleton orbit by an extra factor $p$. Different orbits are independent, and reflection makes no change to orbit membership. Theorem 4.3 now gives the final perfect matching. Its allowed error, $(p^2/3)^{10^{17}} k/(\log k)^{10^{17}}$, dominates every polynomially sublinear error here, and the required lower density holds eventually since $p$ is an inverse power of $\log k$. Assign each terminal child its matched label. All weights, including the edges restored below pinned high vertices, are their actual endpoint sums. This proves the forest lemma. [Müyesser and Pokrovskiy, Definition 4.1 and Theorem 4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2204.09666#S4) · [Published paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-025-01328-x)

## From forests to trees

Use [consecutive preorder blocks](subdivided-blocks.md#consecutive-preorder-blocks), with children ordered by increasing subtree size. Each nonempty block has $b\le k-1$ sites and $O(\log k)$ different original external parents. Cut at its high vertices. A singleton component either starts at a low external parent, is itself high, or is a low child of a high parent. The first case gives $O_D(\log k)$ singletons, the second at most $k^{1-\epsilon}$. In the last case a nonleaf site is immediately followed by its child in preorder and their edge survives, unless the site ends the block. Thus all other singletons are actual leaves directly adjacent to high vertices. There are at most $O_D(\log k+k^{1-\epsilon})$ singletons in total. Every component of order $j\ge2$ contributes $j-1\ge j/2$ internal edges. The unpadded block therefore contributes at least $b/2-O_D(\log k+k^{1-\epsilon})$.

Extend an original block terminal by a temporary path to order $k-1$. This adds at least $k-1-b-1$ internal edges, allowing one cut if the terminal is high. The padded block has at least $k/3$ internal edges for sufficiently large $k$. Its maximum degree outside the high set is at most $D$ and its boundary labels and high count satisfy the forest hypotheses after decreasing the exponent slightly. Label the block avoiding the prefix's surplus vertex residue and using distinct new weights. Discard the temporary vertices. After a full block the next single vertex can restore the deficient edge weight. Starting with one root proves cordiality at every order.

## Verification

The finite checker reconstructs shift-copy triples, matches the unused sources to a separate target set, and recomputes all external weights and capacity counts. It also tests the three successive terminal matchings on complete finite forests and counts internal edges of actual preorder blocks. The probabilistic existence and the threshold for sufficiently large $k$ follow from the proofs above.
