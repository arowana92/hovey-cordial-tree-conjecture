# Two adjacent exceptional degrees at every sufficiently large even modulus

**Theorem.** For each fixed $D\ge3$, every sufficiently large even $k$ has the following property. Every tree with two adjacent specified vertices $a,b$ and with all other degrees at most $D$ is $k$-cordial. The order, both exceptional degrees, and their leaf populations are arbitrary.

**Density consequence.** For fixed $D$, the moduli at which every tree in this class is cordial have natural density one. In fact for each fixed prime $p$, every sufficiently large multiple of $p$ works, by this theorem for two and by the [odd-divisor theorem](adjacent-odd-divisor.md) for the other primes. Along any unbounded sequence of counterexample moduli for this class the least prime factor must tend to infinity. For a finite set of primes, the upper density of exceptions is at most the product of $1-1/p$ over the set, by counting residue classes modulo their product. These products tend to zero. Indeed their reciprocals, expanded as geometric series over primes through $z$, include the harmonic sum through $z$.

## Twelve identical branches at the involution

Put $k=2q$. At a vertex with label $q$, **twelve copies of any rooted branch** can be labeled so their new vertex labels and their actual edge weights are the same set, avoiding any prescribed set. If $N$ is the number of new vertices plus the number of forbidden residues, $k>2048(N+1)$ suffices, uniformly over all branch shapes.

Use the [five signed pieces](signed-tree.md#five-small-pieces) on six identical copies at an abstract root. After processing each copy up to its attachment, its remaining stem is empty, one vertex or two vertices. The six stems are identical. The pairs of single stems or triples of long stems exhaust these at the abstract root. Thus there is no initial piece to scale. In parent-before-child order use the affine forms of each piece at its assigned parent $z$, starting with $z=q$. This supplies the signed edge identities on these six copies, including their edges at the prescribed parent.

At each parameter choice keep both signs of every label fresh and distinct. Among the forms $\pm(At+Bz)$ of any one piece, a pair of equal slopes has intercept difference $z$ or $-z$, as follows directly from the five rows. Every parent is nonzero, including the initial involution. There are at most twelve forms, with nonzero slopes of magnitude at most eight. Avoiding any already forbidden value costs at most 96 choices per value across the forms. Their mutual comparisons of different slopes cost at most $16\binom{12}{2}$ additional choices. Include zero and $q$ among the forbidden values. These costs fit the stated cutoff. On the other six copies take all negatives. Since $q=-q$, even their boundary weights are the negatives of the original ones. Pairing both signs of the signed identities proves the assertion. The same argument at any modulus permits six copies at $z$ and six at $-z$ for any prescribed nonzero $z$, using both actual parents.

## Reduction to a bounded shallow tree

Delete the direct leaf neighbors outside the two sites, leaving ordinary order $h$. The [positive-density theorem](concentrated-leaves.md) settles $h\ge\delta k$ for any fixed $\delta>0$. In the sparse case first treat total order $k$ and let $a$ have the larger direct leaf population. Put $0,q$ at $a,b$ respectively. Write $m_b$ for the smaller population. If $m_b\ge2h$, both sites meet the [order-$k$ fiber bound](cluster-lift.md). Otherwise $h+m_b<3h$, and we may require $k>K_D(h+m_b+1)$ by choosing $\delta$ small. A core consisting solely of the two sites gives a caterpillar.

At each site remove groups of twelve identical ordinary branches of height below ten, preserving one deepest branch. Here height counts vertices outward from the site. If all heights are at most four and four identical nontrivial branches occur at $b$, preserve four such branches as well. The number and sizes of the remaining short branches are bounded in terms of $D$. Groups at $b$ can be restored by the preceding construction. Groups at zero can be restored in six reflected pairs by the [signed branch theorem](signed-tree.md). Each pair there uses a fixed bounded shape and one free scale. The number of exclusions is linear in earlier occupancy.

If the deepest branch has height at least five, the [two-site bounded-batch proof](odd-branch-groups.md#a-sparse-tree-with-one-long-ordinary-branch) applies verbatim after this change of grouping. The algebra of its long batches and free-boundary conclusion uses the actual parent labels $0,q$. Keep $m_b\bmod 2$ leaves at $b$ inside that construction and restore the others in full two-element fibers. This produces the edge weights of the sparse core as its vertex labels with one omission.

For height at most four the entire residual ordinary tree is bounded. If $m_b$ exceeds a sufficiently large constant depending only on $D$, the [bounded residual fiber construction](odd-branch-groups.md#short-branches-and-two-unbounded-leaf-populations) works with divisor two. The underlying fiber theorem allows every divisor. Removing the temporary leaves at zero preserves the sole omission. In the remaining case $m_b$ is bounded. If there were no four identical nontrivial ordinary branches at $b$ in the original tree, then $\deg(b)$ is bounded in terms of $D$. There are only boundedly many possible shapes. The [one-exceptional-degree theorem](one-exceptional-degree.md) now applies.

It remains to label the bounded shallow residual in the case of four equal nontrivial branches at $b$. Keep $m_b\bmod2$ additional leaves at $b$. If one of the equal branches contains a vertex with two children that both have children, its deepest such vertex provides the usual two arms or four-site path with a **free** boundary. The [ordinary four-site proof](modular-injection.md#stopping-with-at-most-twelve-levels) applies. Otherwise the nonterminal vertices of each equal branch form a single chain, with only additional leaves at the chain vertices. Write $p=q$. We give the details for all three possible heights.

## The ordinary labels and the gap

Withhold the branches specified in each case below, including their terminal leaves. Keep the fourth equal branch as an ordinary branch. Apply the first-layer walk and increasing-slope method with actual boundary labels $0,p$. All bulk labels except the two sites have positive integer slope in $X$. The edge histogram minus the vertex histogram is $-[0]-[X]+[L]$, where $L=sX+c$. The fourth branch supplies an ordinary internal edge, so $s\ge2$ and $s$ strictly exceeds the slope of every bulk label. This strict inequality follows because the final ordinary assignment places the current terminal and then increases its slope by the positive slope of its parent. [First-layer construction](modular-injection.md#stopping-with-at-most-twelve-levels)

### Height two or three

Withhold three equal branches. At their tops place $p-L,-L,L$ respectively. At one child of each place $p+L,T+L,T$ respectively, choosing the continuing child if one exists. Here $T=-MX$ with $M>10(n+1)(s+1)$ or equal to that bound, where $n$ is the order of this bounded residual including its retained direct leaves. The six actual new weights replace the label $L$ by zero. For instance the first root edge weighs $-L$ and its child edge weighs zero since $2p=0$. The second root edge supplies $p-L$ and its child edge supplies $T$. The last edges supply $p+L$ and $T+L$. All six vertex forms are fresh. The forms at slope $s$ differ by $p$, as do the two forms at slope $-s$. This leaves gap $X$. [Lean verification of the six edges](InvolutionArms.lean)

At height two the equal numbers of extra leaves at $-L,L$ can be paired. At height three do the same for their terminal children outside the selected chain. In addition pair as many remaining leaves at $p-L$ and $p+L$ as possible, since those parents too are opposite. To place any such pair at $z,-z$, use fresh labels $U,U+z$ in that order. Their weights exchange the labels. Do all paired placements last, with a new sufficiently large positive slope for each pair.

At height two the only unpaired leaves are at $p-L$. Place each at the current gap minus its parent, advancing the gap to that new label. The slopes strictly increase above $s$. At height three each of the parents $T+L,T$ has at least one leaf, since all three selected branches have height three. Process all those leaves first by the gap rule. Every such step increases the slope by $M-s$ or $M$. The remaining unpaired parents are all $p-L$ or all $p+L$. The subsequent gap slopes therefore advance strictly in one direction by $s$ per step. If they decrease, their total decrease is less than $M-s$, the smallest preceding upward step. They stay strictly above all previous slopes except the most recent maximum, which they never revisit. If they increase, they stay above every previous slope. This separates all the unpaired labels.

### Height four

Withhold just one equal branch, whose distinguished path has four exterior vertices. Put
$$
-p-L,\quad -L,\quad L,\quad -p-2L
$$
on this path. Direct addition gives $-L,-p-2L,0,-p-L$. Thus these edges replace $L$ by zero and use no constant vertex label. Let $A,B,C$ be the extra terminal leaf sets at the first, second and third path vertices respectively. First reserve opposite-parent pairs between $B$ and $C$. Pair remaining leaves of $C$ with leaves of $A$ until one of the two lists is exhausted. Each latter pair will stand for a **virtual first-layer jump**.

Include one virtual singleton at $b$ for each such jump in the ordinary construction, before choosing $L$. Give it its ordinary first-layer label $t$. Later use $t$ on the real leaf at $L$ and $t+L$ on the real leaf at $-p-L$. Their weights are $t+L,t-p$. Their edge-minus-vertex contribution equals $[t+p]-[t]$, exactly that of the virtual singleton because $p=-p$. The actual labels replacing the virtual row are distinct across all such pairs. [Lean identity](InvolutionArms.lean)

In the first layer put a genuine root of the fourth branch at $b$ last in the jump list. Every nonzero jump is $p$, so after excising simple cycles the residual walk is empty or consists of this final jump from zero to $p$. Every virtual row is consequently in an excised cycle. In translating all cycles, avoid both intercepts $0,p$ as well as earlier cycles. This costs only boundedly many exclusions. Each virtual label is then $t=X+c_t$ with $c_t\notin\{0,p\}$. In particular the new labels $t+L$ have slope $s+1$ and avoid both $X+L$ and $X+p+L$.

After these exchanges the unpaired leaves are all at negative-slope parents $-L,-p-L$, or are all at $+L$. Use the gap rule from $X$. For negative-slope parents the first label is $X+L$ or $X+p+L$, safe by the two intercept exclusions, and the slopes subsequently increase by $s$. For positive-slope parents the new slopes $1-js$, $j\ge1$, are strictly negative and differ from all negative gadget slopes, which are multiples of $s$. Restore the opposite-parent pairs with fresh slopes as before.

## Completion and checks

All the constructions of the shallow residual use boundedly many affine forms with bounded slopes depending only on $D$. Specialize to distinct residues outside the bounded list of forbidden parameters, allowing up to the absolute slope difference exclusions per comparison. Restore the twelve-copy groups. Put the remaining direct leaves at $b$ in disjoint unoccupied fibers $\{j,j+q\}$, which add identical vertex and weight sets. At sparse order the linear cutoff ensures enough residues for these steps. The resulting core has $a=0$ and one omission, so [the periodic leaf extension](README.md#uniform-witnesses-for-large-moduli) supplies every requested leaf at $a$. At orders below $k$ pad at a site and restrict. Above $k$ in the sparse range remove direct leaves to order $1\bmod k$ or a single leaf at order $1\bmod k$, then restore their actual weights by the [distinct-leaf extension](../counterexample-bound.md#removing-leaves). The dense ordinary case was already settled at every order.

The independent checker reconstructs 96,480 weights on 972 neutral packets, covering every rooted branch through order nine. It also checks 313 complete two-site certificates including 130 virtual jumps. The checks exercise powers of two and twice odd moduli, both parities of the virtual count, and each of the six constructor cases. The uniform claims use the proofs above.
