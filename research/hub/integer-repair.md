# Cubic integer span for a tree rooted at a prescribed zero

**Theorem.** There is an absolute $C$ such that every tree of order $h$, with any prescribed root, has distinct ordinary integer labels in $[-C(h+1)^3,C(h+1)^3]$, with the root at zero, whose edge sums use every vertex label except one. In particular the **same integer labels** reduce to a suitable injection at every modulus above their span. The [quadratic modulus theorem](quadratic-root.md) gives a smaller threshold for labelings chosen individually at each modulus.

We give a version of the repair argument that also works when part of a tree has already been completed to an interval using marked filler leaves. Only the labels at actual boundary vertices enter the formulas below.

## Restoring withheld branches over the integers

Work in a rooted tree, optionally with a bounded number of protected vertices. Use the [three short branch alternatives](quadratic-root.md#short-branches-with-unequal-populations) and the [alternatives on paths left by pruning](quadratic-root.md#paths-left-after-pruning). Take $B=865$ repair jobs of one type, so there is a job per pair from the [integer forest preparation](integer-forest.md). Any surplus jobs use $b=t$ chosen fresh. In the alternatives with one common boundary, pin that vertex as well. The numbers reserved can be selected before its label is known: for the star alternative take 25 branches per job, then at boundary zero split them into five zero-boundary jobs. For the depth-three alternative take 30 per job and partition into fifteens at a nonzero boundary or tens at zero. All job counts and all numbers of spine vertices, groups and auxiliary roots per job are bounded by absolute constants.

Here are details of the structural dichotomy. Remove enough disjoint stars at a common supplier, pairs of stars at distinct suppliers, or branches consisting of two successive supports and their terminal children, when the corresponding alternative is present. A bounded number of pins precludes only boundedly many disjoint interiors. In the third alternative many disjoint branches yield many at a single boundary or many distinct boundaries. If none of these alternatives supplies the required collection, let $J$ consist of vertices of downward height at least two. Each nonroot tip of $J$ has either two child stars or exactly one, in which case its subtree is a branch of the third type. Thus the number of tips of $J$ is bounded. Its branch vertices and degrees are bounded too. At most boundedly many vertices of $J$ have multiple child stars and no vertex has unboundedly many. Delete the tips, junctions, root, and multiple-star positions of $J$. This leaves boundedly many paths of positions with at most one star each. An unbounded number of starred positions gives any fixed number of disjoint 43-position windows with a central star, away from the ends. Otherwise delete the boundedly many starred positions. Unbounded remaining length supplies any fixed number of 87-position blocks of one type: nonconstant direct leaf count, constant positive count, or all zero. Discard windows containing a protected position or any of its off-spine descendants among the removed vertices. From disjoint windows this costs at most the number of pins. All bounds may depend on this number. If no alternative applies, $J$ has bounded order, and so the original tree has boundedly many nonleaf vertices. These assertions follow equally by applying the dichotomy to a sequence on which the number of nonleaf vertices tends to infinity.

Compress just the selected windows to edges, or remove the selected branches. The tree stays connected and retains every pin. Suppose that after labeling it the entire current histogram has the form
$$
 E-V=-[g]+\sum_i([t_i]-[b_i]).
$$
Assume the occupied set has size $O(h+1)$, the pairs have the guards of the forest lemma, and their entries, the initial $g$ and the compressed tree labels have magnitude $O((h+1)^2)$. In the common nonzero star case also require $g\notin\{-p,-2p,-3p\}$ for its boundary $p$. The constants in these hypotheses can pass through to the bounds below. A fresh equal pair for a spare job can be chosen of magnitude $O(h+1)$, avoiding both signs of all occupied or protected values and the finitely many forbidden multiples per boundary.

Use the actual integer tables in the following constructions. Their histogram identities use only addition and hold over the integers. Distinct-slope comparisons can be enforced on a box of side $C_0(h+1)^2$: fixing the other independent integer coordinates, at most one value of a coordinate with nonzero coefficient gives equality. There are $O((h+1)^2)$ comparisons, and a sufficiently large $C_0$ makes their union smaller than the box. This also permits finite exhaustive specialization.

- **Common star supplier.** At a nonzero boundary use the five-root table and put the three largest arities on the three positive-coefficient roots of each control group. At zero use one root per group. The three primary groups give $[b]-[t]$. In the backward leaf walk take a first jump at each of two independent control roots, then process their lists with the sign of the total first. Each control list keeps a nonzero coefficient after it starts. The only first-tail exception uses $g\notin\{-p,-2p,-3p\}$. Avoid these three values for each next gap. The forest guards on $b,t$ give the remaining equal-slope tests. [Table and tests](pendant-stars.md#one-repair-with-a-moving-omitted-weight) · [Unequal arities](quadratic-root.md#short-branches-with-unequal-populations)
- **Distributed pairs.** Use the six four-boundary groups and a fixed extra pool. Choose each boundary quadruple avoiding zero and the specified subset sums involving $t,b,g$, before ordering its suppliers by arity. Each new member excludes boundedly many distinct boundary choices. The unused partners and extra pool give only boundedly many auxiliary root jumps. Translate their erased cycles away from the terminal, then use the primary and two control groups as in the paired-star proof. [Selection, roots and walk](paired-stars.md#completing-one-repair-and-all-unused-stars) · [Permuting unequal profiles](quadratic-root.md#short-branches-with-unequal-populations)
- **Two successive supports.** Use three branches per group at nonzero boundaries and two at a common zero. In a control group at three nonzero boundaries the mean signed coefficient over the six orders is $(m_a+m_b+m_c)/3>0$, where the $m$ are the positive outer arities. At zero one of the multipliers $4,-4$ gives an order with positive total. For distinct suppliers reserve the control triples for all jobs first, with no opposite pair, then the first primary triple of each job with $a\ne-t$ and $b_1\notin\{-a,-a-t\}$. Order each remaining triple with two nonopposite labels first. There is no first-tail slope equality in this case. [Depth-three tables](depth-three-repair.md) · [Allocation and mixed-profile checks](quadratic-root.md#short-branches-with-unequal-populations)
- **Starred positions.** Place opposite centers on six pairs of windows per job. Restore the two bridges on each side of a center with the prescribed center or virtual weight. These fixed-size placements may be made successively. Place the paired star roots and boundedly many auxiliary roots as in the starred-window table. Two independent parameters give positive control coefficients on the direct children of the last two selected pairs. [Exact windows and collision tests](exceptional-degrees.md#windows-centered-at-stars)

In these four alternatives all new labels that **serve as parents of other new vertices** are bounded by $O((h+1)^2)$. Indeed they are bounded-coefficient affine expressions in the original boundary labels, $b,t$, a bounded number of parameters from the stated box, and in some cases a sum of boundedly many boundary jumps. The old omitted weight $g$ affects boundary selection and the backwards walk, but does not enter these parent expressions. The 21-vertex bridges at opposite centers prescribe the center or old virtual weight, again independent of $g$. The raw backward tails therefore have magnitude at most $|g|+O(h(h+1)^2)$. A walk may repeat a vertex value. In forward order erase its simple cycles; the residual path has distinct tails. Translate each cycle away from the residual tails and earlier occupied values, using a shift of magnitude $O((h+1)^2)$ by pair counting. This does not alter any leaf parent or the new omission. Thus over the bounded number of jobs every assigned label has magnitude $O((h+1)^3)$, with exact final discrepancy $-[g']$.

## A common final walk for leaf-only windows

There is a different dependence on $g$ when the windows have only direct leaves. One bridge prescribes $g$ itself. To maintain the preceding degree-three bound, restore **all bare spines before any of their direct leaves**. Number the jobs $1,\ldots,r$, let $G_0=g$, and give job $i$ its own free mark $G_i$. Restore the [active or constant bare spans](exceptional-degrees.md#leaf-counts-that-vary-along-the-path) with difference
$$
 [b_i]-[t_i]+[G_{i-1}]-[G_i]
$$
relative to the old compressed edge or edges. Specialize the first $r-1$ spines successively. Keep their marks, all bridge parameters and other free parameters within the box, avoiding previously occupied and reserved labels except for the prescribed $t_i$. All spine labels have magnitude $O((h+1)^2)$: the next prescribed $G_{i-1}$ is a **parameter in the box**, regardless of the previous job's leaf counts. The bare differences telescope. Keep all last-job coordinates free until its leaf walk is chosen.

In the active case the two control bridges have nonzero total coefficients by the [forty-position lemma](exceptional-degrees.md#leaf-counts-that-vary-along-the-path). From $G_r$ first take one jump on each control list, then all deferred earlier-job leaves, then finish the usual lists with jumps of the total's sign first. The first tail contains $G_r$ and one bridge parameter. Every later tail has both control coordinates nonzero, which no assigned label does.

In the positive constant case let $m$ be the last block's common count. On each of the first two bridges a nonempty proper prefix of its sign-sorted list has positive total coefficient and its whole list totals zero. Take the first jump of each, process the earlier jobs' leaves, and follow the constant-block order with the last jump of each list saved for the end. Through the step preceding those last jumps all tails after the first have both guard coordinates. Write $T$ for the sum of the jumps from the **earlier** jobs, fixed before the last specialization. By the first moment of the bare histogram, the last spine's internal labels sum to $b_r-t_r+G_{r-1}-G_r$. The final gap equals
$$
 (m+1)G_r-m(b_r-t_r+G_{r-1})-T .
$$
Its $G_r$ coefficient is different from that of the mark, and it has no bridge parameter. All non-mark spine labels involve a bridge parameter. The penultimate tail also involves the second guard bridge parameter, while the first involves $G_r$ and the first one. The first two bridges themselves do not depend on $G_r$. These checks give nonconstant equalities against all fixed labels and exclude identically equal assigned labels. All-zero blocks need only the bare restorations.

Specialize the last job in the same box by counting against all assigned and protected labels. Erase and translate cycles in the one combined walk. Its jumps at all stages have magnitude $O((h+1)^2)$ and there are at most $h$ of them. This proves the cubic bound in the final alternatives.

## The prescribed root

For the stated theorem use just the zero root as the initial seed, including any pinned common supplier as an ordinary vertex during preparation; a zero initial gap meets its extra condition automatically. Apply the forest lemma to the compressed tree below the root. If the common supplier is nonzero, the number of branches per job was chosen to handle that case. The result of repair has exactly one omitted label. The histogram forces it to be occupied.

In the bounded-nonleaf alternative it suffices to prove the bound with a constant depending on the absolute nonleaf bound $N$ supplied by the dichotomy. Use the [four-site integer construction](universal-hub.md#labeling-the-remaining-tree) and its [short root-branch case](branches.md#one-short-branch-allows-arbitrary-other-branches). In the four-site case give root neighbors labels through $h$, and process all other remaining supports **before terminal children** in the positive running sum. The at most $N$ support steps can at most double the running value each time. Thus all parents in the remaining tree have magnitude $O_N(h+1)$, and its terminal running value is $O_N((h+1)^2)$. The four specified sites have that same bound. The at most $h$ unpaired jumps and paired leaf placements cost $O_N((h+1)^3)$ in span. In the short-branch case likewise do all available nonterminal vertices first, after the specified initial negative child. Each support step increases the larger of the two running magnitudes by at most a factor two. The terminal steps thus leave both running endpoints $O_N((h+1)^2)$. The reserved root neighbor then has magnitude of that order, and its remaining leaves cost $O_N((h+1)^3)$. A star works directly. This proves the uniform theorem.

## Reproduction

The new window checker uses distinct original edge objects, reconstructs their subdivided paths, and recalculates full histograms on actual endpoint sums after restoring several jobs together. Its nine trees contain 126 jobs and 28,685 edges, including active profiles, unequal positive uniform counts across jobs, all-zero blocks and neutral jobs. The marked application also uses its 792 tests of moving the omission to an extreme. The universal structural and magnitude bounds are proved above.
