# Extending a prescribed rainbow core using lists at the leaves

**Tree theorem.** Let $T$ have $n\ge3$ vertices, of which $h$ are nonleaves. At every prime $p\ge n+h-1$, arbitrary independent additive offsets on its edges admit an injective vertex labeling with distinct edge weights. In fact **every** such labeling on the nonleaf core extends to the whole tree. The threshold for extending a prescribed core is sharp as a bound in the two sizes alone.

More generally, suppose a labeled core uses $h$ distinct vertex residues and $e$ distinct weights modulo $k$. Add $r$ leaves to arbitrary core vertices and prescribe an offset for each leaf edge. Its effective shift $s_i$ is the parent's label plus that offset. Write $U,W$ for the sets of used vertex labels and weights. For leaf $i$ the available labels are
$$
L_i=\mathbb Z_k\setminus(U\cup(W-s_i)).
$$
The following results also allow arbitrarily different lists contained in these available sets.

## Prime and composite moduli

At a prime $p$, if $r<p$ and every list has size at least $r$, all leaves can receive distinct labels $x_i$ with distinct weights $x_i+s_i$. This is the list version of Alon's additive transversal argument. The product of the Vandermondes in $x_i$ and $x_i+s_i$ has degree $r(r-1)$. In its top degree term, the coefficient of $\prod x_i^{r-1}$ is $(-1)^{\binom r2}r!$. Expanding the two determinants pairs each exponent permutation with its reversal, giving $r!$ terms of that same sign. The coefficient is nonzero in the field. Combinatorial Nullstellensatz on $\prod L_i$ now supplies the labels. [Alon, *Additive Latin Transversals*, §2](https://www.cs.tau.ac.il/~nogaa/PDFS/alt2.pdf)

There are at least $p-h-e$ available residues per leaf. For the tree, $e=h-1$ and $r=n-h$. The stated bound gives at least $r$ available residues. The core itself can be labeled greedily along a rooted traversal. With $t$ vertices present, at most $t+(t-1)$ residues at the next vertex are forbidden by earlier labels and weights. This proves existence as well as the stronger prescribed-core statement.

For sharpness, take $k=2h+r-2$ with $h,r\ge2$. On any $h$-vertex core prescribe distinct labels in $U$ and $h-1$ different weights in $W$ disjoint from $U$. Choose each core offset to give its specified weight. Attach the $r$ leaves and choose their offsets so every effective shift is zero. Their common available set has only $r-1$ residues. Leaves can be attached at the ends of a core path to make precisely the $h$ core vertices nonleaves. This obstruction uses a fixed labeling and offsets. It places no restriction on ordinary cordial labelings of that tree.

For general $k$, normalize the shifts by subtracting the same shift from each, and let $m$ be the order of the subgroup generated by their differences. If the multiplicities of different shifts are $a_1,\ldots,a_t$, put $M=r!/\prod_j a_j!$. The same extension holds if every list has size at least $r$ and $M$ is outside the additive semigroup generated by the primes dividing $m$ (the semigroup for $m=1$ is $\{0\}$). In particular the numerical bound $k\ge h+e+r$ suffices under this condition.

To see this, represent residues by complex $k$th roots of unity. The two Vandermondes now have lists of arguments $z_i$ and $\alpha_i z_i$ where $\alpha_i$ represents $s_i$. The coefficient of $\prod z_i^{r-1}$ is, up to a common sign, the sum of $\prod \alpha_i^{\pi(i)}$ over permutations of $0,\ldots,r-1$. After dividing by $\prod a_j!$ and removing the fixed common shift factor, this is a sum of $M$ roots of unity of order dividing $m$. Lam and Leung's weight theorem excludes zero under the stated condition. Apply the grid theorem over the complex numbers. This uses the multiplicative proof of Dasgupta, Károlyi, Serra and Szegedy with the equal shifts grouped before counting terms. [Transversals of additive Latin squares](https://services.math.duke.edu/~dasgupta/papers/finallatin.pdf) · [Lam and Leung](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9511209)

## Exact two-shift criterion for all lists

Fix two different shifts modulo $k$ with multiplicities $a,b$, put $r=a+b\le k$ and let $d$ be the additive order of their difference. The following are equivalent.

1. **Every** family of $r$ lists of size $r$ admits distinct choices with distinct shifted values.
2. This holds whenever all $r$ lists are the same set.
3. $b\bmod d\ \le\ r\bmod d$, or equivalently adding $a$ and $b$ produces no carry in their lowest base-$d$ digit.

For sufficiency, normalize the shifts to zero and a difference represented by a primitive $d$th root $z$. Up to a nonzero factor the coefficient above is
$$
\sum_{0\le i_1<\cdots<i_b<r}z^{i_1+\cdots+i_b}
= z^{\binom b2}\prod_{j=1}^b\frac{1-z^{r-b+j}}{1-z^j}.
$$
The identity as a polynomial follows by splitting the subsets according to whether they contain $r-1$, or by induction using that split. At a primitive $d$th root the order of vanishing is $\lfloor r/d\rfloor-\lfloor a/d\rfloor-\lfloor b/d\rfloor$. Every multiple of $d$ contributes one simple zero. Under condition 3 this number is zero, so the coefficient is nonzero and the grid argument proves 1. Clearly 1 implies 2.

For necessity, let the common list consist of $\lfloor r/d\rfloor$ entire orbits of translation by the shift difference, together with $r\bmod d$ consecutive entries of another orbit if needed. The chosen inputs must exhaust this list. If an entry is assigned the nonzero shift and its successor belongs to the list, that successor must also receive the shift to avoid a collision. Each full orbit therefore contributes zero or $d$ shifted entries, and the partial orbit contributes at most $r\bmod d$. This forces condition 3. The same proof applies to any permutation with all cycles of length $d$ in place of the relative translation. Represent its cycles by disjoint sets of the form $c\{1,z,\dots,z^{d-1}\}$ with different positive $c$.

For tree attachments, this supplies the same $h+e+r$ bound at any modulus whenever the two effective shifts meet condition 3. At a prime every nonzero shift difference has order $p$. At a composite modulus the common-list construction gives explicit counterexamples to the list statement at every failing multiplicity pair.

One additional available residue in each list resolves every exception for two shifts. A progression of shifts and the same argument on cores of arbitrary order give further tree bounds. [Proof and checks](spare-lists.md)
