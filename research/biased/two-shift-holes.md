# Two prescribed shifts with forbidden vertex labels and edge weights

Let $U$ be a set of $h$ forbidden vertex labels and $W$ a set of $e$ forbidden edge weights in $\mathbb Z_k$. Put $r=k-h-e\ge0$. Consider $r$ leaves with prescribed parent colors or effective shifts in $\{a,b\}$. Exactly $j$ of them have shift $b$. We seek distinct sources outside $U$ whose shifted destinations are distinct and outside $W$. At a single shift the construction is immediate since at least $r$ source-destination pairs remain.

**Exact criterion.** Suppose $a\ne b$. Subtract $a$ from the destinations, including $W$. Partition the residues into cosets of the subgroup generated by $b-a$, of order $d$.

* If any coset meets both $U$ and $W-a$, every $0\le j\le r$ works.
* Otherwise let $q$ be the number of cosets containing no forbidden residue on either side, and let $D=(k/d-q)d-h-e$. The possible counts are exactly
$$
                       \bigcup_{i=0}^{q}\{id,id+1,\ldots,id+D\}.
$$

In particular all counts work with a single forbidden destination and any number of forbidden sources, or with the shores interchanged. If its coset is disjoint from the other forbidden set, this single deletion contributes $d-1$ to $D$. The intervals then cover the entire range. If the difference of the two shifts generates the whole group and both forbidden sets are nonempty, every count also works. A result for fewer than $r$ leaves follows whenever all counts at $r$ work, by padding and restricting.

## Proof by matchings

Draw separate source and destination copies of the group. The two available shifts make alternating cycles of length $2d$, one per coset. Delete the forbidden vertices. Each of the two individual shifts still supplies a matching of size at least $r$, since at most $h+e$ pairs of that shift are lost.

If a cycle has a deleted source and a deleted destination, first delete just these two. The two paths between them have even numbers of surviving vertices, so they can be matched perfectly. Together with matchings in the other cycles this gives $k-1$ edges. Each further forbidden vertex costs at most one edge, leaving a matching $M$ of size at least $r+1$.

In any graph containing such an $M$, all matchings of size $r$ are connected by moves replacing one edge with another. To see this, start with a matching $N$ of size $r$. While $N$ has an edge outside $M$, use a path in the symmetric difference that starts and ends with an edge of $M$ and has one more $M$-edge than $N$-edge. Such a path exists because $|M|>|N|$. Starting at one end, replace each encountered $N$-edge by the preceding $M$-edge. The last $M$-edge is now free. If an edge outside $M$ remains, exchange any such edge for that last edge. The intersection with $M$ grows until $N\subseteq M$. Any two size-$r$ subsets of $M$ are connected by exchanges inside $M$. In particular the matchings using exclusively the first and exclusively the second shift are connected. One move changes the number of second-shift edges by at most one, so all intermediate counts occur.

If no coset contains deletions on both shores, a cycle with $c>0$ deletions loses vertices from just one shore. It breaks into odd-order paths and its maximum matching size is $d-c$. On a path with $2l+1$ vertices, leaving any even-indexed vertex unmatched and pairing the two sides consecutively realizes each second-shift count from zero through $l$. Thus the affected cycles together realize every count through $D$ at their maximum sizes. Each intact cycle has two perfect matchings, contributing zero or $d$. Their maximum sizes sum to exactly $r$, so all must be attained. This proves necessity and sufficiency of the interval formula.

**Guarantees from the sizes alone.** Let $m=\lceil h/d\rceil+\lceil e/d\rceil$. For a prescribed $j$, every pair of forbidden sets of these sizes works if and only if
$$
              m\ge k/d\qquad\text{or}\qquad j\bmod d\le md-h-e .
$$
Disjoint collections of affected cosets need at least $m$ cosets. If they use exactly $m<k/d$, concentrating the two forbidden sets in disjoint collections realizes the least possible $D=md-h-e$. The displayed residue test is then equivalent to membership in the intervals, since $0\le j\le r$. Spreading the holes over even one more coset adds $d$ to $D$ and permits every count. When $m\ge k/d$, either some coset has both kinds of deletion or every coset is affected.

## Consequences for trees

The [short star theorem](../rainbow/pinned-star.md) through $k-2$ new vertices holds even with **two** singleton-parent colors, with arbitrary color at the entering parent of the star. Normalize the surplus to zero. Choose a nonzero center label equal to one of the singleton-parent colors when a nonzero one occurs. Otherwise choose one. There are now at most two effective shifts outside the center, including all star children. The available sources exclude zero and the center, and the destinations exclude the center's entering edge weight. The single-destination case above labels up to $k-3$ other vertices. This also permits an independently prescribed nonzero center label whenever its choice leaves at most two shifts. The pinned counterexample at $k-1$ already uses just one singleton-parent color at each composite modulus. Hence the short-length bound is sharp here too.

In a smallest failure of order $mk+1+t$ with $t-1$ leaves and $k-t=2$, the leaves must have at least **four** different parents. Otherwise remove every leaf and then one leaf of the retained tree. The removed star and the remaining singletons have at most two singleton parents and can be restored.

There is a separate bound on the two parents with the most leaf neighbors. Write the order of a least counterexample as $mk+s$, $1\le s\le k$, and call their combined leaf count $B$. The earlier [two-shift list bound](spare-lists.md) gives $B<2s-k$. Test the extremal value $B=2s-k-1$ when it is positive. Delete these leaves and label the smaller core. It has $h=k+1-s$ high vertex bins and $e=h-1$ high edge bins, and exactly $r=k-h-e=B$ leaves to restore. The criterion above applies to these actual high bins.

At $k=27$ this gives $B\le17,19,23$ at residues $s=23,24,26$, respectively. At $s=25$ and $B=22$, every cordial labeling of that core must give the two parents colors differing by 9 or 18. Both leaf multiplicities must equal two modulo three. The three high vertex labels must constitute one coset of the order-three subgroup, and the two high weights, after subtracting the first parent color, must lie together in a different coset. Indeed these are precisely the disjoint placements with $D<d-1$ at this residue. Equal parent colors always work. These are necessary conditions on a smallest failure.

The [independent checker](check_two_shift_holes.py) enumerates matchings directly by trying to skip each available source or send it to either of its allowed destinations. At orders 2 through 8 it compares all 349,555 choices of nonzero normalized shift and forbidden sets with $h+e\le k$ against the exact formula. Intersecting the answers at fixed sizes checks the size-only criterion separately. At 27 it also checks every eligible shift order and multiplicity in the five surviving order residues, and constructs the full matching-count spectrum at the cut with $U=\{0,9,18\}$, $W=\{1,10\}$ and shifts 0 and 9 by exhaustive matching in each three-residue coset.
