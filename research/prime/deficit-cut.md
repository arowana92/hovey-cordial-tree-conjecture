# Joining a tree one vertex short of a full period

**Prime cut theorem.** In a smallest noncordial tree at a prime $p$, no edge with at least two vertices on each side separates a component of order $-1$, $0$ or $1$ modulo $p$. The new case is $-1$. The tree order is unrestricted.

**Gluing lemma.** Let $k$ have least prime factor $\ell\ge5$. Fix a cordial labeling on a core of order $r\bmod k$, where
$$
1\le a=k-r\le\max\{1,(\ell-7)/2\}.
$$
Take any cordially labeled tree of order $qk-1$. Join the trees at any pair of vertices. Some affine permutation of the labels on the second tree gives a cordial labeling of the union, preserving every core label. An offset on the bridge and fixed offsets on the old core edges are allowed.

The affine symmetry used here is Theorem 8 of Graham and Sloane in the harmonious case. Their proof tracks the same change $w\mapsto uw+2x$ of an edge weight. [Graham and Sloane (1980), §4](https://neilsloane.com/doc/RLG/073.pdf#page=10)

## Balancing the two deficits

Use coordinates in which the old bridge endpoint plus the bridge offset is zero. Subtract this constant from all old vertex labels and twice the constant from the old edge weights. We will add the constant back to every label at the end. Let $A$ and $B$ be the high vertex and edge residues on the core. Their complements have sizes $a$ and $a+1$, respectively.

On the second tree, translate its unique low vertex residue to zero. Let $b$ be its bridge endpoint label and $d,e$ its two distinct low edge residues in these coordinates. Under an affine change $y\mapsto uy+x$, with $u$ a unit, the second tree adds uniform counts except for a missing vertex at $x$ and missing edges at $ud+2x,ue+2x$. The bridge weighs $z=ub+x$. It suffices to take $x\in A$ and either fill one of the edge holes with the bridge and put the other in $B$, or put both holes in $B$ and the bridge outside $B$.

First fill the hole from $d$. This fixes $x=u(b-d)$ and leaves the hole $u(e+2b-2d)$. Interchanging $d,e$ gives a second choice. For any nonzero direction $v$, uniform units distribute $uv$ uniformly on an orbit of size $\varphi(\operatorname{ord}(v))\ge\ell-1$. This follows by restricting units to the cyclic subgroup generated by $v$, using the Chinese remainder theorem. If both directions are nonzero, at most a fraction $(2a+1)/(\ell-1)<1$ of units fail one of the two target conditions.

The two possible vertex directions differ by $e-d$ and the edge directions by $3(e-d)$. These are both nonzero. If $0\in A$ we can choose a nonzero edge direction and allow a zero vertex direction. If $0\in B$ we can choose a nonzero vertex direction. The same counting proves existence. The only remaining case has $0\notin A\cup B$ and a zero vertex direction in one choice and a zero edge direction in the other. Swap $d,e$ if necessary. The equations give
$$
d=b,\qquad e=3b/2,\qquad b\ne0.
$$

## The remaining configuration

Write $C=(\mathbb Z/k)\setminus B$. The bridge can now use $z\in C$. With $x$ the vertex hole, the edge holes are $x+z$ and $(x+3z)/2$. First try $z=0$, which belongs to $C$. Here $x=-ub$ ranges over the unit orbit of $b$. The two holes are $x,x/2$. Among the orbit, at most $a-1$ choices miss $A$ and at most $a$ each miss $B$ at $x$ or $x/2$, since zero lies in neither high set. This works whenever $\varphi(\operatorname{ord}(b))>3a-1$. In particular this handles $a=1$, and any composite order of $b$, whose totient is at least $(\ell-1)^2$.

It remains to treat $b$ of prime order $p\ge\ell$ with $a\ge2$. Work in $H=\langle b\rangle$, using $b$ as coordinate one. Let $n=|A\cap H|\ge p-a$ and $m=|C\cap H|\le a+1$. We have $m\ge1$. On the grid $X\in A\cap H$, $Z\in C\cap H$, in these coordinates, use the polynomial
$$
(X-Z)\prod_{c\in C\cap H}(X+Z-c)(X+3Z-2c).
$$
A nonzero value gives two allowed edge holes. Since $X\ne Z$, lift $Z-X$ to a unit $u$ modulo $k$ to realize $z=x+ub$. This produces the required affine labeling.

To prove that the polynomial is nonzero somewhere on the grid, put $D=2m+1<p$. The coefficient of $X^{D-j}Z^j$ is $[t^j]f(t)$ for
$$
f(t)=(1-t)(1+t)^m(1+3t)^m.
$$
Its coefficients have no three consecutive zeros. Indeed $S f'=T f$ for $S=(1-t)(1+t)(1+3t)$ and a polynomial $T$ of degree at most two, by the product rule. Since $S(0)=1$, the coefficient $(j+1)f_{j+1}$ is determined linearly by $f_j,f_{j-1},f_{j-2}$. Three zeros propagate to the nonzero leading coefficient, since $D<p$.

The allowable indices have $0\le j<m$ and $D-j<n$. If zero is allowable, its coefficient is one. Otherwise the interval contains $n-m-2\ge p-2a-3\ge 3$ indices. Some coefficient is nonzero. Finite-grid interpolation now gives the desired pair. [Alon's grid argument](https://www.cs.tau.ac.il/~nogaa/PDFS/alt2.pdf)

## Consequences for the smallest counterexample

Write its order as $mp+s$, $1\le s\le p$. The [six-leaf inequality](../biased/six-spare-leaves.md) gives $2s-p\ge6$. The ordinary cases at small primes are already known. If a component of order $-1\bmod p$ is removed and $s\le p-2$, the complement has order residue $r=s+1$ and $1\le p-r\le(p-7)/2$. Both sides are smaller trees and have cordial labelings. The gluing lemma gives a contradiction. At $s=p-1$ the complement has [divisible order](../divisible-cuts.md). At $s=p$ it has [order one modulo the prime](../census/prescribed-path-surplus.md#other-pieces-of-order-one-modulo-the-modulus) and is larger than one by hypothesis. These two cuts already allow a smaller tree to be labeled and restored. This proves the prime cut theorem.

In particular, a path between two branching vertices whose internal vertices all have degree two has at most $s-3$ edges in a smallest prime counterexample. Along such a path the orders on one side of its successive edges are consecutive, and both sides have at least two vertices. Modulo $p$ these orders avoid $-1,0,1,s-1,s,s+1$. For $s>p/2$ the longest remaining cyclic interval has length $s-3$, also when the two forbidden triples overlap near zero. A pendant path from a branching vertex to a leaf has at most $s-2$ edges, since the orders cut off starting from the leaf are $1,2,\dots$ and $s-1$ is forbidden at every internal cut.
