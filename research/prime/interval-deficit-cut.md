# Gluing several missing consecutive residues at arbitrary moduli

Fix a cordial labeling of a core of order $-a\bmod k$, $1\le a<k$. Join it at any pair of vertices to a cordially labeled piece of order $-h\bmod k$, $1\le h<k$. Suppose the piece has its $h$ low vertex residues and $h+1$ low edge residues in arithmetic progressions with the same step $\delta$. The starting residues may differ, and the terms in each progression must be distinct. In particular any super edge-magic piece of this order qualifies with $\delta=1$.

**Theorem.** Write $N$ for the additive order of $\delta$ and put
$$
E=h(2a+1),\qquad F=a(2h+1),\qquad D=\max(E,F).
$$
An affine change of the piece labels preserves the old labeling and makes the union cordial if either sufficient condition holds.

1. $\varphi(N)>3h E^2F$.
2. For some $p^v$ exactly dividing $k$, the step has additive order $N_p$ modulo $p^v$ with $\varphi(N_p)>p^{\lfloor\log_p(3h)\rfloor}D$.

Both tests allow independent choices of the two ports and an arbitrary bridge offset. At a fixed deficit $h$, the second condition allows the number $a$ of low core bins to grow linearly in the largest prime-power factor when $\delta=1$. The first condition also handles products of many small factors.

For example, at $k=1024$ a piece with three consecutive low vertex residues and four consecutive low edge residues attaches to a prescribed core with five low vertex bins. Here $D=35$ and $\varphi(1024)=512>8\cdot35$. With just one low vertex in the piece, it already applies at $k=64$ for $a=4$, since $\varphi(64)=32>2(3\cdot4)$. At $k=4725=27\cdot25\cdot7$, $a=1$ and $h=2$, the first test applies because $\varphi(4725)=2160>6\cdot6^2\cdot5$. The three local totients are 18, 20 and 6, with respective strict thresholds 18, 30 and 6.

## Three choices for the joining edge

Translate coordinates so the old port plus bridge offset is zero. Write $A$ for the high vertex set, missing $a$ entries, and $C$ for the low edge set of size $a+1$. Write the two progressions as $V_i=v_0+i\delta$ for $0\le i<h$ and $W_j=w_0+j\delta$ for $0\le j\le h$, and the piece's port as $b$. Under the affine change $y\mapsto uy+x$, its missing vertex and edge labels become $uV_i+x$ and $uW_j+2x$. They are deficits of one relative to full periods. This description also applies when the piece has uniform edge counts and all $k$ entries of its last period are missing.

First try to fill the edge hole $W_0$ with the bridge. This sets $x=u(b-W_0)$. All $h$ vertex holes must now land in $A$, and the other $h$ edge holes must land outside $C$. Each expression is a unit times a fixed direction. If all those directions have unit orbits of size greater than $E$, a uniform random unit misses at least one target with probability at most the sum of $h$ terms for $a$ forbidden residues and $h$ terms for $a+1$ forbidden residues, each divided by its orbit size. This sum is less than one. Apply the same test at $W_h$.

Third, fix any bridge value $z\in C$ and set $x=z-ub$. The bridge fills the old low bin at $z$. The $h$ vertex holes must avoid the $a$ low old vertices, and the $h+1$ edge holes must avoid the $a$ entries in $C\setminus\{z\}$. Their directions are $V_i-b$ and $W_j-2b$ around fixed translates. The same counting works if all their orbits have size greater than $F$. Each of the three procedures, when successful, yields balanced histograms on the entire tree.

## Order of the common step

Suppose the three procedures all fail. Each failure supplies a direction with unit orbit of size at most $E,E,F$ respectively. Let $J$ be the subgroup generated by these three directions. Put $T=b+v_0-w_0$ and $S=2b-w_0$. Use additive notation in the quotient by $J$. Writing $[i,j]\delta$ for the indicated multiples of the step, the three failed tests give one condition in each row.

| Bridge choice | A vertex direction vanished | An edge direction vanished |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fill $W_0$ | $T\in[1-h,0]\delta$ | $S\in[-h,-1]\delta$ |
| Fill $W_h$ | $T\in[1,h]\delta$ | $S\in[h+1,2h]\delta$ |
| Use $z\in C$ | $S-T\in[0,h-1]\delta$ | $S\in[0,h]\delta$ |

If the step order in this quotient exceeded $3h$, the two alternatives involving $T$ in the first two rows would be disjoint, as would the alternatives involving $S$. In one crossed case $S-T\in[h+1,3h-1]\delta$ and $S\in[h+1,2h]\delta$. In the other, $S-T\in[-2h,-2]\delta$ and $S\in[-h,-1]\delta$. Neither permits the third row. Thus some $1\le j\le3h$ satisfies $j\delta\in J$. [Lean proof of the quotient interval calculation](DeficitIntervals.lean)

In a cyclic group a direction of order $v$ has orbit size $\varphi(v)$. The order of $J$ is the least common multiple of the three individual orders. The totient of a least common multiple is at most the product of the three totients, by the prime-power formula, so $\varphi(|J|)\le E^2F$. The order of $j\delta$ is $N/\gcd(j,N)$ and divides $|J|$. The inequalities $\varphi(gm)\le g\varphi(m)$ and $\varphi(d)\le\varphi(m)$ for $d\mid m$ now give $\varphi(N)\le3hE^2F$.

In a cyclic prime-power factor, the three projected subgroups are nested. The totient of their joint order is at most the largest of their three orbit sizes and hence at most $D$. Since $N_p$ is a power of $p$, the factor lost on multiplying the step by $j$ is at most the largest power of $p$ not exceeding $3h$. This proves the second sufficient condition.

For arbitrary hole sets, an [exact sparse-profile criterion](sparse-deficit-profiles.md) gives both an existence test and genuine fixed-label affine obstructions. With two missing vertices, even a single gap in a vertex interval can prevent every affine join.

## Trees assembled from pieces

For fixed $t,H$, at every sufficiently large $k$, take any $t$ super edge-magic trees with orders $q_i k-h_i$, where $1\le h_i\le H$. Join them according to any tree on the $t$ pieces, with arbitrary attachment sites and independent offsets on the joining edges. The result is $k$-cordial. The orders $q_i k-h_i$ have no upper bound.

Root the tree of pieces and start with any one piece. At each attachment the old deficit is the sum of the previous $h_i$, at most $(t-1)H$. Apply the first test with $\delta=1$. For an explicit sufficient bound take $k>tH$ and $\varphi(k)>3H[H(2tH+1)]^2[(2H+1)tH]$. The totient tends to infinity with $k$, for example by $\varphi(k)\ge\sqrt{k/2}$ from the prime-power formula. The proof also works for any initial core with a prescribed cordial labeling and the required initial deficit.
