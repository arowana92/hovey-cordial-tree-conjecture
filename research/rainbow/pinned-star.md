# A divisibility obstruction for a prescribed forest

The restriction on the forests in the [rainbow test](README.md) matters for composite moduli. The following family characterizes one source of failure exactly.

**Proposition.** Fix $k\ge2$ and $1\le s\le k-1$. Take $k-1-s$ singleton leaves with boundary label zero and one rooted star on $s$ free vertices. Its center attaches to a prescribed boundary label $b$, and its other $s-1$ vertices are leaves. There is a bijection of the free vertices to the nonzero residues whose edge weights are distinct if and only if $b$ lies in $s(\mathbb Z/k\mathbb Z)$. Equivalently, $\gcd(s,k)$ divides an integer representative of $b$.

**Necessity.** Suppose $b\ne0$ and the center receives $x$. The weight $x$ cannot appear. A singleton would need vertex label $x$, a star leaf would need label zero, and the center edge would require $b=0$. Since the other $k-1$ weights are distinct, their sum is the sum $S$ of the entire group minus $x$. Summing the same weights as the labels of the free children plus the labels of their parents gives $S+(s-1)x+b$. Hence $b=-sx$. For $b=0$ the divisibility condition already holds.

**Sufficiency.** For $b\ne0$, choose $x$ with $sx=-b$ and assign it to the center. Write $j$ for the additive order of $x$, and write $s-1=qj+h$ with $0\le h<j$. Since $sx\ne0$, we have $h\le j-2$. For the $s-1$ star leaves, choose $-x,-2x,\ldots,-hx$ and $q$ whole cosets of the group generated by $x$ outside that subgroup. There are enough cosets since $s-1<k$. These labels avoid zero and $x$. Adding $x$ to them preserves the whole cosets and shifts the displayed segment one position. The center edge has weight $x+b=-hx$. Together, the star edges occupy exactly the leaf-label set together with zero, including when $h=0$. Give the singleton vertices the remaining nonzero labels other than $x$. Their weights equal their labels. The only missing weight is $x$.

If $b=0$, take $x=1$, label the star leaves $-1,-2,\ldots,-(s-1)$, and again give the singleton leaves the remaining nonzero labels other than 1. The star-leaf weights are $0,-1,\ldots,-(s-2)$ when $s\ge2$ and the center edge weighs 1. The resulting weights are distinct, also for $s=1$.

For $k=9$, the choices $s=3,6$ and $3\nmid b$ give twelve impossible pinned inputs. In every one the forest has seven vertices without children. The nine-label proof needs only forests with at most five. The obstruction concerns this stronger pinned extension requirement. The ordinary nine-label theorem allows the core and its coloring to be chosen.
