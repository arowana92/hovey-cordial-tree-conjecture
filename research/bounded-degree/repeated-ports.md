# Repeated attachment vertices and a square-root obstruction

Use the [tree insertion theorem](port-parity.md) with $t$ entering edges on a free tree $U$ of order $k$ and bounded maximum internal degree $D$. Allow several old components to attach at the same free vertex. Write $m_v$ for the number of entries at $v$. Assume the old endpoint labels of the edges entering **each particular vertex** are pairwise distinct. Different free vertices may receive the same old labels.

**Theorem.** The same four parity conditions characterize failure to preserve the old cordial labeling in each of the following regimes at sufficiently large $k$. The augmented degree is now $d_U(v)+m_v$.

| Hypothesis on the attachment sites | Number of entering edges |
|---|---|
| $\max m_v$ bounded by a fixed constant | $t\le k^{1-\epsilon}$ for fixed $\epsilon>0$ |
| Arbitrary multiplicities | $t=o(k^{1/3})$ |
| Arbitrary multiplicities and at least two leaves with $m_v=0$ | $t=o(\sqrt{k})$ |

The [sharp threshold theorem](port-threshold.md) strengthens the arbitrary-multiplicity cases to $c_D\sqrt{k}$ entries for all free tree shapes, with path obstructions at every large modulus.

The last row always admits an extension since a nonport leaf has odd augmented degree. In every positive case each histogram needs at most one transfer. The power $1/2$ in the two-leaf row is best possible for arbitrary multiplicities: the examples below fail at $t=3\sqrt{k}$, even on a free path and for infinitely many odd moduli.

## Changes to the positive proof

Use $c_v=d_U(v)-1+m_v$ and $\delta_v=d_U(v)-2+m_v$. Their sums are still $k+t-2$ and $t-2$. The edge and vertex transfer criteria, the two target moments and the parity obstruction are unchanged because they sum the entering weights with multiplicity.

With at least two nonport leaves, use the [uniform construction](multiport.md) or its [two-leaf repair](two-leaf-repair.md). Use a different wire and anchor for every entering edge, even when two wires must reproduce weights at the same free vertex. The quotient modulus $g=\gcd(k,d-1)$ from a frequent internal degree $d$ is bounded in terms of $D$ alone. The original coefficient representatives, protected vertices and all new marks number $O_D(t+1)$ even when individual coefficients are large. When the gcd obstruction requires a transfer, every internal degree-two nonport is still excluded, so the abundance of a third nonport leaf in the vertex repair persists.

The affine expressions for the initial marks use coefficients $0,1,-1$. Two entering weights on one vertex have the same variable but distinct constant terms modulo $k$. Two site labels still have different negative anchor variables. The other edge and vertex comparisons are exactly those in the original construction. The same statements apply when the omitted weights or vertex label are included. For bounded multiplicity, each independent variable still occurs in boundedly many expressions, so each greedy lifting step costs $O_{D,\max m_v}(t+1)$ choices. For arbitrary multiplicities, take independent random lifts in the assigned residue classes. Each nonconstant collision has probability at most $2g/k$, and there are $O_D((t+1)^2)$ comparisons. This works whenever $t=o(\sqrt{k})$. Complete the two leaf and buffer moments just as in the distinct-port proof. The final auxiliary tree or unicyclic graph still has bounded degree because every wire has its own anchor and site.

When at most one leaf is outside the ports, mark the ports and all nonzero $\delta_v$. Their number, and the number of entering or induced internal edge expressions, are $O_D(t+1)$. With one outside leaf and $t\ge3$, its label is the same affine function of the positive positions as in the [sparse-port proof](sparse-ports.md). With two or more positive positions and no outside leaves, use the same particular solution and affine family as in the [parity theorem](port-parity.md#pinned-labels-at-the-joints). The new formal coincidences from repeated entries have two distinct old constants. In the special case of a constant internal sum on two equal positive coefficients, uniform embedding needs no omitted color on the closed auxiliary graph. When a transfer is needed the gcd is greater than one and the choice of a different particular solution still avoids the omitted color.

For bounded multiplicities all affine coefficients remain bounded, and each parameter occurs in boundedly many expressions. This includes the dependent position, whose number of incident entering edges is bounded too. For arbitrary multiplicities each coefficient in a difference is $O_D(t+1)$ even after substitution in the affine family. Uniform independent parameter choices therefore make the total collision probability $O_D((t+1)^3/k)$. Once these local labels are fixed, the short degree-two segments used to replace the actual entering edges have mutually separate endpoints. Their number and all constraints on their assigned pairs remain $O_D(t+1)$. For a uniform closed auxiliary graph one can equally use independent pair variables at its cut endpoints and wire endpoints, as in the sparse-port proof.

For a single positive position $J$ with no nonport leaves, write $d=\delta_J=t-2$ and $h=\gcd(k,d)$. The transfers can force the joint label $x$ through $dx=T+E-2V$ while permitting one bin to vary over at least $k/(2h)$ values. For $h=1$ take a genuine edge transfer if the old edges are nonuniform, and otherwise vary the duplicated vertex bin with its unique old high bin fixed. The other ports are leaves with a single entry each, since $\delta_v=0$ there. At $J$ its several entering weights $x+a_i$ are distinct. Avoid all old attachment labels at $x$, the vertex gap when present, and equality of any $x+a_i$ at $J$ with a prescribed edge gap. Each fixed forbidden joint value costs at most two choices of the moving bin. If the edge gap itself varies, a coincidence $x=F_e-a_i$ implies a congruence with coefficient $d+1$ on $F_e$, and so costs at most $d+1$ moving-bin values per entering edge at $J$. If the vertex gap varies, $x=F_v$ similarly costs at most $|d-2|$ choices for $d\ne2$. For $d=2$ and $h>1$ there are two joint solutions and at most one equals that varying gap. For $h=1$ the vertex-only choice above varies the duplicated bin. Choose any artificial edge gap after fixing the joint label. Thus the exclusions have bounded size for bounded multiplicity, since $d\le D+\max m_v-2$. In general there are $O_D((t+1)^2)$ exclusions and $h\le t$. This suffices when $t=o(k^{1/3})$. The cases of one or two entries follow the same path and one-nonport-leaf arguments as in the parity theorem, including two distinct old labels at the same free tip.

## Every prescribed boundary list occurs on actual cordial trees

Start with any labeled tree and select a vertex as the first hub. At a hub add $2k$ leaves with two copies of every residue. To realize the next prescribed removed weight $W_j$, take one of the new leaves of label $W_j-x_{\rm hub}$ as the next hub, recording its entering edge for deletion. Repeat for $t$ double stars in total, with no further cut at the last. Each double star adds two copies of every vertex label and every edge weight. After cutting the $t-1$ recorded edges, each component retains a second new leaf of every label. It can supply any prescribed old endpoint $a_i$ independently. Thus every list of removed weights and entering labels can be realized without changing either old histogram's high set. In particular, starting at a single zero vertex gives uniform old edges and the sole high vertex bin zero.

## A fixed-core obstruction with distinct old labels at each site

Let $k=m^2$, $m\ge3$. Use any $k$-vertex path and two distinct internal sites. Enter the first at all labels from $A=\{0,1,\ldots,2m-1\}$ and the second at all labels from $B=m\mathbb Z_k$. Use the preceding actual old tree with uniform old edge bins and all $3m-1$ removed weights zero. For any choices $x,y$ of the two free labels, the sets $x+A$ and $y+B$ have exactly two common elements: every run of $2m$ consecutive residues meets each class modulo $m$ twice. At least one common weight is nonzero. It already occurs twice among the entering edges, and its old copies all survive. Uniform old edge bins must increase by exactly one each, so no extension can work. Odd $m$ already gives an infinite family. Both nonport leaves and bounded internal degree are present. At $k=9$ and $25$ the whole trees admit fresh cordial labelings by the separate all-tree proofs.

Local distinctness cannot simply be dropped even for a bounded number of entries. Give two distinct free sites two entries each, all at old label zero. Start at a single zero vertex and use the same actual old construction with all three removed weights zero. Any free site labeled nonzero would give a repeated nonzero weight with no deleted copy. Both sites must consequently be zero, but the original zero vertex bin was already uniquely high, and cannot take two additional zeros in a balanced extension.
