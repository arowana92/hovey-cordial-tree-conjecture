# Prescribed labels at vertices of unbounded degree

Let $T$ have $k$ vertices, let $P$ be a set of prescribed vertices with labels $x_v\in\mathbb Z_k$, and prescribe the missing edge weight $w$. Write $p=|P|$, $e=\sum_v(d_T(v)-D)_+$ for a fixed $D\ge3$, and $M=p+e+1$. Let $B$ be the largest number of pinned neighbors of any **unpinned** vertex. Assume at least two leaves are unpinned.

**Theorem.** Fix $0<\epsilon<1$. For all sufficiently large $k$, it suffices that $M\le k^{1-\epsilon}$ and $(B+1)M\le c_D k$, for a sufficiently small positive constant $c_D$. A bijection retaining the pins and using every edge weight except $w$ exists exactly under the following conditions.

1. The prescribed labels are distinct. The edges with both ends pinned have distinct sums, all different from $w$.
2. The integer $\gcd(k,d_T(v)-1:v\notin P)$ divides $w+\sum_{v\in P}(d_T(v)-1)x_v$.

In particular $p+e\le c'_D\sqrt{k}$ allows pins at arbitrary positions. When the number of pinned neighbors of an unpinned vertex is bounded, $p+e$ can grow as $k^{1-\epsilon}$ after reducing the exponent slightly if necessary. The order $\sqrt{k}$ guarantee for unrestricted positions is sharp up to constants, even for caterpillars.

## Preserving the pinned labels across degree reduction

The two conditions are necessary by injectivity and by subtracting the sum of the vertex labels from the sum of the edge weights. Reserve two free leaves. A degree $2\le d\le D$ occurs linearly often, since degrees above $D$ contribute only $O_D(e)$ to $\sum_v(d_T(v)-1)=k-2$. Set $g=\gcd(k,d-1)$. Among unpinned vertices choose one representative of each nonzero residue of $d_T(v)-1$ modulo $g$. The divisibility test permits choosing representative labels modulo $g$ to give the required coefficient sum modulo $g$, including the contributions of the pins. All remaining free variables can initially have residue zero modulo $g$. Indeed, an unpinned vertex of degree $d$ exists, so the coefficient gcd with $g$ equals the gcd with $k$.

Root at an unpinned vertex $R$ of degree $d$. Cut excess child edges so that each vertex retains at most $D$ children, keeping the two reserved leaves attached to their parents. There are $O_D(e)$ cuts. For each cut $ab$, oriented toward its child $b$, cut a **separate additional edge** $st$ oriented toward its child $t$. These additional edges are disjoint and avoid all pins, reserved leaves, representatives, the root and the original cut endpoints. Their endpoints have original degrees at most $D$. Such a matching exists greedily in the capped forest. The excluded vertices meet $O_D(M)$ capped edges, the original vertices of degree above $D$ meet $O_D(e)$ edges, and each selected edge rules out only $O_D(1)$ further choices.

Assign the former weight of $ab$ to the new edge at component root $t$, and the former weight of $st$ to the new edge at component root $b$. The component of $R$ provisionally has no assigned edge. Each new edge will run to a separate site of original degree $d$, outside the capped closed neighborhoods of all marks, cut endpoints and reserved leaves. Select mutually nonadjacent sites. Across the components there are $\Omega_D(k)$ available. In decreasing order of site counts, keep the first component and attach each next root to an unused site in an earlier component. Every initial segment has at least its proportional share of all sites, so there is an available site at each step. If the first component is not that of $R$, give $R$ the assignment formerly at the first component root. This constructs a tree $A$ of maximum degree at most $D+2$. [Earlier pool argument](sparse-incidence.md#distributing-the-high-degrees)

Make variables for the original marked vertices. For a cut $ab$ the site at the paired root $t$ has label $X_a+X_b-X_t$. Its paired site's label is $X_s+X_t-X_b$. In the one reassignment, replace the corresponding subtracted variable by $X_R$. These formulas preserve the cut weights exactly. In the first form the negative buffer variable is free. In the second form the two positive buffer variables are private to this edge, even if $b$ was prescribed. A reassigned site with $-X_R$ also has a private negative variable since $R$ belonged to no cut. Thus distinct site and base labels are distinct affine forms after imposing the pins. Among the marked edge weights the forms are those of distinct original edges. Two with the same single variable have distinct pinned neighbors. Constant weights and the missing weight satisfy the initial test.

Assign free variables within their chosen congruence classes. Order the original variables parent before child, then $R$, then all the additional-edge variables. In the vertex list $O_D(1)$ forms have their latest variable at any position. In the weight list at most $O_D(B+1)$ do. A vertex whose child is prescribed may determine that edge's sum. The root and additional-edge endpoints themselves have bounded degree. Both lists have $O_D(M)$ elements, since the original induced edges form a forest. Comparisons with different latest variables therefore cost $O_D((B+1)M)$ exclusions at any position. Across each whole list the number of pairs with the same latest variable is also $O_D((B+1)M)$. If that variable cancels, charge the comparison to its last surviving variable. Constant nonzero comparisons are already satisfied. Each surviving coefficient has absolute value at most two and excludes at most two choices in a coset of size $k/g$. Our bound on $(B+1)M$ leaves a legal choice at every step.

In the original tree the coefficient sum on these marks is correct modulo $g$. The new sites had original coefficient $d-1$, and every changed edge has both ends marked and its weight preserved in the multiset. Thus the same congruence holds in $A$. Enlarge the marks to a degree core by including both reserved leaves and adding a bounded number of positions per degree class. Give additional ordinary marks labels zero modulo $g$, avoiding used labels and weights. Two additional isolated positions of degree $d$ solve the exact coefficient sum by their pair sum. The two reserved leaves then solve the unweighted sum. At each pair only $O_D(M)$ choices create label or weight collisions. The marked vertex sum equals $S=\sum\mathbb Z_k$, and the marked degree-weighted sum equals $S-w$. The marked tree has size $O_D(M)$, so Lemma 4.9 extends it to the required bijection on $A$. Restoring the cut edges gives the result on $T$. [Two-pair arithmetic](rooted-gap.md#preserving-the-root-through-degree-redistribution) · [Müyesser and Pokrovskiy, Lemma 4.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05280v2#S4)

## A sharp scale even on a caterpillar

For every sufficiently large $k$, put $q=\lceil\sqrt{k}\rceil$, $m=\lceil k/q\rceil$. Attach $q$ pinned leaves labeled $A=\{0,\ldots,q-1\}$ to an unpinned hub. Along a path from the hub, place $m$ disjoint pinned edges separated from one another and from the hub by unpinned vertices. Prescribe their sums as $W=\{q,2q,\ldots,mq\}\bmod k$. These are distinct. Realize them by fresh pairs outside $A$, choosing one endpoint freely and then subtracting it from the target. Fewer than $2(q+2m)+2$ first choices can fail at a step. Add an unpinned leaf at the hub and extend the path by unpinned vertices to reach order $k$. The two free leaves and an ordinary unpinned degree-two vertex remain. Every $w\notin W$ meets both necessary tests.

Nevertheless $W-A=\mathbb Z_k$ since the integer differences cover $1$ through $mq$. Every hub label creates an edge sum already present on a pinned edge. The host is a caterpillar, with $p=q+2m$ and a single vertex of degree above three. Its ordinary cordial labeling is covered by Hovey's caterpillar result. The obstruction concerns the prescriptions.
