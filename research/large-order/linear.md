# A linear order bound for every counterexample

**Theorem.** There is an absolute constant $C$ such that every tree with at least $Ck$ vertices is $k$-cordial, at every positive modulus $k$. One explicit sufficient value is $C=2^{80}$.

This bounds the order of **every** counterexample. The [previous linear decision bound](hall-pools.md) applied to a smallest counterexample at a given modulus. The [earlier unrestricted order bound](interval-paths.md) was $O(k\sqrt{\log(2k)})$. The linear theorem leaves open, in particular, the order-$k$ and order-$(k+1)$ cases as $k$ varies. The constructions below use Hall’s permutation theorem for cyclic groups and the floating-variable rounding argument for groups of columns. [Hall (1952)](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1952-0050579-7) · [Beck and Fiala (1981)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-218X(81)90022-6)

## Selecting jumps while growing a subtree

Assume $k\ge128$. Write $H$ for the tree of nonleaves. In a counterexample $h=|H|\le Ak$ with the absolute $A=2^{28}$ by the [nonleaf theorem](hall-pools.md#bounds-for-every-counterexample). The [degree theorem](gap-path.md) bounds each individual leaf population by $13001k$. Trees on at most two vertices are immediate. Work in one bipartition of $H$ and rank its sites by decreasing original leaf populations $p_j$. Ignore ranks beyond $k$ for now.

Take any increasing list of existing dyadic ranks $128\le r_i\le k$ and put $R_i=r_i/64$. At rank $r_i$ we want one actual parent for each ordinary jump $d\in[1-R_i,R_i-1]$. A parent chosen for an earlier jump stays its holder. The first rank at which a jump is required determines its minimum available population $p_{r_i}$. Write $R$ for the largest radius. Root $H$ at the top-ranked parent and give it label zero and jump zero. The other jumps are assigned in dyadic sets
$$
s\le |d|<2s,\qquad s=1,2,4,\ldots,R/2.
$$
For a given set use the first retained rank with $R_i\ge2s$, so its first $r_i$ sites are eligible and $r_i\ge128s$.

The *hull* is the union of the paths from the root to the holders chosen so far. Keep marked the root, all holders, every branching point of the hull, and the auxiliary vertices described below. Joining consecutive marks along the hull produces a tree on the marks. Its edges stand for original paths, called segments. Begin with the root alone. We maintain odd segment lengths, so the ends belong to opposite bipartitions in the actual tree.

For the $2s$ new holders exclude existing marks and original neighbors of a marked endpoint **along its incident old segments** (one excluded position per incidence). Choose any $2s$ remaining eligible sites. Enlarge the hull, adding the sites and newly required branching points to the marks. In an old segment that has been split, if a new holder is now consecutive with an old endpoint, also mark an intervening original vertex as a buffer. Such a vertex exists by the exclusion. One buffer adjacent to the old endpoint suffices at each affected end. Finally, in each even segment mark the first vertex after its initial endpoint, making both resulting segments odd. Labels on old marks are retained.

Here and below the same growth operation can add free designated terminals instead of holders, without any exclusions or buffers for those terminals. After a cumulative $N$ designated nonroot sites, there are at most $1+18N$ marks. In fact at most $2N$ nonroot sites have been designated or have become branching points, since the terminal count is at most $N$ (or the hull consists of the root). Every old segment split before the buffer step contains a newly designated or newly branching mark, so the cumulative buffer count is at most $4N$. For a previously odd segment with $a\ge1$ new marks after the buffer step, at most $a+1\le2a$ parity marks are needed. On hanging branches the number of new segments is at most the number of new marks there. Thus at most $12N$ parity marks suffice cumulatively. An unchanged old segment requires none. Before the shell of size parameter $s$, fewer than $2s$ nonroot holders have been chosen. The forbidden set has at most three times the old mark count, hence cardinality at most $3+108s$. The eligible prefix of size at least $128s$ contains enough sites. After the stage there are at most $1+100s$ marks.

## Assigning the integer labels of one stage

We show that the marked tree has uniformly bounded vertex and edge multiplicities in the integers. Its fixed integer labels will also lie in an interval of length proportional to the largest scale. Within a shell the jump values can be permuted between its newly selected holders, since they all meet its population requirement.

A stage $s<128$ gives zero to its new free vertices and any bijection of the jumps to the new holders. The total number of marks created in all such stages is bounded by an absolute constant. For $s\ge128$ put $t=s/128$. Use separate absolute-value bands for the new free vertices. Use $[4t,8t)$ in the root bipartition and $[16t,24t)$ in the opposite part. New holders take the indicated signed shell values. We will bound the counts of all new vertices and new edges at this stage by an absolute constant per integer. All these observations have absolute value between $s/512$ and $4s$.

First handle the portion **on the old hull**. Its new marks split old paths and have at most two neighbors there. Assign arbitrary distinct available shell labels to the holders in this portion. No such holder is consecutive there with an old mark, by the buffers. Two holders are never consecutive since the segments have odd length. Consequently every new edge of this portion has a new free endpoint. Label the remaining new marks there one at a time, using positive values in their free band. For each already known neighbor of value $a$ avoid $|x+a|<t/4$. This excludes at most $t$ integers per neighbor. Also avoid a vertex value or sum at which the count for the present stage and histogram has reached $Q=\lfloor12(M+1)/t\rfloor+10$, where $M$ is the current mark count. There are fewer than $2M/Q$ full rows across both histograms, and each forbids at most three choices. Even the narrower free band has $4t$ choices, so one remains. A row increases by at most two in a step and $Q=O(1)$ since $M\le1+100s$. This proves the count and magnitude claims on the old hull. Edges between two old marks that survive from earlier stages retain their earlier counts.

All new branches **off the old hull** attach to the now labeled portion, with one attachment per component. Order the boundary parents by their signed integer values. Starting with their children, list the new vertices in breadth-first order, appending each parent's children consecutively. Give the outside holders the remaining shell values in increasing signed order along this list. A free vertex in the first generation takes the sign of its boundary parent (use positive at zero). Deeper free vertices take positive values. Within each of the four free types determined by part and sign, assign the integers in the corresponding signed band in nondecreasing order, spreading the sites so each value occurs at most $\lceil M/(4t)\rceil$ times.

For fixed parent and child types, the parent labels and the child labels on the resulting edge list are each nondecreasing. Boundary parents form a separate type and are already sorted. For the other parents this follows because their children are visited in parent order. All outside holders form one more type with increasing labels. Equality of sums within any such sublist forces equality of the child labels. There are only constantly many type pairs, so every sum occurs $O(1)$ times. A first-generation free vertex agrees in sign with its boundary parent. Two free vertices from the present stage in opposite parts have separated absolute-value bands. For an edge incident to a new holder, a neighbor of the present stage has magnitude at most $24t$, and an older boundary neighbor in the opposite part is also free and has magnitude at most $24t$. These facts give the claimed lower bound on the magnitude of each new edge. They also cover a free child with a current-stage holder as parent. The upper bound follows from the band sizes and the earlier scales.

An old marked edge can disappear upon splitting. No edge between two old marks is introduced. A nonzero integer occurs in $[s/512,4s]$ in absolute value for only constantly many dyadic $s$. Thus each integer occurs $O(1)$ times in the final vertex and edge lists, even after arbitrarily many stages. Zero can occur only in the bounded small stages.

## Finishing the compressed core

Protect all holders, and peel 8,400 synchronous rounds of unprotected nonroot terminals of $H$, stopping sooner if necessary. The remaining connected tree contains the entire hull. Let $u$ count its unprotected terminals. Each supplies a disjoint downward path of 8,400 edges in the removed part, by the [peeling argument](gap-path.md#finding-enough-paths-in-a-tree). If $u>0$, add those terminals in one final free stage with dyadic $s$ the least power of two at least $\max(R,u)$. The same construction and counts apply with no new pin constraints. The hull is now the entire remaining tree, since all its terminal vertices are protected or designated. The final stage has at most $1+18(2R+u)\le1+100s$ marks. Set $S_0=\max(R,s)$ if this stage occurs and $S_0=R$ otherwise.

Shorten each marked segment to its representative length in $[1,12]$ modulo twelve. This representative is odd and at most eleven. Denote the resulting tree by $C_0$. Its edge count satisfies
$$
m\le198(2R+u).
$$
Take the scale of the root as one. For a segment requiring intermediate sites in $C_0$, put $s$ equal to the larger scale of its endpoints. At most $1+100s$ final marks have scale at most $s$, since all were present at the end of that stage. They induce a forest. Hence there are at most $1+100s$ such segments with this scale and at most ten intermediate sites on each. Give their intermediate sites positive integers in $[8s,10s)$, choosing them one by one. A site has at most two known neighbors. The two original endpoint magnitudes are at most $2s$. Thus the new vertex values and edge sums have magnitudes in $[6s,20s]$. Avoid rows in the two histograms of this auxiliary step at that scale once their count reaches $6B/s+6$, rounded up, where $B$ is the total of intermediate sites and replaced segments at this scale. At most $2B/(6B/s+6)$ rows are full, and each forbids at most three choices. This gives an absolute count per integer per scale. Bounded overlap of the displayed magnitude ranges gives an absolute constant $b_0$ bounding both integer histograms of $C_0$. All its labels and sums have magnitude at most $20S_0$.

Reduce these values modulo $k$ and [restore all original segment lengths](flexible-path.md#subdividing-edges-with-controlled-multiplicities). The two histograms gain the same vector, bounded by $h/k+96$ per residue. Since $S_0\le2\max(R,u)\le 2Ak$ (increase $A$ to at least one), each original integer count bound also gives an $O_A(1)$ bound after reduction.

**If $u\ge R$**, then $m\le594u$. The paths provide $8400/14=600$ fourteen-vertex blocks per terminal. Cancel each compressed parent-edge discrepancy with one block as in the [gap-transfer theorem](gap-path.md#finding-enough-paths-in-a-tree). The fixed vertex labels in the repairs are the old weights up to sign and have bounded multiplicity. The variable vertices cost $O_A(1)$ per bin since $m\le h\le Ak$. This produces one gap in the histograms.

**If $u<R$**, then $m\le594R$, $S_0=R$, and the integer labels and weights lie in $[-20R,20R]$. For each compressed edge with child $x$ and parent $y$, route one integer demand from $x+y$ to $x$ using the [short-jump construction](interval-paths.md#short-jumps-for-the-small-compressed-tree), with width $R/2$. Each demand takes $O(1)$ steps since the full integer range has length $40R$. Thus there are $O(R)$ jumps. The two-phase rounding bounds the intermediate multiplicity per integer by $2m/(R/2)+12=O(1)$ and the uses of each signed jump by $O(1)$. The source tails have multiplicity at most $b_0$. All tails lie in $[-21R,21R]$. Each jump $d$ from $a$ uses one leaf at the holder of $d$, labeled $a\bmod k$. Its edge has weight $(a+d)\bmod k$. The walk cancels the edge discrepancy, and $R\le k/64$ bounds the multiplicities after reduction. Write $J$ for an absolute upper bound on repair leaves used at any one holder.

In either case the current connected tree has $E=V-\mathbf e_g$, initially with $g$ the root label. Restore the peeled layers in reverse order, omitting vertices already used on repair paths. There are at most $8400+h/k$ batches of size at most $k$. The [single-gap Hall extension](gap-path.md#completing-all-peeled-layers) keeps one gap and costs at most two vertex observations per bin per batch. We now have all of $H$, together with any short-jump leaves. There is an absolute $L$ such that $1+\max V_x\le L$ regardless of the ranks or populations. This uses the absolute choice $A=2^{28}$. The histograms use the actual edges of the original branches throughout.

## Paying for the leaves at all ranks

Choose a fixed integer $P_0\ge\max(128L,4J,1)$. Discard dyadic ranks $128\le r\le k$ with $p_r<P_0$. Their sum of $rp_r$ is at most $2kP_0$. For the other ranks keep only the largest in each dyadic population band, and use exactly these ranks in the construction. Write $p_i=p_{r_i}$ and $W=\sum_i r_i p_i$. The sum before band selection is at most $4W$. Also $\sum_{j\ge i}p_j\le4p_i$ and there are at most $4p_i/P_0$ such indices. These are the geometric-series bounds for ranks and populations. An empty retained list needs no construction.

If $W\ge16\cdot64 L k$, complete a uniform edge histogram as follows. Set $N=\max_x V_x$. The remaining common increment needed in both histograms is $N\mathbf 1+\mathbf e_g-V$, with entries at most $L$. Partition the residue representatives $[0,k)$ into consecutive intervals by floors of the cumulative lengths $k r_i p_i/W$. Within interval $i$ use blocks of length at most $R_i$. Pair the sorted list of required label copies in each block with its reverse. The differences are available holder labels, and each difference is used at most $L$ times per block by [monotonicity of the two lists](leaf-routing.md#moving-a-unit-with-leaves). Give a leaf the label of the first coordinate at the parent labeled by the difference to the second. Its edge supplies that second coordinate.

At a holder first required in interval $i$, the filling uses at most
$$
L\sum_{j\ge i}\left(\frac{k r_jp_j}{W R_j}+2\right)
\le 4\cdot64 Lk p_i/W+8L p_i/P_0 \le p_i/2.
$$
The short repairs used at most $J\le p_i/4$ leaves there. All leaves fit their actual parent budgets. The labeled tree has uniform edges and one surplus vertex at order $1\bmod k$. All other original leaves at arbitrary sites extend by full Hall batches and a final partial one. [Leaf extension](../counterexample-bound.md#removing-leaves)

Consequently $W<16\cdot64 Lk$ in a counterexample. The entire sum of $rp_r$ over dyadic ranks from 128 up to $k$ is $O(k)$ in each bipartition. Each term bounds the populations from rank $r$ through $2r-1$. The first 127 sites contribute $O(k)$ by the degree theorem. If rank $k$ exists, its population is $O(1)$ by the largest dyadic rank at most $k$, which exceeds $k/2$. There are at most $Ak$ further sites. Applying the argument to both bipartitions and adding $h$ proves the theorem. For $k<128$, the same nonleaf and degree bounds give the result with an absolute constant.

## An explicit sufficient constant

Here is one nonoptimized accounting. For $s\ge128$ there are at most $101s$ marks, so the old-hull threshold plus two is below $160000$. There are at most six exterior parent types including the boundary, and five child types including holders. Each such child value has multiplicity at most $\lceil101s/(4t)\rceil\le3232$. The combined count per integer per stage is below $2^{19}$. The small stages together contribute fewer than $13000$ observations to either histogram. The magnitude bounds allow at most twelve large dyadic stages at any nonzero magnitude. In the intermediate-site step $B\le1111s$, and the magnitude interval $[6s,20s]$ meets a given magnitude at at most two dyadic scales. In particular $b_0=2^{24}$ suffices.

The integer range $[-20S_0,20S_0]$ has at most $80A+1$ representatives at a residue. The reduced compressed counts are each below $2^{31}A$. Neutral subdivisions cost at most $A+96$. In the many-terminal repair the two fixed labels per block cost less than $2^{32}A$ per residue and the other labels cost at most $36\lceil12m/k\rceil+12\le432A+48$. For the short walks, a demand visits at most 81 blocks and makes at most 82 jumps. Thus $J=2^{18}$ suffices since $2(82\cdot594R)/(R/2)+12<2^{18}$. The intermediate tail load is at most $2(594R)/(R/2)+12=2388$. Their integer range has length less than $k$. Source tails are separately bounded by the compressed edge counts. The $8400+h/k$ Hall batches give $L=2^{34}A=2^{62}$ in either case. Take $P_0=128L$.

In either class the dyadic sum from rank 128 through $k$ is below $(256+4096)Lk<2^{13}Lk$. To economize on the ranks above $k$, use the [single-size criterion, formula (1)](leaf-routing.md#a-sufficient-condition-at-one-population-size). For $k\ge1024$ take its radix $R'=\lfloor k/16\rfloor\ge k/32$. Two digits suffice since $(R')^2\ge k$, and the list has at most $10R'\le k$ entries. Its multiplicity bound is $s_2=16$, its defect bound is $b\le258A$, and its filling height is $13b+24$. Formula (1) is at most $480b+825<2^{17}A$. Hence the sites above rank $k$ in either class have smaller population, and there are at most $Ak$ such sites in total. Adding the two dyadic sums, these remaining leaves, at most $254\cdot13001k$ leaves at the leading sites, and the $Ak$ nonleaves gives less than $2^{80}k$. For $k<1024$ use $n\le Ak(1+13001k)<2^{80}k$ directly.

## Reproduction and scope

The independent checks reconstruct adjacency between successive marked sites, check signed scale ranges, and compare the two histogram gains on the original paths. Whole-tree tests cover both repairs, recount every edge and charge the selected leaf parents. Ten successive growth stages were checked individually on a 140,000-vertex host. Four further 66,000-vertex hosts exercise 716 buffers and 688 placements on earlier paths. At modulus 16,384 the verifier recounts all 131,073 vertices and their actual edges in a seed. It checks a continuation to order 268,566,529 by verifying the residue permutation for every parent label used in the repeated full batches. Lean proves the sum-multiplicity bound for each nondecreasing parent/child type pair, the separation of the signed free bands and the scale-overlap inequality. It also checks the final numerical coefficient. [Lean lemmas](RankedHulls.lean)
