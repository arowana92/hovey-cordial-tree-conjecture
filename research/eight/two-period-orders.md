# A two-period depth-first obstruction at every composite modulus

**Theorem.** At every composite modulus $k$, a tree of diameter four on $2k$ vertices has a depth-first preorder for which no labeling is cordial on every prefix. In fact, the three prefixes of orders $k$, $k+1$ and $2k$ cannot all be cordial under one labeling. The whole tree is cordial at every positive modulus by the [diameter-four theorem](../hub/all-radius-two.md).

At eight and nine labels the minimum order of such an example is exactly **16** and **18**, respectively, even among all prescribed connected orders. A finite classification below decides every prescribed connected order through 23 vertices at eight and 26 vertices at nine.

## Two stars following a double star

The following criterion works at all $k\ge2$. Choose $1\le r,s<k$. Start at a hub, visit a star of order $r$ attached by its center, then visit $k-1-r$ single leaves at the hub. At this point the prefix has $k$ vertices. Append two more stars by their centers at the same hub, of orders $k-s$ and $s$ in that order. Each star is visited center first, followed immediately by all its children. This is a depth-first preorder.

Write $d=\gcd(k,s)$. One labeling works at every prefix of this order exactly when

$$
                         \frac{k\,\gcd(d,r-1)}{d}\ \nmid\ r .
$$

To prove this, normalize the hub label to zero. The first $k$ vertices must use every label once. If the first star center receives $y\ne0$, the missing edge weight is $D=-(r-1)y$. Indeed, start with the nonzero residues as edge weights and add $y$ to the labels of the $r-1$ children. If the additive order $h$ of $y$ divides $r$, this formula gives $D=y$, already used by the center edge.

Conversely, if $h\nmid r$, write $r-1=qh+e$ with $0\le e\le h-2$. Give the children the labels $-y,\ldots,-ey$ and all residues in $q$ cosets of $\langle y\rangle$ outside that subgroup. There are enough cosets since $r-1\le k-2$. These labels exclude zero and $y$. Shifting by $y$ preserves the full cosets and slides the segment one position, leaving a single missing weight $-ey=D$. Give the hub leaves the remaining labels. This proves the exact first-period spectrum. Within that period, injectivity of the vertices and of the edge sums makes every prefix cordial.

The next center has forced label $D$ and the edge counts become uniform. Its children are now singleton components at the surplus. The last star of order $s$ attaches at relative parent $-D$. The [pinned-star theorem](../rainbow/pinned-star.md) makes the final batch possible exactly when $d\mid D$. Its new vertex labels avoid the surplus and its new weights are distinct, proving cordiality at each intermediate prefix too.

It remains to choose $y$ with $d\mid(r-1)y$ and additive order not dividing $r$. The allowed multiples form the subgroup generated by $d/\gcd(d,r-1)$. A generator has order $k\gcd(d,r-1)/d$. Every subgroup element has order dividing this number, which proves the criterion.

**Composite moduli.** Take $r=p$, the smallest prime divisor of $k$, and $s=k/p$. Every prime factor of $s$ is at least $p$, so $\gcd(s,p-1)=1$. The displayed number is $p$ and divides $r$. This proves the obstruction, using only the three stated prefix sizes. The tree has three branching neighbors at the hub. All three branches contain leaves at distance two. Its diameter is four. At a prime modulus the displayed number is $k$ and every order in this family works.

For example, at $k=8$ visit a star of order two and five single leaves after the hub. Append stars of orders four and four. With hub zero the possible first deficits are exactly $1,2,3,5,6,7$, while the second period requires its first center to carry zero or four. At $k=9$ visit a star of order three and five single leaves, then stars of orders six and three. The possible first deficits are the six units and the second period requires a multiple of three.

## All connected orders at eight and nine through the third incomplete period

For $k=8,9$, every prescribed connected order of fewer than $2k$ vertices works. Up to $k$ vertices, take any cordial labeling of a $k$-vertex tree extending the tree in question. The full permutation and distinct edge sums restrict successfully to every prefix. Above $k$ vertices first label the initial $k$ vertices this way. The next label is forced. The remaining batch has at most $k-2$ vertices, so the complete one-surplus classification extends it with distinct fresh labels and distinct weights. This also proves the sharp lower bound claimed above. After a successful prefix of order $2k$, the same argument gives every prescribed continuation of at most $k-1$ steps. [Eight-label packets](surplus.md) · [Nine-label packets](../rainbow/surplus-nine.md)

It therefore suffices to classify the prefix of order $2k$. Distinguish in its first $k$ vertices the parent $a$ of vertex $k+1$, using one-based positions. Root the first tree at $a$ for enumeration and normalize its label to zero. Try every bijection with distinct edge weights. If its deficit is $D$, the forced vertex gets $D$. Relative to the new surplus, the old colors are the former labels minus $D$, and the forced vertex has color zero.

A remaining forest can fail only if it consists of a star and singletons. Its placement is specified by the number of children at the star center, the old site of its center, and the multiset of old sites for its singleton components. These sites can include the forced vertex. For the first admissible base labeling expand each exceptional colored packet to **all placements** giving those colors. For each further admissible base labeling retain only the placements still exceptional. A surviving placement precludes every initial labeling. Conversely a discarded placement admits a successful extension, including every intermediate prefix, in any parent-first ordering within the two bijective portions.

| Modulus | Colored packet failures | Rooted bases | Bases with failures | Surviving placements before symmetry | Isomorphism types |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 42 | 115 | 32 | 180 | 159 |
| 9 | 12 | 286 | 27 | 176 | 163 |

The isomorphisms preserve the distinguished old root, the center site and the multiplicity of singleton attachments at every site, including at the forced vertex. The [classification program](check_two_period_orders.py) computes the table and exposes `obstructed(k, parents, table)` for a tree numbered in its prescribed connected order. It derives the exceptional colors by bit masks over all nonzero-label permutations. Two rooted-tree enumerations agree on the whole base inventory. A separate replay materializes one whole tree for each of the 322 obstructed types, exhausts its initial labelings and independently tests each resulting final forest by permutations. Moving a singleton to a child of its star gives 322 successful orders for which the program recounts every prefix.

The [two-period checker](check_two_period_depth_first.py) independently exhausts both periods for every parameter pair at moduli two through nine. It also constructs and recounts the ordinary labeling on 201 composite-modulus examples through 256 using the integer three-branch construction. Run from the repository root.
