# Hovey’s cordial tree conjecture

**Work in progress.** This repository gives reproducible computer-assisted proofs that every finite tree is $k$-cordial for each modulus from 8 through 21. Hovey’s conjecture for every positive integer $k$ remains open. [Eight labels](research/eight/README.md) · [Nine through thirteen](research/rainbow/README.md) · [Fourteen through seventeen](research/rainbow/ordered-cut.md) · [Eighteen through twenty-one](research/rainbow/native-spine.md)

At eight labels, Lean proves the theorem for finite trees built by joining rooted subtrees. It checks the finite certificate, the induction and the counts on each tree’s actual edges. [Formal proof and replay](research/eight/README.md#the-complete-eight-label-theorem-in-lean)

For **any finite tree and any chosen vertex**, adding at least $C(h+1)^2$ leaves at that vertex gives a super edge-magic tree, where $h$ is the original order and $C$ is absolute. One integer labeling then works at every positive modulus. For each fixed bound on the matching number or on the number of nonleaf vertices, only finitely many trees can fail to be super edge-magic. [Quadratic integer bound and independent checks](research/hub/quadratic-endpoint.md) · [Bounded-nonleaf result](research/hub/endpoint-hub.md)

Every tree of diameter at most four is cordial for **every** positive modulus. A computer-assisted construction gives one integer labeling with consecutive vertex labels and consecutive edge sums. [Proof, certificates and independent checks](research/hub/all-radius-two.md)

For diameter five, every tree is $k$-cordial for $1\le k\le36$. At any modulus, testing only trees of order exactly $k$ suffices. Every diameter-five tree of order at least $2k-5$ is $k$-cordial regardless of that test. [Reduction, finite proofs and reproducible checks](research/hub/diameter-five.md)

A vertex labeling takes values in the residues modulo $k$. Each edge receives the sum of its endpoint labels modulo $k$. Among vertices, every two residue counts must differ by at most one. The same condition holds independently among edges, including residues with count zero.

## Primary literature

- At one label every tree is immediate. Cahit proved that every tree is cordial at two labels in 1987. [Cahit, *Cordial graphs*, Ars Combinatoria 23, pp. 201–207](https://combinatorialpress.com/ars/vol23/)
- Hovey introduced the conjecture, proved the tree results for three through five labels, and gave a sufficient finite test using rooted forests. His caterpillar theorem includes the case of eight labels for that family. [Mark Hovey, *A-cordial graphs* (1991)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-365X(91)90254-Y)
- Hegde and Srinivasa Murthy report a result for trees of order below a prime modulus. Lemma 2.6 in their author-posted manuscript fails on four distinct residues modulo five, leaving a gap in that derivation. [2014 paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/09720529.2013.867698) · [Four-variable example, manuscript and Lean verification](research/prime/restricted-value.md)
- Driscoll, Krop and Nguyen proved the six-label case by choosing smaller families of rooted pieces and fitting their label and weight counts to the core. Their paper states Hovey’s finite test in Theorem 2.2. [*All trees are six-cordial* (2017)](https://www.ejgta.org/index.php/ejgta/article/view/231)
- Driscoll proved the seven-label case by splitting trees, listing local labelings and matching them to deficient weights or surplus vertex labels. [*All Trees are Seven-Cordial* (2019 preprint, version 2)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12351)
- Youssef’s Theorem 4 shows that consecutive vertex labels and consecutive edge sums imply cordiality at every modulus. This relates the tree question to super edge-magic labelings. [*On k-cordial labelling* (2009)](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/43/ajc_v43_p031.pdf)
- Müyesser and Pokrovskiy discuss how their bounded-degree methods could approach Hovey’s conjecture when the modulus is sufficiently large relative to the degree bound. Section 6 describes the extra strengthening that such a proof would require. [*On the Graham–Sloane harmonious labelling conjecture* (2025 preprint, version 2)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05280v2#S6)

At modulus $k=n-1$, a cordial labeling of an $n$-vertex tree uses every edge residue once and repeats exactly one vertex residue. Thus the full conjecture includes the Graham–Sloane harmonious tree conjecture. [Müyesser and Pokrovskiy, introduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05280v2#S1)

A related polynomial argument by Gnang and Williams concerns the order-equals-modulus base case. A check of its symmetrized term on the roots of unity gives zero when $n$ does not divide 4. A degree-dependent normalization gives an exact count of valid labelings. [Calculation and corrected polynomial stabilizer](research/orbit-polynomial.md) · [Gnang and Williams, version 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.02240v1)

Primary-literature searches on 26–27 November 2026 found these results and no earlier all-orders proof for eight labels. The eight-label argument here uses a finite attachment lemma and induction on tree order. The certificate verifies that lemma.

## General moduli

At any modulus $k$, testing all trees below order $2^{26}k$ suffices to decide the conjecture at that modulus. A smallest counterexample has fewer than $k$ leaves, and three collections of degree-two vertices give a labeling of every larger tree with that few leaves. Every counterexample has fewer than $2^{28}k$ nonleaf vertices. [Linear order test, permutation construction and checks](research/large-order/hall-pools.md)

**Every counterexample at modulus $k$ has order $O(k)$**, with unrestricted tree shape. In particular, order $2^{80}k$ suffices at every modulus. The proof assigns the available leaf parents to signed jumps in successive ranges, keeping the count of each label and endpoint sum bounded as the subtree joining those parents grows. The remaining edges can then be repaired with bounded cost per parent. [Linear order theorem, construction and independent checks](research/large-order/linear.md) · [Earlier square-root logarithmic bound](research/large-order/interval-paths.md)

If a tree excludes a nonempty forest $F$ as an induced subgraph, and each component of $F$ has diameter at most four, then order $Ck\max\{\sqrt m,\Delta(F),1\}$ suffices for $k$-cordiality. Here $m$ counts the components and $C$ is absolute. Thus a counterexample of order above $Ctk$ must contain $t^2$ mutually nonadjacent depth-two trees with $t$ arms and $t$ original leaves on each arm, after increasing the absolute constant if necessary. [Proof and induced-subgraph checks](research/large-order/neighborhood-pins.md)

Every tree of maximum degree at least $13001k$ is $k$-cordial. In fact, adding $13000k$ leaves at any one prescribed vertex suffices. Seven successive vertices can transfer a missing edge weight between arbitrary residues. Enough disjoint paths make the vertex and edge histograms agree except for one unit, with absolute constant discrepancy in each bin. [Proof, Lean identity and whole-tree checks](research/large-order/gap-path.md)

A separate linear order bound holds whenever the nonleaf tree has bounded degree apart from a bounded number of vertices, for arbitrary leaf populations. Earlier interval rounding gives an unrestricted $O(k\log(2k))$ bound. [Leaf routing](research/large-order/leaf-routing.md) · [Interval rounding](research/large-order/primitive.md) · [Squared differences](research/large-order/diagonal.md) · [Neutral subdivisions](research/large-order/flexible-path.md)

For every fixed $k$, **every sufficiently large tree is $k$-cordial**, with unrestricted degrees. In fact order $(2^{34}+4)k^2$ suffices, and independently a single vertex of degree at least $4k^2$ suffices at any order. For every fixed $\gamma>0$ and sufficiently large $k$, a vertex of degree at least $k^{3/2+\gamma}$ also suffices. [Asymptotic degree bound](research/large-order/pruning-depth.md) [Quadratic bound for every counterexample](research/large-order/quadratic.md) · [High-degree construction](research/large-order/high-degree.md) · [Basic correction and Lean verification](research/large-order/README.md)

For every $\gamma>0$ and all sufficiently large $k$, if a counterexample exists then a smallest one has fewer than $k^{1+\gamma}$ vertices. In addition, its order must lie within $o(k)$ of a multiple of $k$ from below, including that multiple. These restrictions hold at unrestricted maximum degree. The first proof uses a larger cyclic group, and the second adapts the almost-spanning rainbow embedding theorem to prescribed parent labels. [Near-linear counterexample bound](research/bounded-degree/projection.md) · [Order residue and prescribed embeddings](research/rainbow/pinned-prefix.md)

A path on at least $k^{1+\gamma}$ vertices, for any fixed $\gamma>0$ and sufficiently large $k$, also suffices for the entire tree to be $k$-cordial, regardless of the branches attached to it. At each modulus a separate criterion uses the sizes of successive neighborhoods of a cordial subtree. [Protected subtrees and the neighborhood criterion](research/large-order/balls.md)

For every fixed maximum degree $\Delta$, there is a threshold $k_0(\Delta)$ above which every tree of that maximum degree is $k$-cordial, with no restriction on its order. A bounded-degree auxiliary tree realizes the prescribed forest boundary weights, so the embedding lemma of Müyesser and Pokrovskiy applies directly. [Proof and checks](research/bounded-degree/README.md)

For **any fixed $D,s$**, every tree with at most $s$ vertices of degree greater than $D$ is $k$-cordial for all sufficiently large $k$, at every tree order. Consequently, for every fixed $r$, the $r$-th largest degree tends to infinity along any infinite sequence of distinct counterexample pairs. The proof handles the leaves and short stars along the paths left by two pruning rounds. [Proof and reproducible checks](research/hub/exceptional-degrees.md) · [Lean proof for the 40-position window](research/hub/DecoratedWindow.lean) · [Earlier one-vertex theorem](research/hub/one-exceptional-degree.md)

For fixed $D,\epsilon$ with $D\ge3$ and $0<\epsilon<1$, the same all-orders conclusion holds with up to $k^{1-\epsilon}$ vertices of degree greater than $D$, provided at most $k^{1-\epsilon}$ leaves lie within distance two of those vertices. In particular, subdividing each pendant edge at least twice suffices for every tree in this degree-count class at large $k$. The high-degree vertices may be adjacent. [Multiple boundary labels, consecutive preorder blocks and checks](research/hub/subdivided-blocks.md)

It suffices to bound the number of leaves **directly adjacent** to those high-degree vertices by $k^{1-\epsilon}$. Consequently a single subdivision of each pendant edge at a high vertex suffices throughout this class, at every sufficiently large modulus. The proof allocates prescribed shifts to terminal parents and completes their edges in three matchings. [Forest theorem, primary inputs and independent checks](research/hub/terminal-shifts.md)

For fixed $D,\epsilon$ and sufficiently large $k$, **testing only trees of order exactly $k$ suffices** for the class with at most $k^{1-\epsilon}$ vertices of degree greater than $D$. Along any sequence of counterexamples in this class with $k\to\infty$, deleting the leaves adjacent to those vertices must leave $o(k)$ vertices. [Order-$k$ reduction, leaf concentration and weighted cut checks](research/hub/concentrated-leaves.md)

At every sufficiently large **even** modulus, suppose all degrees except at two adjacent vertices are bounded by a fixed $D$. Delete the leaves at these two vertices and let $h$ be the surviving order. If each vertex had at least $h-2$ such leaves, the tree is $k$-cordial at every order. When $k\equiv2\pmod4$, it also suffices for each vertex to have as many leaf neighbors as nonleaf neighbors outside the common edge, or more. Contracting a larger fixed connected set of sites gives a related result for multiples of a suitable divisor. [Divisor construction, Lean interval lemma and whole-tree checks](research/hub/cluster-lift.md)

For any fixed odd $d\ge3$, at every sufficiently large multiple $k$ of $d$ **every tree with two adjacent exceptional vertices is $k$-cordial**, with a fixed degree bound $D$ on all other vertices. The construction covers all diameters and all leaf populations. It uses two ordinary branches below the same prescribed site to complete the last edge weights. [Proof and independent checks](research/hub/adjacent-odd-divisor.md) · [Neutral groups of repeated branches](research/hub/odd-branch-groups.md)

The same all-orders theorem holds at **every sufficiently large even modulus**. Consequently the moduli for which every tree in this two-site class is cordial have natural density one. Any unbounded sequence of counterexample moduli in the class must have least prime factor tending to infinity. [Even-modulus proof, density consequence and independent checks](research/hub/adjacent-even.md)

At **every modulus**, for fixed $D,s$ a tree with at most $s$ vertices above degree $D$ has a labeling with balanced vertex counts whose edge counts can be balanced by changing at most $C(D,s)$ individual recorded weights. The sites may be far apart. At order $k$ this gives a bijection with at least $k-1-C(D,s)$ distinct actual endpoint sums, including for primes. Exact cordiality would require zero corrections. [Bounded error theorem and actual-edge checks](research/hub/bounded-error.md)

For any fixed number $s$ of sites, every tree with **all but at most $c_s\sqrt k$ leaves adjacent to those sites** is $k$-cordial for all sufficiently large $k$, at every tree order. On an ordinary core of order $h$ with $l$ leaves, a largest added population of at least $A_s(h+(l+1)^2)$ gives one super edge-magic labeling for all moduli, uniformly over the other populations. [Integer and modulus theorems](research/hub/quadratic-endpoint.md) · [Simultaneous integer construction and checks](research/hub/weighted-forest.md)

For every rooted tree of order $h$ and **every modulus $k>C(h+1)^2$**, there is an injection taking its root to zero whose edge sums give every vertex label except one. Consequently, for an absolute $c>0$, every tree with all but at most $c\sqrt{k}$ of its leaves adjacent to one vertex is $k$-cordial for all sufficiently large $k$. This covers arbitrary degrees, branch lengths and tree orders. [Quadratic theorem and square-root leaf bound](research/hub/quadratic-root.md) · [Mixed-arity checks](research/hub/check_mixed_sparse.py)

With **unrestricted degrees**, every rooted tree on $h$ vertices has an injective labeling at $k>100(h+1)^2$ with at most seven edge weights needing correction to match the vertex labels except zero. A path with 129 internal degree-two vertices away from the root makes the construction exact at $k>10^5(h+1)^2$. With such a path, at sufficiently large $k$ it suffices to have at most $\sqrt{k}/10^5$ leaves away from one vertex, regardless of tree order and branch lengths. [Seven-error preparation, exact theorem and checks](research/hub/seven-errors.md)

A sufficiently long path whose internal vertices have degree two eliminates all those errors. For fixed $D,s$, one length works at **every modulus** for every tree with at most $s$ vertices above degree $D$. Both ends of the path may be internal. Consequently only finitely many counterexample pairs can arise by attaching any numbers of leaves at $s$ sites of any tree with a fixed bound on its original leaf count, allowing arbitrary subdivisions. A second exact repair applies to short branches. Fix $D,s,m$. If at most $s$ vertices exceed degree $D$, sufficiently many branches at one vertex each consisting of a neighbor with $m$ leaf children guarantee cordiality at **all moduli**. Under this degree restriction only finitely many counterexample pairs can have all leaves within distance two of a bounded number of vertices. This includes diameter five. For fixed $D,s$ it also suffices to have two pendant stars at each of sufficiently many **distinct** vertices, with distances unrestricted and no equality required between the two arities. The construction uses one shared parent and three other parents for each five-star group. [Distributed pairs, proof and actual-edge checks](research/hub/paired-stars.md)

For fixed $D,s$ there are only finitely many counterexample pairs of **any fixed bounded diameter** with at most $s$ degrees above $D$. More generally, the leaves can lie in any fixed number of balls of fixed radius, with their centers arbitrarily far apart. If the tree has diameter at most six, every modulus congruent to two modulo four works. [Construction, star extension and checks](research/matching-expansion.md)

Joining an odd number $q$ of equally large $m$-cordial trees in a path gives a $qm$-cordial tree whenever $q$ and $m$ are coprime. For other quotient trees with distinct quotient edge sums, the determinant governing the construction is always odd, so every power-of-two factor $m$ works. There are also sufficient conditions and exact obstructions at singular factors. [Odd quotients](research/odd-quotient.md) · [Path theorem and checks](research/path-quotient.md)

For any prime $p$, join equally large $p$-cordial trees along an odd quotient tree with a bijective labeling and distinct edge sums. If $p$ does not divide the common piece order, the joined tree is $qp$-cordial, where $q$ is the number of pieces. A leaf of the quotient has at most two simultaneous equations, which can be satisfied by scaling and translating its labels. For every odd-order $q$-vertex tree with a bijective cordial labeling, adding the same number of leaves at every vertex gives a tree cordial at every multiple of $q$. A construction with repeated copies of an arbitrary labeled tree also permits iterated rooted products at shared prime factors. [Product theorem and explicit labelings](research/repeated-product.md)
