# Hovey’s cordial tree conjecture

**Work in progress.** This repository gives reproducible computer-assisted proofs that every finite tree is $k$-cordial for each modulus from 8 through 25. Hovey’s conjecture for every positive integer $k$ remains open. [Eight labels](research/eight/README.md) · [Nine through thirteen](research/rainbow/README.md) · [Fourteen through seventeen](research/rainbow/ordered-cut.md) · [Eighteen through twenty-one](research/rainbow/native-spine.md) · [Twenty-two](research/rainbow/twenty-two.md) · [Twenty-three](research/rainbow/twenty-three.md) · [Twenty-four](research/rainbow/twenty-four.md) · [Twenty-five](research/rainbow/twenty-five.md)

At eight labels, a prescribed cordial core of order one modulo eight extends across any attached forest of any size other than seven. At seven new vertices exactly 42 colored forests obstruct extension. This classification gives a second proof for every ordinary tree. [Eight-label attachment theorem](research/eight/surplus.md)

At nine labels, every prescribed divisible-order core extends across every forest. Starting at order one modulo nine, only twelve eight-vertex colored forests fail. Each consists of a star and singleton leaves. [Nine-label proof and independent enumeration](research/rainbow/surplus-nine.md)

At ten labels, a prescribed divisible-order core extends across every attachment of every size with one exception up to affine symmetry, a pair of stars on ten new vertices. A one-surplus core has exactly 34 exceptional nine-vertex attachments and eight ten-vertex attachments. The same pair of stars gives a fixed-label obstruction at every modulus of the form $12j+2$ or $12j+10$ greater than two. Its underlying tree is a caterpillar and admits a cordial labeling. At any modulus, one new center with its children and independent leaves extends a one-surplus core whenever at least $k+1$ vertices are added, even with arbitrary edge offsets. At prime powers $k$ new vertices suffice. [Ten-label census and two-star theorem](research/rainbow/ten-prescribed.md) · [One-period criterion](research/rainbow/star-periods.md) · [All moduli with one spare leaf](research/rainbow/one-center-slack.md)

Every tree with **at most one branching vertex** is cordial at every modulus through **45**. With **two branching vertices** the proof reaches modulus **40**. Both results hold at every tree order. The construction extends a fixed cordial labeling over arbitrarily many paths attached at any one vertex whenever the original order is zero or one modulo the modulus. In both ranges a specified leaf can receive a most-frequent label. For trees with a perfect or near-perfect matching this gives cordiality at twice every odd modulus in the corresponding range, reaching 90 and 78 labels. [Terminal paths, branching bound and certificates](research/rainbow/terminal-paths.md) · [Marked leaves and matching expansions](research/rainbow/marked-branches.md)

At every modulus through 47, paths attached at one joint with a whole number of periods of new vertices can increase every vertex and edge count equally, at any old order. The sole exception is a pair of two-edge paths at four labels. At odd $m\le47$, chains of matched pairs adding a whole number of $m$-pair periods have the same uniform property at $2m$. Through odd $m\le45$, chains of matched pairs of arbitrary lengths and port choices extend any prescribed $2m$-cordial core of divisible order. [Uniform path and matching constructions](research/rainbow/uniform-paths.md)

Every finite tree with **at most four leaves** is cordial at **every positive modulus**. The construction gives one integer labeling with consecutive vertex labels and consecutive edge sums, for arbitrary lengths of all the arms and the path between branching vertices. The spider construction also allows arbitrarily many additional one-edge legs. [Four-leaf proof](research/census/four-leaves.md) · [Spiders with up to four long legs](research/census/spider-leaves.md)

Every tree with **at most eight leaves** is cordial at every modulus through 30, for arbitrary tree order. At moduli 26 through 30, checking orders up to $2k$ would suffice to settle the class with at most eleven leaves. At 26 and 27 labels every tree with at most ten leaves is cordial. At 26 labels a smallest counterexample would have 49 through 546 vertices, and its order residue must be 23, 24, 25 or 0. With eleven through thirteen leaves, only orders 49 through 52 remain at 26 labels, and 50 through 54 at 27 labels. Every tree through order 48 is 26-cordial, and every tree through order 49 is 27-cordial. **Every tree with a perfect or near-perfect matching is cordial at 26, 30, 34, 38, 42, 46 and 50 labels**, with no restriction on its order. For every modulus through 25 a chosen leaf can receive a most-frequent vertex label. More generally, a smallest 26-label counterexample on $26q-H$ vertices, $0\le H\le4$, must leave at least $H+4$ vertices uncovered in every matching. The proof constructs a single labeling at 13 labels that is cordial on every prefix of any prescribed connected ordering. **Every tree with a matching missing at most three vertices is 26-cordial**, at every tree order. For matching deficiency at most five, a smallest counterexample must have order 51, 52, 77, 78, 103 or 104. A matching that covers every nonleaf also suffices when its size is 0 or 12 modulo 13, even with arbitrarily many unmatched leaves. At 26 labels, any rooted tree with 26 free vertices that have a perfect matching after root deletion can be added at its root to **any prescribed cordial core**. Every vertex and edge count increases by one. The proof covers 42,187,148 rooted shapes. The same construction and certificates cover moduli $2,6,10,14,18,22,30$ with one full period of free vertices. Every tree on **at most 27 vertices** is cordial at **every positive modulus**. A census verifies one integer labeling with consecutive endpoint sums for each of the 1,198,738,055 unrooted trees of orders 2 through 27. This also proves cordiality at modulus $k\le27$ for arbitrary-order trees with at most $k$ nonleaf vertices. For trees with at most six leaves, the integer census reaches order 30. At **2, 3, 5, 7, 11 and 13 labels**, every finite tree remains cordial even when each edge has its own prescribed additive offset. The proof reduces arbitrary orders to trees of order exactly the modulus and uses signed counts to cover the possible offsets. At every prime it holds for any tree in which all but at most four edges meet one vertex. At eight labels, each edge can independently receive offset 0 or 4. At sixteen labels, each edge can independently receive 0 or 8. The allowable offset sets are fully classified for every modulus through 16 and for the mixed composite moduli 18, 20, 21, 22 and 24. [Prime proof](research/biased/README.md) · [Prime powers, obstructions and classification](research/biased/prime-powers.md)

At **every prime** $p$, paths of all orders and any tree with every edge replaced by a path of length at least $p-1$ admit independently prescribed offsets on every edge. For a cycle of length divisible by an odd prime, uniformity is equivalent to the offsets summing to zero. [Prime path, cycle and circulant theorems](research/biased/prime-paths.md) Independent cycle certificates give the same conclusions at $k=6,8,9,10,12$. At all these primes and composites, lengthening only the leaf-to-branch paths to $k-2$ edges suffices. [Composite path proofs](research/biased/composite-paths.md) · [Terminal-arm theorem](research/biased/prime-paths.md#it-suffices-to-lengthen-the-terminal-arms)

At every modulus $k\ge2$, all trees on at least $Ck^2\log^3(2k)$ vertices remain cordial under arbitrary prescribed offsets on their edges, for an absolute $C$. Thus even at composite moduli, where some order-$k$ examples fail with offsets, only finitely many decorated trees can fail. [Proof, Lean cyclic identity and independent histogram checks](research/biased/large-order.md)

At every modulus $k$, arbitrary labels prescribed at all $h$ parents of a rooted tree extend if $n\ge kh$ and each parent has at least $k-1$ leaf children per nonleaf child. A stronger statement pools leaves across parents and allows independent edge offsets. For an attachment with one free parent and size divisible by $k$, one congruence characterizes all center labels that give uniform increments. [Prescribed parents, exact criterion and checks](research/biased/pinned-periods.md)

At any modulus $k$, a bare path with **$35k$ internal degree-two vertices** guarantees cordiality for arbitrary tree order and degrees. At odd $k$, $25k$ suffice. Subdividing any chosen edge of any $n$-vertex tree by at least $n+3k-3$ vertices also suffices. For any prescribed cordial core and any subdivision length $t\ge3k$, a single parity condition characterizes exactly when the old labels can be preserved. [Euler circuit construction and proof](research/census/euler-subdivision.md)

At every odd modulus $k\ge5$ and every even modulus from 10 through 64, inserting at least $(k-2)\lfloor(n-2)/2\rfloor$ vertices on any chosen edge of an $n$-vertex tree suffices for cordiality. Each step restores two leaves and adds $k-2$ subdivisions while increasing every frequency by one. [Two-leaf construction and exact small exceptions](research/census/two-leaf-padding.md)

At every $k\ge5$, any edge of any prescribed cordial core can gain an entire period, except when $k\equiv2\pmod4$ and the old order is one modulo $k$. In particular, any tree on at most 27 vertices stays $k$-cordial after inserting independent multiples of $k$ on every edge, at every modulus. [Single-edge padding and the uniform bound](research/census/edge-padding.md) · [Complete period classification](research/census/period-subdivision.md)

At every $k\ge5$, an edge of a prescribed cordial core of order one modulo $k$ can be subdivided by any number $t\ge2$ of vertices, except when $k\equiv2\pmod4$ and $t$ is an odd multiple of $k$. In that case the old labels cannot all be preserved. Every smallest counterexample of order $mk+s$ has at most $s-2$ internal vertices on each connector between branching vertices. At 26 and 27 labels, subdividing any one edge of any tree of order at most 27 preserves cordiality for every subdivision length. At **every modulus**, any prescribed cordial core of order zero or one modulo $k$ extends across any mixture of leaves and two-edge paths attached to one vertex. In a smallest counterexample of order $qk+s$, $2\le s\le k$, at most $s-2$ vertices can lie on the one- and two-edge terminal arms at a branching vertex. At **every odd modulus** $k$, a path on $qk+1$ vertices, $q\ge1$, can have its surplus vertex residue and the label at **any chosen vertex** prescribed independently, while all edge counts stay uniform. Thus it attaches to any fixed cordial core of any order. An arbitrary tree of such paths is cordial even with independent offsets on the joining edges. At even moduli the same two prescriptions work for $q\ge4$, improving to $q\ge2$ for $k\equiv2\pmod4$. At every $k$, a path on at least $2k^2$ vertices attaches to any prescribed cordial core at any position, for every residue of both orders. At every modulus, a partially subdivided star can give its hub a most-frequent label, except at the middle of the five-vertex path at modulus four. At **every odd modulus** $k$, attach a leaf at one chosen vertex and $(k-1)/2$ paths of length two at any other chosen vertex. Every vertex and edge count can increase by exactly one, preserving all old labels. Replacing each added vertex by a matched pair gives uniform increments at $2k$ with arbitrary attachment ports. A vertex labeling takes values in the residues modulo $k$. Each edge receives the sum of its endpoint labels modulo $k$. Among vertices, every two residue counts must differ by at most one. The same condition holds independently among edges, including residues with count zero.

## Primary literature

- At one label every tree is immediate. Cahit proved that every tree is cordial at two labels in 1987. [Cahit, *Cordial graphs*, Ars Combinatoria 23, pp. 201–207](https://combinatorialpress.com/ars/vol23/)
- Hovey introduced the conjecture, proved the tree results for three through five labels, and gave a sufficient finite test using rooted forests. His caterpillar theorem includes the case of eight labels for that family. [Mark Hovey, *A-cordial graphs* (1991)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-365X(91)90254-Y)
- Hegde and Srinivasa Murthy report a result for trees of order below a prime modulus. Lemma 2.6 in their author-posted manuscript fails on four distinct residues modulo five, leaving a gap in that derivation. [2014 paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/09720529.2013.867698) · [Four-variable example, manuscript and Lean verification](research/prime/restricted-value.md)
- Driscoll, Krop and Nguyen proved the six-label case by choosing smaller families of rooted pieces and fitting their label and weight counts to the core. Their paper states Hovey’s finite test in Theorem 2.2. [*All trees are six-cordial* (2017)](https://www.ejgta.org/index.php/ejgta/article/view/231)
- Driscoll proved the seven-label case by splitting trees, listing local labelings and matching them to deficient weights or surplus vertex labels. [*All Trees are Seven-Cordial* (2019 preprint, version 2)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12351)
- Youssef’s Theorem 4 shows that consecutive vertex labels and consecutive edge sums imply cordiality at every modulus. This relates the tree question to super edge-magic labelings. [*On k-cordial labelling* (2009)](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/43/ajc_v43_p031.pdf)
- Montgomery, Pokrovskiy and Sudakov proved asymptotic injective harmonious labeling for arbitrary finite abelian groups. In the cyclic case, any fixed positive relative surplus of labels suffices for sufficiently large trees. [Theorem 1.5 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.4171/JEMS/982)
- Müyesser and Pokrovskiy discuss how their bounded-degree methods could approach Hovey’s conjecture when the modulus is sufficiently large relative to the degree bound. Section 6 describes the extra strengthening that such a proof would require. [*On the Graham–Sloane harmonious labelling conjecture* (2025 preprint, version 2)](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05280v2#S6)

At modulus $k=n-1$, a cordial labeling of an $n$-vertex tree uses every edge residue once and repeats exactly one vertex residue. Thus the full conjecture includes the Graham–Sloane harmonious tree conjecture. [Graham and Sloane, *On additive bases and harmonious graphs* (1980), §5](https://neilsloane.com/doc/RLG/073.pdf#page=11) · [Müyesser and Pokrovskiy, introduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05280v2#S1)

A related polynomial argument by Gnang and Williams concerns the order-equals-modulus base case. A check of its symmetrized term on the roots of unity gives zero when $n$ does not divide 4. A degree-dependent normalization gives an exact count of valid labelings. [Calculation and corrected polynomial stabilizer](research/orbit-polynomial.md) · [Gnang and Williams, version 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.02240v1)

Primary-literature searches on 26–28 November 2026 found these results and no earlier all-orders proof for eight labels. The eight-label argument here uses a finite attachment lemma and induction on tree order. The certificate verifies that lemma.

## General moduli

At any modulus $k$, testing all trees below order **$66k$** suffices to decide the conjecture at that modulus. Every tree with at most $k+1$ leaves and more than $66k$ vertices is cordial. Any tree containing a path on $66k+1$ vertices is also cordial, with arbitrary branching along the path.
